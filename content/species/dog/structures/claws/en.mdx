## Overview

The claw of the dog (*unguicula*) is a modified skin structure. It is a capsule of hard keratin that wraps the ungual process (*processus unguicularis*) of the distal phalanx.

At its base, the ungual crest (*crista unguicularis*) of the phalanx forms a bony shelf that covers the proximal edge of the claw.

### Number of claws

- **Thoracic limb**. Five clawed digits. Digit I is the dewclaw: it sits on the medial side, above the ground, and does not bear weight.
- **Pelvic limb**. Usually four digits. Digit I is absent or vestigial, although some breeds have hind dewclaws, sometimes double.

## Structure

- **Wall**. Forms the dorsal and side surfaces of the claw. It is curved and laterally compressed.
- **Sole** (*solea unguiculae*). Palmar or plantar surface of softer keratin, between the edges of the wall.
- **Claw dermis**. Highly vascular, innervated connective tissue between the phalanx and the keratin. It is the living part of the claw, known as the quick. In unpigmented claws it shows as a pink zone.
- **Growth**. The claw grows continuously from its base, under the fold of skin around it, and is worn down by use.

## Clinical relevance

### Overgrowth

In dogs with little exercise or on soft surfaces the claw does not wear. It curls and alters how the digit bears weight.

The dewclaw never touches the ground and can grow round until it pierces the pad.

### Nail trimming

- The cut must stay distal to the quick.
- In white claws the quick can be seen. Pigmented claws are trimmed in small steps.
- The quick lengthens as the claw grows, so frequent trims let it recede gradually.
- Cutting into the quick causes pain and bleeding. It is controlled with pressure or a styptic powder.

### Other disorders

- claw fractures and avulsions, common in the dewclaw
- paronychia, infection of the claw fold
- symmetric lupoid onychodystrophy
- tumours of the nail bed, such as squamous cell carcinoma and digital melanoma
