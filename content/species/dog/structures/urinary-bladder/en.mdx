## Topography

The neck (*cervix vesicae*) lies at the cranial border of the pubis and continues as the urethra.

- **Empty**, the bladder can sit almost within the pelvic inlet.
- **Full**, the body and apex move cranially along the abdominal floor and may approach the umbilicus.

The ureters arrive on the dorsal surface and open at the trigone.

## Clinical relevance

- **Urolithiasis**. Struvite and calcium oxalate stones are the most common. Dalmatians are predisposed to urate stones. In males stones often lodge in the urethra just caudal to the os penis.
- **Cystocentesis**. Puncture of the bladder through the ventral abdominal wall to collect sterile urine.
- **Transitional cell carcinoma**. Usually at the trigone. Scottish terriers are predisposed.
- **Urinary tract infection**. More common in females because of their short urethra.
