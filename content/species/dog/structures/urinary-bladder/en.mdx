## Topography

The neck (*cervix vesicae*) lies at the cranial border of the pubis and continues as the urethra. When empty, the bladder can sit almost within the pelvic inlet; when full, the body and apex move cranially along the abdominal floor and may approach the umbilicus. The ureters arrive on the dorsal surface and open at the trigone.

## Clinical relevance

- **Urolithiasis**: struvite and calcium oxalate stones are the most common; Dalmatians are predisposed to urate stones. In males stones often lodge in the urethra just caudal to the os penis.
- **Cystocentesis**: puncture of the bladder through the ventral abdominal wall to collect sterile urine.
- **Transitional cell carcinoma**: usually at the trigone; Scottish terriers are predisposed.
- **Urinary tract infection**: more common in females because of the short urethra.
