## Overview

The dog skull is divided into two parts:

- the **braincase** (*neurocranium*), protecting the brain
- the **facial skeleton** (*viscerocranium*), forming the nose, the orbits and the mouth

Its shape varies enormously between breeds:

- dolichocephalic, such as the Greyhound and the Collie
- mesocephalic, such as the Labrador and the German Shepherd
- brachycephalic, such as the Bulldog and the Pug

Shape affects eye position, dentition and the airway. This model is a mesocephalic skull.

## Key features

- **Occipital bone**. Forms the base and caudal part of the skull. It carries the foramen magnum and the occipital condyles, which articulate with the atlas. The external occipital protuberance is palpable on the midline.
- **Sagittal crest and zygomatic arches**. Origin and insertion of the powerful masticatory muscles, the temporalis and masseter.
- **Frontal bones**. Contain the frontal sinuses and form the dorsal rim of the orbit. In the dog the orbit is incomplete caudally and is closed by the orbital ligament.
- **Maxilla and incisive bone**. Hold the upper teeth. The maxilla contains the maxillary recess and the infraorbital foramen, a site for nerve blocks.
- **Nasal bones and conchae**. Form the nasal cavity, highly developed in the dog.
- **Tympanic bulla**. Ventral bony cavity housing the middle ear.
- **Temporomandibular joint**. Formed by the mandibular fossa of the temporal bone, bounded caudally by the retroarticular process.

## Clinical relevance

The most frequent conditions are:

- head trauma
- zygomatic arch and maxillary fractures
- otitis media (tympanic bulla, bulla osteotomy)
- nasal neoplasia
- brachycephalic syndrome

The infraorbital and maxillary foramina are used for dental nerve blocks.
