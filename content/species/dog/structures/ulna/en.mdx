## Overview

The ulna is the longest bone of the forearm, although it bears less weight than the radius.

Its proximal end is bulky and forms most of the elbow joint. Distally the shaft narrows and ends in the **lateral styloid process**, which articulates with the ulnar carpal bone.

## Key features

- **Olecranon** (*olecranon*). Proximal prominence, the "point of the elbow". The triceps brachii inserts here, and it works as the lever arm for elbow extension.
- **Anconeal process** (*processus anconeus*). Sits in the olecranon fossa of the humerus when the elbow is extended.
- **Trochlear notch** (*incisura trochlearis*). Semilunar notch that embraces the humeral trochlea.
- **Medial and lateral coronoid processes**. The medial one is larger and shares load with the head of the radius.
- **Radial notch**. Articular surface for the articular circumference of the radial head.

## Clinical relevance

The components of **elbow dysplasia** sit in the ulna:

- fragmented medial coronoid process
- ununited anconeal process, typical of the German Shepherd
- joint incongruity

Olecranon fractures need tension-band fixation because of the pull of the triceps.

The distal ulnar physis provides most of the growth in length. Its premature closure bows the radius (*radius curvus*).
