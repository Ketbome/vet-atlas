---
title: Ulna
summary: Caudolateral bone of the forearm. Its olecranon and trochlear notch form the elbow, and it tapers toward the carpus.
---

## Overview

The ulna is the longest bone of the forearm, although it bears less weight than the radius. Its proximal end is bulky and forms most of the elbow joint; distally the shaft narrows progressively and ends in the lateral styloid process, which articulates with the ulnar carpal bone.

## Key features

- **Olecranon (*olecranon*)**: proximal prominence, the "point of the elbow", where the triceps brachii inserts. It works as the lever arm for elbow extension.
- **Anconeal process (*processus anconeus*)**: sits in the olecranon fossa of the humerus when the elbow is extended.
- **Trochlear notch (*incisura trochlearis*)**: semilunar notch that embraces the humeral trochlea.
- **Medial and lateral coronoid processes**: the medial one is larger and shares load with the head of the radius.
- **Radial notch**: articular surface for the articular circumference of the radial head.

## Clinical relevance

The ulna hosts the components of **elbow dysplasia**: fragmented medial coronoid process, ununited anconeal process (typical of the German Shepherd) and joint incongruity. Olecranon fractures need tension-band fixation because of the pull of the triceps. Premature closure of the distal ulnar physis (which provides most of the growth in length) bows the radius ("radius curvus").
