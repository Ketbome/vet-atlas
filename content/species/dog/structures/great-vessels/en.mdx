---
title: Aorta and caudal vena cava
summary: Great vessels of the trunk. The descending aorta runs along the roof of the thorax and abdomen; the caudal vena cava returns blood from the abdomen and pelvic limbs to the right atrium.
---

## Topography

- **Aorta**: leaves the left ventricle, forms the aortic arch (giving the brachiocephalic trunk and the left subclavian artery) and descends ventral to the vertebral bodies, slightly left of the median plane. It crosses the diaphragm through the aortic hiatus between the crura and in the abdomen gives the coeliac, cranial mesenteric, renal and caudal mesenteric arteries. It ends at the last lumbar vertebrae by dividing into the external and internal iliac arteries.
- **Caudal vena cava (*vena cava caudalis*)**: formed by the common iliac veins in the caudal lumbar region, it runs right of the aorta, receives the renal veins, passes through the dorsal border of the liver and the caval foramen of the diaphragm, and opens into the right atrium.

## Clinical relevance

- **Radiography**: on a lateral view the caudal vena cava is seen between the heart and the diaphragm; its width helps assess volume status and right-sided heart failure.
- **Portosystemic shunts**: anomalous vessels that divert portal blood into the caudal vena cava or the azygos vein, bypassing the liver.
- **Aortic thromboembolism**: uncommon in dogs; causes acute paresis of the pelvic limbs.
