## Overview

The dog keeps only a vestige of the clavicle, embedded in a fibrous band (*intersectio clavicularis*) cranial to the shoulder joint. This intersection divides the muscle in two:

- **Cleidobrachialis** (*m. cleidobrachialis*). From the clavicular intersection to the humerus.
- **Cleidocephalicus** (*m. cleidocephalicus*). From the clavicular intersection to the head and neck. It has a cervical part (*pars cervicalis*) and a mastoid part (*pars mastoidea*).

## Origin and insertion

- The cleidobrachialis inserts on the cranial surface of the distal end of the humerus.
- The cervical part of the cleidocephalicus reaches the dorsal fibrous raphe of the cranial part of the neck.
- The mastoid part inserts on the mastoid process of the temporal bone.

## Action

The main muscle that advances the thoracic limb, by extending the shoulder.

With the limb fixed, it draws the head and neck ventrally and laterally.

## Innervation

- Cleidocephalicus: accessory nerve and ventral branches of the cervical nerves.
- Cleidobrachialis: axillary nerve.

## Clinical relevance

It forms the dorsal border of the **jugular groove**, where the external jugular vein is punctured.

It covers the superficial cervical lymph nodes.
