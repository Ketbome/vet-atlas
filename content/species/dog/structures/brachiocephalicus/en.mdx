---
title: Brachiocephalicus
summary: Long muscle joining the arm to the head and neck through the clavicular intersection.
---

## Overview

The dog keeps only a vestige of the clavicle, embedded in a fibrous band (*intersectio clavicularis*) cranial to the shoulder joint. This intersection divides the muscle in two:

- **Cleidobrachialis (*m. cleidobrachialis*)**: from the clavicular intersection to the humerus.
- **Cleidocephalicus (*m. cleidocephalicus*)**: from the clavicular intersection to the head and neck, with a cervical part (*pars cervicalis*) and a mastoid part (*pars mastoidea*).

## Origin and insertion

- The cleidobrachialis inserts on the cranial surface of the distal end of the humerus.
- The cervical part of the cleidocephalicus reaches the dorsal fibrous raphe of the cranial part of the neck.
- The mastoid part inserts on the mastoid process of the temporal bone.

## Action

The main muscle that advances the thoracic limb (shoulder extension). With the limb fixed, it draws the head and neck ventrally and laterally.

## Innervation

Accessory nerve and ventral branches of the cervical nerves for the cleidocephalicus; axillary nerve for the cleidobrachialis.

## Clinical relevance

It forms the dorsal border of the jugular groove, where the external jugular vein is punctured. It covers the superficial cervical lymph nodes.
