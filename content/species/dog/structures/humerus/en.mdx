## Overview

The humerus is the bone of the arm. It is a strong bone that carries the insertions of most muscles moving the shoulder and elbow.

Its shaft is slightly twisted in an S shape. The head faces caudally at the proximal end and the trochlea faces cranially at the distal end.

## Proximal end

- **Head** (*caput humeri*). Convex articular surface, larger than the glenoid cavity, facing caudally.
- **Greater tubercle** (*tuberculum majus*). Lateral projection where the supraspinatus and infraspinatus insert. It is palpable and forms the "point of the shoulder".
- **Lesser tubercle** (*tuberculum minus*). Medial projection where the subscapularis inserts.
- **Intertubercular groove**. It lies between both tubercles and carries the tendon of the biceps brachii.

## Shaft and distal end

- **Deltoid tuberosity**. Prominent lateral ridge where the deltoideus inserts.
- **Humeral condyle**. It is made of the trochlea, medial, which articulates with the ulna and radius, and the capitulum, lateral, which articulates with the radius.
- **Olecranon and radial fossae**. The olecranon fossa is caudal and the radial fossa cranial. In the dog they are connected by the **supratrochlear foramen**.
- **Medial and lateral epicondyles**. The medial one gives origin to the carpal and digital flexors; the lateral one, to the extensors.

## Clinical relevance

- The proximal humerus is, together with the distal radius, the most common site of **osteosarcoma** in large breeds. The rule is "away from the elbow, toward the knee".
- Humeral condylar fractures are typical in chondrodystrophic puppies.
- Incomplete ossification of the humeral condyle (IOHC) predisposes Spaniels to fractures.
- The humeral head is the classic site of shoulder osteochondrosis.
