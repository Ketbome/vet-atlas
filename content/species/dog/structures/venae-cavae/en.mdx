## Topography

### Cranial vena cava

The *vena cava cranialis* forms at the thoracic inlet by the union of the brachiocephalic veins.

It runs through the cranial mediastinum, ventral and to the right of the trachea, and opens into the right atrium. It receives the right azygos vein.

### Caudal vena cava

The *vena cava caudalis* forms in the caudal lumbar region by the union of the common iliac veins. Its course:

- runs right of the aorta and receives the renal veins
- passes through the dorsal border of the liver, where it receives the hepatic veins
- crosses the caval foramen of the diaphragm
- opens into the right atrium

## Clinical relevance

- **Radiography**. On a lateral view the caudal vena cava is seen between the heart and the diaphragm. Its width helps assess volume status and right-sided heart failure.
- **Gastric dilatation-volvulus**. The distended stomach compresses the caudal vena cava and the portal vein. This reduces venous return and contributes to shock.
- **Portosystemic shunts**. Anomalous vessels that divert portal blood into the caudal vena cava or the azygos vein, bypassing the liver.
