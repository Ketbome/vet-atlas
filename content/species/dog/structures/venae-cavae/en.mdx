---
title: Venae cavae
summary: The two great veins that return blood to the right atrium. The cranial vena cava drains the head, neck and thoracic limbs, and the caudal vena cava drains the abdomen, pelvis and pelvic limbs.
---

## Topography

- **Cranial vena cava (*vena cava cranialis*)**: formed at the thoracic inlet by the union of the brachiocephalic veins, it runs through the cranial mediastinum ventral and to the right of the trachea and opens into the right atrium. It receives the right azygos vein.
- **Caudal vena cava (*vena cava caudalis*)**: formed in the caudal lumbar region by the common iliac veins, it runs right of the aorta and receives the renal veins. It then passes through the dorsal border of the liver, where it receives the hepatic veins, crosses the caval foramen of the diaphragm and opens into the right atrium.

## Clinical relevance

- **Radiography**: on a lateral view the caudal vena cava is seen between the heart and the diaphragm; its width helps assess volume status and right-sided heart failure.
- **Gastric dilatation-volvulus**: the distended stomach compresses the caudal vena cava and the portal vein, reducing venous return and contributing to shock.
- **Portosystemic shunts**: anomalous vessels that divert portal blood into the caudal vena cava or the azygos vein, bypassing the liver.
