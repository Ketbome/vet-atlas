## Overview

The dog has seven lumbar vertebrae, with longer and sturdier bodies than the thoracic ones. L1 follows the last thoracic vertebra (T13) and no longer has costal foveae.

Its **transverse processes** are long and flat, and point cranially and ventrally. They increase in length up to L5 or L6.

## Key features

- **Body**. Long, with a slightly keeled ventral surface.
- **Spinous process**. Wide and tilted cranially. From the anticlinal vertebra, T11, onward the spinous processes point cranially.
- **Articular processes**. The cranial ones face medially and the caudal ones laterally. They interlock in a sagittal plane that allows flexion and extension but almost no rotation.
- **Mammillary and accessory processes**. The mammillary processes sit on the cranial articular processes. The accessory processes are small caudal projections.
- **Vertebral canal**. Contains the last thoracic and first lumbar segments of the spinal cord.

## Clinical relevance

The thoracolumbar junction (T11-L3) hosts most **disc herniations**, Hansen type I intervertebral disc disease. It occurs in chondrodystrophic breeds such as the Dachshund and causes acute-onset paraparesis or paraplegia.

It is also a frequent site of traumatic vertebral fractures and luxations.

The lumbar spinous processes are landmarks for lumbar puncture, at L5-L6 in the dog.
