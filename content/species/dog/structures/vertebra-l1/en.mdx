title: First lumbar vertebra (L1)
summary: First of the seven lumbar vertebrae. Long body, transverse processes pointing cranially and ventrally, and articular processes that restrict movement to flexion and extension.

## Overview

The dog has seven lumbar vertebrae, with longer and sturdier bodies than the thoracic ones. L1 follows the last thoracic vertebra (T13) and no longer has costal foveae. Its **transverse processes** are long, flat and point cranially and ventrally, increasing in length up to L5 or L6.

## Key features

- **Body**: long, with a slightly keeled ventral surface.
- **Spinous process**: wide, tilted cranially (from the anticlinal vertebra T11 onward the spinous processes point cranially).
- **Articular processes**: the cranial ones face medially and the caudal ones laterally, interlocking in a sagittal plane that allows flexion and extension but almost no rotation.
- **Mammillary** and **accessory processes**: the mammillary processes sit on the cranial articular processes; the accessory processes are small caudal projections.
- **Vertebral canal**: contains the last thoracic and first lumbar segments of the spinal cord.

## Clinical relevance

The thoracolumbar junction (T11-L3) hosts most **disc herniations** (Hansen type I intervertebral disc disease) in chondrodystrophic breeds such as the Dachshund, with acute-onset paraparesis or paraplegia. It is also a frequent site of traumatic vertebral fractures and luxations. The lumbar spinous processes are landmarks for lumbar puncture (L5-L6 in the dog).
