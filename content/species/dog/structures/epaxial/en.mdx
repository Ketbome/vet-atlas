---
title: Epaxial muscles
summary: Muscle mass dorsal to the transverse processes, along the vertebral column.
---

## Overview

The epaxial muscles form three parallel columns, from lateral to medial:

- **Iliocostalis (*m. iliocostalis*)**: from the ilium and lumbar vertebrae to the ribs.
- **Longissimus (*m. longissimus*)**: the largest, from the ilium to the skull, between the transverse and spinous processes.
- **Transversospinalis (*m. transversospinalis*)**: short bundles next to the spinous processes (spinalis, semispinalis, multifidus, rotatores).

The model shows the three columns as a single mass, from the ilium to the axis; in the neck it also stands for the deep dorsal neck muscles.

## Origin and insertion

Ilium, sacrum, vertebral processes and ribs; each bundle spans one or several segments.

## Action

Extend and stabilise the vertebral column. Acting on one side only, they bend it laterally.

## Innervation

Dorsal branches of the spinal nerves.

## Clinical relevance

- The lumbar epaxial muscles are a common intramuscular injection site.
- Their bulk is palpated for muscle condition scoring: atrophy is an early sign of chronic disease.
- Painful spasm of these muscles often accompanies intervertebral disc disease.
