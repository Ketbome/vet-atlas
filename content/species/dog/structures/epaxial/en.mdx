## Overview

The epaxial muscles form three parallel columns, from lateral to medial:

- **Iliocostalis** (*m. iliocostalis*). Runs from the ilium and lumbar vertebrae to the ribs.
- **Longissimus** (*m. longissimus*). The largest. Runs from the ilium to the skull, between the transverse and spinous processes.
- **Transversospinalis** (*m. transversospinalis*). Short bundles next to the spinous processes: spinalis, semispinalis, multifidus and rotatores.

The model shows the three columns as a single mass, from the ilium to the axis. In the neck it also stands for the deep dorsal neck muscles.

## Origin and insertion

Ilium, sacrum, vertebral processes and ribs. Each bundle spans one or several segments.

## Action

They extend and stabilise the vertebral column. Acting on one side only, they bend it laterally.

## Innervation

Dorsal branches of the spinal nerves.

## Clinical relevance

- The lumbar epaxials are a common **intramuscular injection** site.
- Their bulk is palpated for muscle condition scoring. **Atrophy** is an early sign of chronic disease.
- Painful spasm of these muscles often accompanies intervertebral disc disease.
