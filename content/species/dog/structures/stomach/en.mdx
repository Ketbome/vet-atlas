## Topography

When empty it lies within the costal arch, caudal to the liver.

- The **cardia** and **fundus** sit on the left and dorsally, at the level of the last thoracic vertebrae.
- The **body** runs ventrally.
- The **pyloric part** crosses the median plane. The pylorus lies on the right, next to the cranial duodenal flexure.

When full, it extends past the costal arch and rests on the abdominal floor.

## Parts

- **Cardia** (*pars cardiaca*). Opening of the esophagus.
- **Fundus**. Dorsal blind sac on the left. On radiographs it usually holds gas in right lateral recumbency.
- **Body** (*corpus*). The main part, with the greater curvature, left and ventral, and the lesser curvature.
- **Pyloric part**. Antrum and pyloric canal, ending at the pyloric sphincter.

## Clinical relevance

### Gastric dilatation-volvulus (GDV)

An emergency of large deep-chested breeds. The stomach rotates on its axis and the pylorus moves from the right to the left and dorsally.

On a right lateral radiograph the stomach looks compartmentalised, the "double bubble". **Gastropexy** prevents recurrence.

### Other conditions

- **Foreign bodies and gastric ulcers**. Frequent. Diagnosed by radiography, ultrasound or endoscopy.
- **Gastric axis**. On a lateral radiograph the fundus-pylorus line should run almost parallel to the ribs. A shifted axis suggests a change in liver size.
