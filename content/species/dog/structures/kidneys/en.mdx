## Topography

- **Right kidney**. Ventral to the transverse processes of T13 to L1-L2. Its cranial pole sits in the renal fossa of the caudate process of the liver.
- **Left kidney**. More caudal and somewhat more mobile, roughly L1 to L3. It moves caudally when the stomach is full.

Both lie dorsally in the abdomen, outside the peritoneum, wrapped in perirenal fat.

## Structure

- **Cortex and medulla**, with a common renal crest. The dog has no separate renal papillae.
- **Renal pelvis** and **hilus**. The renal artery enters at the hilus, and the renal vein and ureter leave there.

## Clinical relevance

### Radiographic size

On a ventrodorsal view each kidney measures about **2.5 to 3.5 times** the length of the body of L2.

### Diseases

- Chronic kidney disease, with small, irregular kidneys in older dogs.
- Urolithiasis. Stones can form in the renal pelvis and obstruct the ureter.
- Pyelonephritis, from infection ascending from the bladder.
