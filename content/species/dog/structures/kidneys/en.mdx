title: Kidneys
summary: Bean-shaped retroperitoneal organs on either side of the aorta and caudal vena cava. The right kidney lies cranial to the left.

## Topography

- **Right kidney**: ventral to the transverse processes of T13 to L1-L2; its cranial pole sits in the renal fossa of the caudate process of the liver.
- **Left kidney**: more caudal and somewhat more mobile, roughly L1 to L3; it moves caudally when the stomach is full.
- Both lie dorsally in the abdomen, outside the peritoneum, wrapped in perirenal fat.

## Structure

- **Cortex and medulla**, with a common renal crest (the dog has no separate renal papillae).
- **Renal pelvis** and **hilus**, where the renal artery enters and the renal vein and ureter leave.

## Clinical relevance

- **Radiographic size**: on a ventrodorsal view each kidney measures about 2.5 to 3.5 times the length of the body of L2.
- **Chronic kidney disease**: small, irregular kidneys in older dogs.
- **Urolithiasis**: stones can form in the renal pelvis and obstruct the ureter.
- **Pyelonephritis**: infection ascending from the bladder.
