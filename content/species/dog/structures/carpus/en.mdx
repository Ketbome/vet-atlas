## Overview

The carpus of the dog has seven bones arranged in a proximal and a distal row. Together they form the carpal joint (*articulatio carpi*), a composite joint.

Movement is shared between three levels:

- **antebrachiocarpal**, where most of it happens
- **middle carpal**, with less movement
- **carpometacarpal**, with almost none

In the standing dog the carpus is slightly hyperextended.

## Proximal row

- **Radial carpal bone** (*os carpi radiale*). The largest carpal bone, formed by the fusion of the radial, intermediate and central carpal bones. It articulates with most of the distal radius.
- **Ulnar carpal bone** (*os carpi ulnare*). Lateral. It articulates with the radius and the styloid process of the ulna.
- **Accessory carpal bone** (*os carpi accessorium*). Projects palmarly from the lateral side and acts as a lever for the flexor carpi ulnaris. It is palpable behind the carpus.

## Distal row

The **first to fourth carpal bones** (*ossa carpalia I–IV*), from medial to lateral.

- The fourth is the largest and articulates with metacarpals IV and V.
- The first is the smallest and supports metacarpal I, the dewclaw.

## Clinical relevance

- **Carpal hyperextension injury**. A fall or jump ruptures the palmar ligaments and fibrocartilage, and the dog walks on the back of the carpus. It is usually treated by arthrodesis.
- **Fractures of the accessory carpal bone**. Typical of racing greyhounds.
- **Carpal laxity**. Hyperextension or flexion deformity in growing puppies. It usually resolves with controlled exercise.
