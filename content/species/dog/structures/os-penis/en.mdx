## Overview

The os penis is a heterotopic bone of the visceral skeleton. It develops within the corpus cavernosum of the glans penis.

It is long and fairly straight. It has a broad caudal base and a tapering cranial apex prolonged by fibrocartilage.

It keeps the penis rigid during intromission, before full erection.

## Key features

- **Urethral groove** (*sulcus urethrae*). Deep groove on the ventral surface that lodges the penile urethra and the corpus spongiosum. It is widest at the base and becomes shallower towards the apex.
- **Base**. Caudal, thick end, continuous with the corpus cavernosum.
- **Apex**. Cranial, thin end. It ends in a fibrocartilaginous tip inside the glans.

## Clinical relevance

- **Urethral obstruction**. Struvite, oxalate, urate or cystine stones often lodge just caudal to the os penis, where the urethra cannot expand inside the bony groove. A urethrostomy, scrotal or prescrotal, bypasses this narrow segment.
- **Fractures**. Rare. They can cause pain, haematuria and urethral stricture.
- **Radiology**. The os penis and its groove must not be confused with stones or mineralisation.
