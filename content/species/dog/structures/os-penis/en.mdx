## Overview

The os penis is a heterotopic bone (part of the visceral skeleton) that develops within the corpus cavernosum of the glans penis. It is long and fairly straight, with a broad caudal **base** and a tapering cranial **apex** prolonged by fibrocartilage. It keeps the penis rigid during intromission, before full erection.

## Key features

- **Urethral groove (*sulcus urethrae*)**: deep groove on the ventral surface that lodges the penile urethra and the corpus spongiosum. It is widest at the base and becomes shallower towards the apex.
- **Base**: caudal, thick end, continuous with the corpus cavernosum.
- **Apex**: cranial, thin end, ending in a fibrocartilaginous tip inside the glans.

## Clinical relevance

- **Urethral obstruction**: urinary stones (struvite, oxalate, urate, cystine) often lodge just caudal to the os penis, where the urethra cannot expand inside the bony groove. A urethrostomy (scrotal or prescrotal) bypasses this narrow segment.
- **Fractures of the os penis** are rare; they can cause pain, haematuria and urethral stricture.
- On radiographs the os penis and its groove must not be confused with stones or mineralisation.
