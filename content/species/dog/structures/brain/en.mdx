## Topography

The brain fills the cranial cavity.

- The **olfactory bulbs**, large in the dog, sit rostrally in the ethmoidal fossa, between the orbits.
- The **cerebral hemispheres** fill most of the vault.
- The **cerebellum** lies caudal to them, in the caudal cranial fossa. It is separated from the cerebrum by the tentorium cerebelli, which is partly ossified in the dog.
- The **brainstem** rests on the floor of the skull and leaves through the *foramen magnum* towards the atlas.

## Parts

- **Cerebrum**. Two hemispheres with gyri and sulci, joined by the corpus callosum. It includes the olfactory bulbs and tracts.
- **Cerebellum**. Median vermis and lateral hemispheres. It coordinates posture and movement.
- **Brainstem** (*truncus encephali*). Midbrain, pons and medulla oblongata. Most cranial nerves arise from it.

## Clinical relevance

- **Idiopathic epilepsy**. The most common cause of recurrent seizures in young and adult dogs.
- **Hydrocephalus**. Common in toy and brachycephalic breeds, with a domed skull and persistent fontanelle.
- **Chiari-like malformation**. In the Cavalier King Charles Spaniel the cerebellum is pushed into the *foramen magnum*, often with syringomyelia.
- **Meningioma**. The most common intracranial tumour of older dogs.
