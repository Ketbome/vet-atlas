## Overview

The caudal, or coccygeal, vertebrae are usually about twenty. Their number varies between dogs and breeds.

They articulate with the apex of the sacrum and with each other through intervertebral discs, which makes the tail very mobile. Their length increases over the first vertebrae and then decreases steadily towards the tip.

## Key features

### First caudal vertebrae

- complete vertebral arch
- vertebral canal containing only the last nerve roots of the **cauda equina**, not spinal cord
- short spinous processes
- well-developed transverse processes
- cranial and caudal articular processes

### Middle and distal vertebrae

The arch disappears and the processes are reduced to small cranial and caudal tubercles. What remains is a cylindrical body.

### Hemal arches

The **hemal arches** (*arcus haemales*) are small V-shaped bones on the ventral side of the first caudal vertebrae. Together with the ventral processes they protect the median caudal artery, which runs along the ventral surface of the tail.

## Clinical relevance

- **Sacrocaudal luxation and fractures**. They follow trauma, such as tail-pull injuries. They can damage the caudal and pelvic nerves and cause loss of tail tone and urinary or faecal incontinence.
- **Screw tail**. Hemivertebrae and fused caudal vertebrae in brachycephalic breeds. They produce a tightly curled tail and skin-fold dermatitis.
