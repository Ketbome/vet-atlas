## Overview

The tarsus of the dog consists of seven bones arranged in three rows. Proximally lie the tibia and fibula; distally, the metatarsal bones.

Together they form the hock joint (*articulatio tarsi*), a composite joint:

- almost all flexion and extension takes place in the **proximal row**
- the middle and distal rows are nearly rigid and act as shock absorbers

## Proximal row

### Talus

The talus (*talus*) is the medial bone of the proximal row.

Its trochlea (*trochlea tali*), with two sagittal ridges, fits into the tibial cochlea and defines the hinge axis of the tarsocrural joint.

Its head articulates distally with the central tarsal bone.

### Calcaneus

The calcaneus (*calcaneus*) is the lateral bone and the largest of the tarsus.

It extends proximally as the **calcaneal tuber** (*tuber calcanei*), the lever of the hock, where the common calcanean tendon, or Achilles tendon, inserts.

On its medial face it carries the sustentaculum tali (*sustentaculum tali*). The tendon of the lateral digital flexor glides over its plantar surface.

Distally it articulates with the fourth tarsal bone.

## Middle row

The central tarsal bone (*os tarsi centrale*) lies between the talus and tarsal bones I, II and III. It exists only in the medial half of the tarsus.

## Distal row

These are tarsal bones I, II, III and IV (*ossa tarsalia I–IV*).

- I is small and sometimes rudimentary.
- II and III lie between the central bone and metatarsals II and III.
- IV is the largest. It occupies both the middle and distal rows on the lateral side, and articulates proximally with the calcaneus and distally with metatarsals IV and V.

## Joints

- **Tarsocrural**, between the tibia and fibula and the talus and calcaneus. The main hinge. It is stabilised by the medial and lateral collateral ligaments, each with a long and a short part.
- **Proximal and distal intertarsal and tarsometatarsal joints**. Low-motion joints reinforced by strong plantar ligaments.

## Clinical relevance

- **Osteochondrosis of the talus**. Mainly affects the medial ridge of the trochlea, in young Rottweilers and Labradors.
- **Central tarsal bone fractures**. The typical injury of the racing greyhound. They almost always affect the right limb, because of the loading in the bends.
- **Calcaneal fractures and rupture of the common calcanean tendon**. The animal rests the hock on the ground, in a plantigrade stance.
- **Tarsal luxations and shearing injuries**. Loss of a malleolus or a collateral ligament destabilises the tarsocrural joint. Irreparable injuries are managed by pantarsal or partial tarsal arthrodesis.
