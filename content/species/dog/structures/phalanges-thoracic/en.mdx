## Overview

The forepaw of the dog has five digits.

- Digits II to V each have a proximal, a middle and a distal phalanx (*phalanx proximalis, media et distalis*).
- Digit I, the dewclaw, has only a proximal and a distal phalanx.

The dog stands on its digits, in a digitigrade stance. The phalanges of digits III and IV are the longest and carry most of the load, under the digital pads.

## Key features

- **Proximal phalanx**. The longest; it articulates with the head of the metacarpal bone.
- **Sesamoids**. Two proximal sesamoid bones lie on the palmar side of each metacarpophalangeal joint, in the tendon of the interosseous muscle. A small dorsal sesamoid sits on the dorsal side.
- **Middle phalanx**. Shorter, with a proximal base and a distal head.
- **Distal phalanx**. Carries the claw. Its ungual crest (*crista unguicularis*) overlaps the base of the claw and its unguicular process (*processus unguicularis*) forms the core of the claw.

Elastic dorsal ligaments keep the distal phalanx raised when the digital flexor muscles relax.

## Clinical relevance

- **Subungual tumours**. Squamous cell carcinoma and melanoma destroy the distal phalanx. Treatment is amputation of the digit.
- **Sesamoid disease**. Fragmentation of the proximal sesamoid bones, especially the second and seventh of the forepaw, is common in Rottweilers and other large breeds. It is often incidental on radiographs.
- **Dewclaw injuries**. Dewclaw claws are often torn or overgrown because they do not wear down on the ground.
