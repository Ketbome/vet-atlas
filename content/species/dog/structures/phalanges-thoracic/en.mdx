## Overview

The forepaw of the dog has five digits. Digits II to V each have a **proximal**, a **middle** and a **distal phalanx** (*phalanx proximalis, media et distalis*); digit I (the dewclaw) has only a proximal and a distal phalanx. The dog stands on its digits (digitigrade stance): the phalanges of digits III and IV are the longest and carry most of the load, under the digital pads.

## Key features

- **Proximal phalanx**: the longest; articulates with the head of the metacarpal bone. Two **proximal sesamoid bones** lie on the palmar side of each metacarpophalangeal joint, in the tendon of the interosseous muscle, and a small dorsal sesamoid sits on its dorsal side.
- **Middle phalanx**: shorter, with a proximal base and a distal head.
- **Distal phalanx**: carries the claw. Its **ungual crest (*crista unguicularis*)** overlaps the base of the claw and its **unguicular process (*processus unguicularis*)** forms the core of the claw. Elastic dorsal ligaments keep the distal phalanx raised when the digital flexor muscles relax.

## Clinical relevance

- **Subungual tumours** (squamous cell carcinoma, melanoma) destroy the distal phalanx; treatment is amputation of the digit.
- **Sesamoid disease**: fragmentation of the proximal sesamoid bones, especially the second and seventh of the forepaw, is common in Rottweilers and other large breeds and often incidental on radiographs.
- **Dewclaw injuries**: torn or overgrown dewclaw claws are frequent because they do not wear down on the ground.
