## Overview

The atlas is the most atypical vertebra. It has no body and no spinous process.

It consists of two arches, dorsal and ventral, joined by two lateral masses. These extend into the **wings of the atlas** (*alae atlantis*), wide and palpable just caudal to the skull.

## Key features

- **Cranial articular foveae**. Concave surfaces that receive the occipital condyles. The atlanto-occipital joint allows flexion and extension, the "yes" movement.
- **Caudal articular foveae**. They articulate with the axis (C2). The atlantoaxial joint allows rotation around the dens of the axis, the "no" movement.
- **Fovea dentis**. On the dorsal surface of the ventral arch. The dens of the axis rests here, held by the transverse ligament.
- **Lateral vertebral foramen and alar notch**. Passage of the first cervical nerve root and the vertebral artery.
- **Transverse foramen**. At the base of the wing.

## Clinical relevance

- **Atlantoaxial instability**. Caused by hypoplasia or aplasia of the dens, or by rupture of the transverse ligament. It causes cervical spinal cord compression in young toy breeds.
- **Cerebellomedullary cistern puncture**. The wings of the atlas are the landmark for collecting cerebrospinal fluid between the occiput and the atlas.
- **Fractures**. Rare and usually traumatic.
