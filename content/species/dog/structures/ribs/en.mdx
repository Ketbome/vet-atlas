## Overview

The dog has thirteen pairs of ribs, matching the thirteen thoracic vertebrae.

- The first nine are **sternal**: their cartilage reaches the sternum.
- The last four are **asternal**: their cartilages join each other and form the costal arch.
- The thirteenth is usually **floating**.

Each rib has a dorsal bony part and a ventral costal cartilage, joined at the costochondral junction.

## Key features

- **Head** (*caput costae*). Articulates with the costal foveae of two adjacent vertebrae, except in the last ribs.
- **Neck and tubercle** (*tuberculum costae*). The tubercle articulates with the transverse process of the vertebra of the same number.
- **Costal angle**. Point of greatest curvature, most marked in the middle ribs.
- **Body**. Flattened. Its caudal border carries the costal groove, where the intercostal vessels and nerve run.
- **First rib**. Short, wide and almost straight. With T1 and the manubrium it bounds the thoracic inlet.

## Clinical relevance

- **Rib fractures**. Common after trauma such as road accidents; they may come with pneumothorax or pulmonary contusion.
- **Intercostal spaces**. The access route for thoracocentesis, at the 7th-8th space, and for thoracotomy.
- **Last rib**. A landmark for locating the kidney and for body condition scoring.
