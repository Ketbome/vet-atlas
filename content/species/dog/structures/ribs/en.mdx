## Overview

The dog has thirteen pairs of ribs, matching the thirteen thoracic vertebrae. The first nine are **sternal** (their cartilage reaches the sternum), the last four are **asternal** (their cartilages join each other forming the costal arch) and the thirteenth is usually **floating**. Each rib has a dorsal bony part and a ventral costal cartilage, joined at the costochondral junction.

In this model ribs 1, 4, 9 and 13 come from scans of a real dog; the others were interpolated between those four, and the costal cartilages are schematic. The right side mirrors the left.

## Key features

- **Head (*caput costae*)**: articulates with the costal foveae of two adjacent vertebrae (except in the last ribs).
- **Neck and tubercle (*tuberculum costae*)**: the tubercle articulates with the transverse process of the vertebra of the same number.
- **Costal angle**: point of greatest curvature, most marked in the middle ribs.
- **Body**: flattened, with the costal groove on the caudal border carrying the intercostal vessels and nerve.
- **First rib**: short, wide and almost straight; with T1 and the manubrium it bounds the thoracic inlet.

## Clinical relevance

Rib fractures are common after trauma (road accidents) and may come with pneumothorax or pulmonary contusion. The intercostal spaces are the access route for thoracocentesis (7th-8th space) and thoracotomy. The last rib is a landmark for locating the kidney and for body condition scoring.
