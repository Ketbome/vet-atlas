## Parts

- **Caecum** (*caecum*). Short spiral diverticulum on the right, at the level of the cranial lumbar vertebrae. It communicates only with the colon, through the caecocolic orifice.
- **Ascending colon**. A short segment running cranially on the right.
- **Transverse colon**. Crosses from right to left, cranial to the root of the mesentery and caudal to the stomach.
- **Descending colon**. The longest segment. It runs along the left side of the dorsal abdomen, ventral to the sublumbar muscles, to the pelvic inlet.
- **Rectum and anal canal**. They cross the pelvic cavity, ventral to the sacrum, to the anus. The anal sacs open on either side of the anus, at 4 and 8 o'clock.

## Clinical relevance

### Rectal palpation

Assesses the prostate, pelvic urethra, sublumbar lymph nodes and pelvic canal.

### Diseases

- Colitis, with mucoid diarrhoea, tenesmus and frequent small stools.
- Anal sac disease: impaction, sacculitis and abscesses. **Anal sac adenocarcinoma** can cause hypercalcaemia.
- Perineal hernia, in older intact males. The rectum deviates between the muscles of the pelvic diaphragm.
