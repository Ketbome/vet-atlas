---
title: Triceps brachii
summary: Large muscle mass caudal to the humerus; the main extensor of the elbow.
---

## Overview

In the dog it has four heads ending on the olecranon: long, lateral, medial and accessory. The model shows the first three; the accessory head is deep and hidden between them.

## Origin

- **Long head (*caput longum*)**: caudal border of the scapula. The only head that crosses the shoulder.
- **Lateral head (*caput laterale*)**: tricipital line on the lateral surface of the proximal humerus.
- **Medial head (*caput mediale*)**: medial surface of the humerus, in its proximal third.

## Insertion

Olecranon tuber (*tuber olecrani*) of the ulna.

## Action

Extends the elbow and supports body weight when standing. The long head also flexes the shoulder.

## Innervation

Radial nerve (*n. radialis*).

## Clinical relevance

Radial nerve injury prevents elbow extension: the dog cannot bear weight on the limb and drags it. The long head is an intramuscular injection site; the distal arm is avoided because the radial nerve lies close to the humerus there.
