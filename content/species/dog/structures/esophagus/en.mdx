## Topography

- **Cervical part**. Starts dorsal to the cricoid cartilage and runs dorsal to the trachea. In the caudal half of the neck it shifts to the trachea's left side.
- **Thoracic part**. Enters the thorax dorsal and to the left of the trachea. It passes dorsal to the tracheal bifurcation and the heart base, right of the aortic arch, and continues through the caudal mediastinum.
- **Abdominal part**. Very short. It crosses the diaphragm through the esophageal hiatus, ventral to the aorta, and opens into the cardia of the stomach.

## Structure

In the dog the muscular coat is **striated along its whole length**. This is why the esophagus is involved in neuromuscular diseases.

## Clinical relevance

- **Megaesophagus**. Dilation with regurgitation. It can be congenital or acquired, for example from myasthenia gravis or hypoadrenocorticism.
- **Foreign bodies**. They lodge at three narrow points: the thoracic inlet, the heart base and the esophageal hiatus.
- **Vascular ring anomalies**. A **persistent right aortic arch** strangles the esophagus at the heart base and causes regurgitation from weaning.
