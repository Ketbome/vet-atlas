---
title: Esophagus
summary: Muscular tube carrying food from the pharynx to the stomach, through the neck, the mediastinum and the esophageal hiatus of the diaphragm.
---

## Topography

- **Cervical part**: starts dorsal to the cricoid cartilage and runs dorsal to the trachea, shifting to its left side in the caudal half of the neck.
- **Thoracic part**: enters the thorax dorsal and left of the trachea, passes dorsal to the tracheal bifurcation and the heart base, right of the aortic arch, and continues through the caudal mediastinum.
- **Abdominal part**: very short; it crosses the diaphragm through the esophageal hiatus, ventral to the aorta, and opens into the cardia of the stomach.

## Structure

In the dog the muscular coat is striated along its whole length, which explains its link with neuromuscular diseases.

## Clinical relevance

- **Megaesophagus**: dilation with regurgitation; congenital or acquired (myasthenia gravis, hypoadrenocorticism).
- **Foreign bodies**: lodge at three narrow points: the thoracic inlet, the heart base and the esophageal hiatus.
- **Vascular ring anomalies**: a persistent right aortic arch strangles the esophagus at the heart base and causes regurgitation from weaning.
