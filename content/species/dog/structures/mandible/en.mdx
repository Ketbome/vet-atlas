## Overview

The dog mandible is made of two **hemimandibles**. They are joined rostrally by the **mandibular symphysis**, a fibrocartilaginous joint that never fully ossifies.

Each half has:

- a horizontal **body**, holding the teeth
- a vertical **ramus**, where the masticatory muscles insert and which articulates with the skull

## Parts

- **Body** (*corpus mandibulae*). With the alveoli of the lower incisors, canine, four premolars and three molars. The adult dental formula is 3/3 I, 1/1 C, 4/4 PM, 2/3 M. The first lower molar is the lower **carnassial tooth**.
- **Mental foramina**. On the lateral surface of the body. The inferior alveolar nerve leaves through them; this is the site of the mental nerve block.
- **Mandibular foramen**. On the medial surface of the ramus. The inferior alveolar nerve enters through it; this is the site of the mandibular nerve block.
- **Coronoid process**. Tall and wide. The temporalis muscle inserts on it.
- **Condylar process**. Articulates with the mandibular fossa of the temporal bone at the temporomandibular joint.
- **Angular process**. Caudal projection where the masseter and pterygoid muscles insert.

## Clinical relevance

- **Mandibular fractures**. Common after trauma. Small dogs with advanced periodontal disease get pathological fractures of the body at the first molar.
- **Symphyseal separation**. Frequent in cats and in dogs after falls.
- **Temporomandibular luxation** and **open-mouth jaw locking** by the coronoid process. Other conditions to recognise.
