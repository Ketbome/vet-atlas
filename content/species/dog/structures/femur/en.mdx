## Overview

The femur is the bone of the thigh, and the longest and strongest bone of the dog. It carries the weight of the trunk to the leg through two joints:

- the **hip**, or coxofemoral joint, at its proximal end
- the **stifle** (*articulatio genus*), the dog's knee, at its distal end

## Proximal end

- **Head** (*caput ossis femoris*). Spherical articular surface that sits in the acetabulum. It carries a small depression, the *fovea capitis*, where the ligament of the femoral head attaches.
- **Neck** (*collum ossis femoris*). Joins the head to the shaft. The angle between them, the angle of inclination, is about 145° in the dog and is one of the measurements used when assessing hip dysplasia.
- **Greater trochanter** (*trochanter major*). Large lateral projection where the gluteal muscles insert. It is palpable in the live animal and is the landmark for hip joint injection.
- **Lesser trochanter** (*trochanter minor*). Smaller medial projection where the iliopsoas muscle inserts.
- **Trochanteric fossa** (*fossa trochanterica*). Depression between the neck and the greater trochanter.

## Shaft

The shaft, or diaphysis, is cylindrical and slightly convex cranially. Its caudal surface carries the *facies aspera*, the "rough surface", with medial and lateral lips where the adductor muscles insert.

## Distal end

- **Trochlea** (*trochlea ossis femoris*). Cranial groove in which the patella glides. The shallower it is, the more easily the patella slips out.
- **Medial and lateral condyles** (*condylus medialis et lateralis*). Caudal articular surfaces that contact the menisci and the tibia.
- **Intercondylar fossa**. The space between the condyles, which houses the cruciate ligaments.
- **Fabellae**. Two small sesamoid bones in the tendons of origin of the gastrocnemius, caudal to the condyles. They show on lateral stifle radiographs and must not be mistaken for fragments.

## Clinical relevance

Several of the dog's most frequent orthopaedic diseases involve the femur:

- hip dysplasia, in the head and neck
- Legg-Calvé-Perthes disease, a necrosis of the head seen in small breeds
- patellar luxation, tied to the trochlea
- diaphyseal fractures after trauma
- osteosarcoma of the distal femur, in large breeds
