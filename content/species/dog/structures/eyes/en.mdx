## Overview

The eye (*oculus*) consists of the eyeball (*bulbus oculi*) and its accessory organs: eyelids, conjunctiva, lacrimal apparatus and extrinsic muscles. In the dog the orbit is not closed by bone caudolaterally: the orbital ligament completes the ring between the zygomatic process of the frontal bone and the zygomatic arch.

## What is visible from outside

- **Cornea (*cornea*)**: transparent anterior part of the fibrous tunic, through which the iris and pupil are seen.
- **Sclera (*sclera*)**: white, opaque part of the fibrous tunic; in the dog it barely shows with the eye in its normal position.
- **Eyelids (*palpebrae*)**: skin folds with tarsus, tarsal glands and eyelashes on the upper lid. The lower lid has few or no lashes.
- **Third eyelid (*plica semilunaris conjunctivae*)**: a conjunctival fold with a T-shaped cartilage at the medial angle. Its gland supplies a large share of the tear film.

## Clinical relevance

- **Prolapse of the third eyelid gland ("cherry eye")**: a red mass at the medial angle, common in young and brachycephalic dogs.
- **Entropion and ectropion**: the lid margin rolls in or out; entropion irritates the cornea with hair.
- **Keratoconjunctivitis sicca**: deficient aqueous tear production, measured with the Schirmer tear test.
- **Corneal ulcer**: confirmed with fluorescein, which stains exposed stroma.
