## Topography

The heart, wrapped in the pericardium, fills the middle mediastinum between the two lungs.

- It spans roughly the 3rd to 6th intercostal spaces.
- It lies mostly left of the median plane.
- The **base**, where the great vessels leave, faces dorsocranially at mid-thoracic height.
- The **apex** points caudoventrally and to the left, close to the sternum and the costochondral junction of the 6th–7th rib.

## Parts

- **Right atrium and ventricle**. They receive blood from the venae cavae and pump it into the pulmonary trunk. The right ventricle forms the cranial border on a lateral radiograph.
- **Left atrium and ventricle**. The dorsocaudal left atrium receives the pulmonary veins. The thick-walled left ventricle forms the apex and ejects blood into the aorta.
- **Valves**. Tricuspid or right atrioventricular, mitral or left atrioventricular, pulmonary and aortic.

## Clinical relevance

### Auscultation

- Mitral area at the left 5th intercostal space, at the costochondral junction.
- Aortic area at the left 4th intercostal space.
- Pulmonic area at the left 3rd intercostal space.
- Tricuspid area at the right 3rd–4th intercostal space.

### Radiographic size

The vertebral heart score adds the long and short cardiac axes, measured in thoracic vertebrae from T4. In most breeds the normal value is about **9.7 ± 0.5**.

### Common diseases

- **Myxomatous mitral valve disease** is the most common acquired heart disease, especially in small breeds.
- Dilated cardiomyopathy is typical of large and giant breeds, such as the Dobermann and the Great Dane.
- Patent ductus arteriosus is a common congenital defect, with a continuous murmur at the left heart base.
