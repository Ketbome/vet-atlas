## Overview

The axis (C2) is the longest vertebra of the dog's neck. Unlike the atlas, it has a long body and a very large spinous process.

With the atlas it forms the atlantoaxial joint, a pivot joint responsible for most of the rotation of the head.

## Key features

- **Dens** (*dens axis*). Peg-like cranial projection of the body. It fits into the vertebral foramen of the atlas, on its ventral floor. It is held in place by the transverse ligament of the atlas and by the apical and alar ligaments.
- **Cranial articular surfaces**. On each side of the dens. They articulate with the caudal articular foveae of the atlas.
- **Spinous process** (*processus spinosus*). A long, blade-like crest that overhangs the atlas cranially. It ends caudally in a thickened part bearing the caudal articular processes. It gives attachment to the nuchal ligament.
- **Transverse processes**. Small and directed caudally. Their bases are perforated by the transverse foramen.
- **Caudal articular processes**. They articulate with the third cervical vertebra.

## Clinical relevance

**Atlantoaxial instability** is a congenital subluxation of toy breeds: Yorkshire terrier, Chihuahua and toy poodle.

It results from an absent, hypoplastic or separated dens, or from missing ligaments. The axis moves dorsally into the vertebral canal and compresses the spinal cord. It causes neck pain and tetraparesis.

Traumatic fractures of the dens produce the same instability.
