## Overview

The fibula is a long, gracile bone lying lateral to the tibia and of similar length. In the dog it keeps its full length; in ungulates it is reduced.

Its main function is not to bear weight, which falls on the tibia. It gives origin to several muscles and stabilises the lateral side of the tarsal joint.

## Head

The **head** (*caput fibulae*) is the flattened proximal end. It articulates with a small facet on the caudolateral face of the lateral tibial condyle. This proximal tibiofibular joint has very little movement.

The lateral collateral ligament of the stifle attaches to the head.

The **common fibular nerve**, or common peroneal nerve, crosses just caudal to the head, in a very superficial position.

## Shaft

The shaft (*corpus fibulae*) is thin and slightly flattened. It is separated from the tibia by the **interosseous space of the leg** (*spatium interosseum cruris*), which holds the interosseous membrane and is wider in the proximal half.

- Its lateral surface gives origin to the fibularis longus, fibularis brevis and lateral digital extensor.
- Its caudal surface gives origin to part of the digital flexors.

## Lateral malleolus

The distal end thickens to form the **lateral malleolus** (*malleolus lateralis*). It articulates with the tibia at the distal tibiofibular joint and, laterally, with the talus and the calcaneus.

- It carries a groove for the tendons of the fibular muscles and the lateral digital extensor.
- It gives origin to the **lateral collateral ligament of the tarsus**, with its long and short parts.
- It is palpable as the lateral prominence of the hock.

## Clinical relevance

- The fibula rarely breaks on its own; it usually fractures together with the tibia. It then needs no fixation of its own, unless it is used to add rotational stability to the repair.
- A **fracture of the lateral malleolus** amounts to an injury of the lateral collateral ligament of the tarsus and causes instability. It is reduced and fixed with pins and a tension band.
- In shearing injuries from road traffic accidents, friction against the asphalt can strip off the lateral malleolus and the collateral ligament.
- The TPLO osteotomy is made in the proximal tibia, next to the fibula. Fibular fracture is a recognised complication of the technique.
- The superficial course of the fibular nerve beside the head makes it vulnerable to trauma and to compression by bandages.
