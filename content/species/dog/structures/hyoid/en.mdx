## Overview

The hyoid apparatus (*apparatus hyoideus*) is a set of small bones and cartilages hanging from the base of the skull. It supports the root of the tongue and suspends the larynx.

Its central piece is the **basihyoid**, an unpaired transverse bone in the floor of the pharynx, between the mandibular rami. From it the horns run craniodorsally to the skull and the thyrohyoids run caudally to the larynx.

The model comes from a plastinate in which the hyoid is preserved articulated with the **larynx** and the start of the trachea. The larynx includes the epiglottic, thyroid, arytenoid and cricoid cartilages. Only the hyoid is bone; the larynx is shown because it forms a functional unit with it.

## Parts

- **Basihyoid** (*basihyoideum*). Transverse body, palpable between the mandibular rami in a relaxed dog.
- **Rostral horns**. The ceratohyoid, epihyoid and stylohyoid form a chain from the basihyoid to the **tympanohyoid**. The tympanohyoid is cartilaginous and attaches to the mastoid process of the temporal bone.
- **Caudal horns, or thyrohyoids**. They join the basihyoid to the lamina of the thyroid cartilage.
- **Muscles**. The mylohyoideus, geniohyoideus, sternohyoideus and thyrohyoideus move the tongue and larynx during swallowing, breathing and vocalisation.

## Clinical relevance

- The hyoid takes part in **swallowing** and in positioning the larynx.
- Its fracture or luxation, after cervical trauma or bite wounds, causes dysphagia and dysphonia.
- It is a radiographic landmark for the larynx and for diagnosing laryngeal paralysis and laryngeal collapse in brachycephalic breeds.
- In laryngeal surgery, such as arytenoid lateralisation, and in tracheostomy the basihyoid and thyroid cartilage are identified.
