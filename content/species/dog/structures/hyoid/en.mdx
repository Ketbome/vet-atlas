## Overview

The hyoid apparatus (*apparatus hyoideus*) is a set of small bones and cartilages hanging from the base of the skull that supports the root of the tongue and suspends the larynx. Its central piece is the **basihyoid**, an unpaired transverse bone in the floor of the pharynx between the mandibular rami; from it the horns run craniodorsally to the skull and the thyrohyoids run caudally to the larynx.

The model comes from a plastinate in which the hyoid is preserved articulated with the **larynx** (epiglottic, thyroid, arytenoid and cricoid cartilages) and the start of the trachea. Only the hyoid is bone; the larynx is shown because it forms a functional unit with it.

## Key features

- **Basihyoid (*basihyoideum*)**: transverse body, palpable between the mandibular rami in a relaxed dog.
- **Rostral horns**: **ceratohyoid**, **epihyoid** and **stylohyoid**, chained from the basihyoid to the **tympanohyoid**, which is cartilaginous and attaches to the mastoid process of the temporal bone.
- **Caudal horns (thyrohyoids)**: join the basihyoid to the lamina of the thyroid cartilage.
- **Muscles**: the hyoid muscles (mylohyoideus, geniohyoideus, sternohyoideus, thyrohyoideus) move the tongue and larynx during swallowing, breathing and vocalisation.

## Clinical relevance

The hyoid takes part in **swallowing** and in positioning the larynx; its fracture or luxation (cervical trauma, bite wounds) causes dysphagia and dysphonia. It is a radiographic landmark for the larynx and for diagnosing laryngeal paralysis and laryngeal collapse in brachycephalic breeds. In laryngeal surgery (arytenoid lateralisation) and tracheostomy the basihyoid and thyroid cartilage are identified.
