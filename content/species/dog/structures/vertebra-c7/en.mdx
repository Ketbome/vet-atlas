## Overview

The dog has seven cervical vertebrae. C7 is the last and shows transitional features: its **spinous process** is clearly taller than in the preceding cervical vertebrae and its body is shorter. Unlike C3 to C6, it **has no transverse foramen**, because the vertebral artery enters the transverse canal from C6 onward.

## Key features

- **Caudal costal foveae**: small facets on the caudal border of the body that, together with T1, receive the head of the first rib.
- **Transverse processes**: simple, without the ventral lamina typical of C6.
- **Articular processes**: cranial surfaces facing dorsomedially and caudal surfaces facing ventrolaterally, as in the rest of the neck.
- **Wide vertebral canal**, carrying the cervical intumescence of the spinal cord.

## Clinical relevance

The cervicothoracic junction (C6-T1) is the usual site of **caudal cervical spondylomyelopathy** ("Wobbler syndrome") in large breeds such as the Dobermann and the Great Dane, with canal stenosis, disc protrusion and spinal cord compression. It is also a hard region to assess on lateral radiographs because the scapulae and shoulders overlap it.
