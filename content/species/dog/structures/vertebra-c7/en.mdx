## Overview

The dog has seven cervical vertebrae. C7 is the last and shows transitional features:

- its **spinous process** is clearly taller than in the preceding cervical vertebrae
- its body is shorter
- it **has no transverse foramen**, unlike C3 to C6, because the vertebral artery enters the transverse canal from C6 onward

## Key features

- **Caudal costal foveae**. Small facets on the caudal border of the body. Together with T1 they receive the head of the first rib.
- **Transverse processes**. Simple, without the ventral lamina typical of C6.
- **Articular processes**. The cranial surfaces face dorsomedially and the caudal ones ventrolaterally, as in the rest of the neck.
- **Vertebral canal**. Wide. It carries the cervical intumescence of the spinal cord.

## Clinical relevance

The cervicothoracic junction (C6-T1) is the usual site of **caudal cervical spondylomyelopathy**, or Wobbler syndrome. It affects large breeds such as the Dobermann and the Great Dane, with canal stenosis, disc protrusion and spinal cord compression.

It is also a hard region to assess on lateral radiographs, because the scapulae and shoulders overlap it.
