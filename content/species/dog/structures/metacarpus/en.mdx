## Overview

The metacarpus has five metacarpal bones (*ossa metacarpalia I–V*), numbered from medial to lateral.

- **Metacarpal I** is short and does not reach the ground.
- II to V lie side by side.
- **III and IV** are the longest and the main weight-bearing bones. II and V are slightly shorter.

## Parts of each bone

- **Base** (*basis*). Proximal. It articulates with the distal row of carpal bones and with the neighbouring metacarpals.
- **Body** (*corpus*). Slender and slightly curved, convex dorsally.
- **Head** (*caput*). Distal. It articulates with the proximal phalanx at the metacarpophalangeal joint. A sagittal ridge on its palmar side fits between the two **proximal sesamoid bones**, which lie in the palmar part of the joint capsule.

## Clinical relevance

- **Metacarpal fractures**. They usually follow trauma (being stepped on, falls, car accidents) and often involve several bones. Fractures of the weight-bearing III and IV need stabilisation more often than those of II and V.
- **Racing and working dogs**. Stress fractures and luxations of the metacarpophalangeal joints are seen in them.
