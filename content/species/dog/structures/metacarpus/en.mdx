## Overview

The metacarpus has five metacarpal bones (*ossa metacarpalia I–V*), numbered from medial to lateral. Each has a proximal **base**, a **body** and a distal **head**. Metacarpal I is short and does not reach the ground; II to V lie side by side, III and IV being the longest and the main weight-bearing bones, II and V slightly shorter.

## Key features

- **Base (*basis*)**: articulates with the distal row of carpal bones and with the neighbouring metacarpals.
- **Body (*corpus*)**: slender and slightly curved, convex dorsally.
- **Head (*caput*)**: articulates with the proximal phalanx at the metacarpophalangeal joint. A sagittal ridge on its palmar side fits between the two **proximal sesamoid bones**, which lie in the palmar part of the joint capsule.

## Clinical relevance

- **Metacarpal fractures** usually follow trauma (being stepped on, falls, car accidents) and often involve several bones; fractures of the weight-bearing III and IV need stabilisation more often than those of II and V.
- Stress fractures and luxations of the metacarpophalangeal joints are seen in racing and working dogs.
