## Overview

The radius is the craniomedial bone of the forearm and carries most of the weight of the thoracic limb.

It is shorter than the ulna and crosses slightly cranial to it. The two bones are joined by the interosseous ligament across a narrow interosseous space.

In the dog, radius and ulna articulate at both ends and allow limited pronation and supination.

## Key features

- **Head** (*caput radii*). The proximal end. Its fovea articulates with the capitulum of the humerus and its articular circumference with the radial notch of the ulna.
- **Radial tuberosity**. Medial; the biceps brachii and brachialis insert here.
- **Shaft**. Flattened craniocaudally and slightly curved.
- **Trochlea of the radius** (*trochlea radii*). The distal end, a concave articular surface for the radial carpal bone. It shows the ulnar notch laterally and the styloid process medially.

## Clinical relevance

- **Osteosarcoma**. The distal radius is one of its most common sites in large-breed dogs.
- **Radius and ulna fractures**. Among the most frequent in toy breeds, after falls from the owner's arms. They carry a risk of non-union.
- **Premature physeal closure**. Premature closure of the distal radial or ulnar physis causes angular limb deformities of the forearm and elbow incongruity.
