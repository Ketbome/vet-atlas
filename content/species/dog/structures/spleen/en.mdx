## Topography

The gastrosplenic ligament attaches it to the greater curvature of the stomach.

- **Head**. The dorsal end. It lies under the last left ribs, between the gastric fundus and the cranial pole of the left kidney.
- **Body**. Runs down the left flank.
- **Ventral end**. Its position varies. It can reach the abdominal floor and even cross to the right.

Its size changes greatly with its blood content.

## Clinical relevance

- **Splenic masses**. Haemangiosarcoma, in breeds such as the German Shepherd Dog and the Golden Retriever, is a common cause of haemoabdomen. Haematoma and nodular hyperplasia are benign but look similar on ultrasound.
- **Anaesthetic splenomegaly**. Barbiturates and acepromazine congest the spleen.
- **Splenic torsion**. Occurs in large deep-chested breeds, alone or with gastric dilatation-volvulus.
