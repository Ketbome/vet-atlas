## Overview

The skin (*cutis*) covers the whole body surface. At the natural openings it is continuous with the mucous membranes: lips, nostrils, eyelids, anus and external genitalia.

It is the largest organ of the dog, about 12% of body weight in the adult.

Its functions are:

- physical, chemical and microbiological barrier
- thermoregulation
- perception of touch, temperature, pain and itch
- synthesis of vitamin D
- storage of water, electrolytes and fat

Together with the hair, skin glands, pads and claws it forms the common integument (*integumentum commune*).

## Layers

### Epidermis

Keratinised stratified squamous epithelium without blood vessels.

From deep to superficial it has the basal, spinous, granular, clear and cornified layers. The clear layer exists only in the nasal planum and pads.

Besides keratinocytes it contains melanocytes, Langerhans cells and Merkel cells.

In the haired skin of the dog it is thin. It becomes much thicker where the skin is hairless and bears friction.

### Dermis

The dermis (*dermis* or *corium*) is connective tissue of collagen and elastic fibres that gives strength and elasticity.

It holds the vessels and nerves of the skin, the hair follicles, the arrector pili muscles and the sebaceous and sweat glands.

### Hypodermis

The hypodermis (*tela subcutanea*) is loose connective tissue with a variable amount of fat. It binds the skin to the fascia and muscles and lets it move.

It is loosest over the dorsal neck and the lateral thoracic wall.

## Hair and glands

- **Hair follicles**. In the dog they are compound: one primary, or guard, hair and several secondary, or wool, hairs leave through the same opening. Hair grows in cycles of anagen, catagen and telogen.
- **Tactile hairs** (*pili tactiles*). Long whiskers with a venous sinus around the follicle and a rich nerve supply. They are on the lips, chin, cheeks and above the eyes.
- **Sebaceous glands**. Open into the follicles. Their secretion lubricates the hair and is part of the surface film of the skin.
- **Sweat glands**. Apocrine, or epitrichial, glands open into the follicles all over the haired skin. Eccrine, or atrichial, glands are found only in the pads. That is why dogs sweat little and lose heat mainly by panting.
- **Specialised glands**. The circumanal glands, the tail gland, dorsal at the base of the tail, and the mammary glands. The mammary glands are modified skin glands, usually five pairs.

## Specialised regions

- **Nasal planum** (*planum nasale*). Hairless, heavily keratinised skin around the nostrils. Its surface forms a groove pattern unique to each dog. It has no glands; its moisture comes from nasal secretion and licking.
- **Pads** (*tori*). Thickened skin with a papillated epidermis. Their hypodermis, rich in fat and elastic fibres, cushions weight bearing. There are digital pads (*tori digitales*), one metacarpal or metatarsal pad (*torus metacarpeus/metatarseus*) and, on the thoracic limb only, a carpal pad (*torus carpeus*) that does not touch the ground at a walk.
- **Other regions**. The skin of the ear pinna is thin and attached to the cartilage. Scrotal skin is thin and sparsely haired. The skin of the dorsal neck and trunk is thick and mobile.

## Clinical relevance

### Dermatology

Itching and skin lesions are among the most common reasons for consultation. Frequent causes are:

- atopic dermatitis
- flea allergy dermatitis
- bacterial pyoderma
- demodicosis
- dermatophytosis
- otitis externa

The basic tests are skin scraping, cytology, trichogram, culture and biopsy.

**Mast cell tumour** is the most common malignant skin tumour of the dog.

### Hydration

A skin fold that is slow to return suggests dehydration. Age and body condition affect the result.

### Routes of administration

- **Subcutaneous**. Into the loose skin of the dorsal neck or the lateral thoracic wall.
- **Intradermal**. Allergy tests are done on the clipped lateral thoracic wall.
- **Intramuscular**. Into muscles such as the quadriceps femoris or the lumbar epaxial muscles. On the caudal thigh the sciatic nerve must be avoided.
