## Overview

The canine sternum has eight segments, the **sternebrae** (*sternebrae*), joined by intersternebral cartilages:

- the manubrium, described on its own
- six cylindrical sternebrae
- the xiphoid process

It forms the floor of the thorax and rises cranially towards the thoracic inlet. The pectoral muscles arise from it.

## Key features

- **Sternebrae 2 to 7**. Short, cylindrical bones of similar size, slightly compressed laterally.
- **Intersternebral cartilages**. They join consecutive sternebrae. They ossify in old dogs.
- **Costal notches** (*incisurae costales*). Articular surfaces at the intersternebral junctions. The first costal cartilage articulates with the manubrium and the following ones at the junctions between sternebrae. The eighth and ninth articulate together at the last junction.
- **Xiphoid process** (*processus xiphoideus*). The last sternebra, flattened dorsoventrally. It is continued by the thin **xiphoid cartilage** (*cartilago xiphoidea*), to which the linea alba attaches.

## Clinical relevance

- **Pectus excavatum**. Dorsal deviation of the caudal sternum and costal cartilages that narrows the thorax. It is seen in puppies of brachycephalic breeds and may compromise breathing.
- **Median sternotomy**. Gives surgical access to the whole thorax.
- **Lateral thoracic radiographs**. The sternebrae and their junctions should not be mistaken for lesions. Calcified intersternebral cartilages are a normal finding in older dogs.
