## Overview

Cattle have **six lumbar vertebrae**. They form the skeleton of the loin between the last rib and the sacrum. Their bodies are longer than those of the thoracic vertebrae.

They support the epaxial and sublumbar muscles and transmit the thrust of the pelvic limbs to the trunk.

## Key features

- **Transverse processes** (*processus transversi*). Very long, plate-like and horizontal, longest in the middle of the series. Their tips are palpable under the skin and form the dorsal boundary of the **paralumbar fossa**, the flank, which is bounded cranially by the last rib. The sublumbar muscles attach to their ventral surface.
- **Spinous processes**. Broad and sturdy. Their dorsal border is palpable on the midline of the loin.
- **Articular processes**. The concave cranial ones embrace the convex caudal ones of the vertebra in front. This interlocking limits rotation and lateral bending. The cranial ones carry **mamillary processes**.
- **Lumbosacral space**. Between the arch of L6 and the sacrum. It is palpable as a midline depression caudal to the spinous process of L6.

## Clinical relevance

### Body condition

The tips of the transverse processes and the spinous processes are palpated or inspected to score body condition. They are prominent in a thin cow and covered with fat in a fat one.

### Paravertebral anaesthesia

It anaesthetises the flank for standing laparotomy: caesarean section, abomasal surgery or rumenotomy. It blocks the T13, L1 and L2 nerves.

- **Proximal technique**. Blocks the nerves as they leave the intervertebral foramina. The landmark is the cranial border of the transverse processes of L1, L2 and L3.
- **Distal technique**. Blocks them at the tips of the transverse processes of L1, L2 and L4.

### Lumbosacral space

Puncture site for collecting cerebrospinal fluid in the standing animal and for lumbosacral epidural anaesthesia.

### Other diseases

- **Vertebral body abscesses**, in calves.
- **Ankylosing spondylosis**, in old bulls. The fused vertebrae can fracture, for example during mounting.
