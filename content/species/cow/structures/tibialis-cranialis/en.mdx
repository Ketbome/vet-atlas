## Overview

It lies directly on the cranial surface of the tibia, partly covered by the long digital extensor and the peroneus tertius.

Its tendon crosses the dorsal surface of the tarsus towards the medial side.

## Origin

Proximal end of the tibia: lateral condyle and region of the tuberosity.

## Insertion

Medial side of the distal tarsus and proximal end of the metatarsus.

## Action

Flexes the tarsus.

## Innervation

Common fibular or peroneal nerve (*n. fibularis communis*), through the deep fibular nerve.

## Clinical relevance

**Peroneal nerve paralysis** is common in cattle. The nerve runs superficially over the lateral condyle of the tibia and is compressed during prolonged recumbency or after calving.

Without the tibialis cranialis and the digital extensors, the hock is overextended. The animal stands on the dorsal surface of the hoof, with the fetlock knuckled forward.
