---
title: Biceps femoris
summary: Broad muscle of the lateral thigh; in cattle it is fused with the gluteus superficialis to form the gluteobiceps.
---

## Overview

It covers most of the lateral surface of the thigh. In ruminants it is **fused with the gluteus superficialis**: the two form a single muscle, the **gluteobiceps** (*m. gluteobiceps*), which spreads from the sacrum and the ischiatic tuberosity to the stifle and the leg. Distally it divides into a **cranial part** and a **caudal part**, shown separately in the model.

In the carcass it is one of the hindquarter muscles that make up the outside round (silverside).

## Origin

- Vertebral head: sacrum and broad sacrotuberous ligament, with the part corresponding to the gluteus superficialis.
- Pelvic head: ischiatic tuberosity (*tuber ischiadicum*).

## Insertion

Through a broad aponeurosis continuous with the fascia lata and crural fascia:

- Cranial part: patella and lateral patellar ligament.
- Caudal part: tibial crest and crural fascia. It also gives off a tarsal tendon to the calcaneal tuber, which is part of the common calcanean tendon.

## Action

Extends the hip. The cranial part extends the stifle and the caudal part flexes it; through the tarsal tendon it helps extend the tarsus.

## Innervation

Caudal gluteal nerve (*n. gluteus caudalis*) for the vertebral head, and branches of the sciatic nerve (*n. ischiadicus*).

## Clinical relevance

- The **sciatic nerve** runs deep to this muscle. Intramuscular injections into the gluteal region or caudal thigh of calves can injure it.
- Beef Quality Assurance guidelines call for **no injections in the hindquarter**, because injection-site lesions damage the most valuable cuts; injections go in the neck.
- In downer cows, prolonged recumbency compresses the large muscle masses of the pelvic limb and causes **ischaemic myopathy**, which worsens downer cow syndrome.
- **Blackleg** (*Clostridium chauvoei*) often affects the large hindquarter muscles of young cattle.
