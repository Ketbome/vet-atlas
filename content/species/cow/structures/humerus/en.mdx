## Overview

The humerus (*humerus*) forms the skeleton of the arm. It runs obliquely in a caudoventral direction, from the shoulder to the elbow, and is almost entirely covered by muscles.

In cattle it is relatively short and sturdy. The greater tubercle forms the **point of the shoulder**, which is palpable in the live animal.

## Proximal end

- **Head** (*caput humeri*). Caudal. It articulates with the glenoid cavity of the scapula.
- **Greater tubercle** (*tuberculum majus*). Lateral and very large. It is divided into a **cranial part**, which rises above the head, and a **caudal part**. The supraspinatus and infraspinatus insert on it.
- **Lesser tubercle** (*tuberculum minus*). Medial. The subscapularis inserts on it.
- **Intertubercular groove** (*sulcus intertubercularis*). A single groove between the two tubercles, through which the tendon of the biceps brachii runs. Cattle lack the intermediate tubercle of the horse.

## Shaft

- **Deltoid tuberosity** (*tuberositas deltoidea*). On the lateral surface. The deltoideus inserts on it.
- **Teres major tuberosity**. On the medial surface. The teres major and latissimus dorsi insert on it.
- **Brachial groove**. Spiral groove on the lateral surface that holds the brachialis muscle and the radial nerve.

## Distal end

- **Humeral condyle** (*condylus humeri*). Articular surface for the radius and ulna at the elbow.
- **Medial and lateral epicondyles**. Origin of the flexors and extensors of the carpus and digits.
- **Olecranon fossa**. Deep, on the caudal surface. It receives the anconeal process of the ulna when the elbow extends. There is no supratrochlear foramen, a feature of the dog.

## Clinical relevance

- **Fractures** are uncommon and mainly affect young animals after trauma. The large muscle mass makes it impossible to immobilise the humerus with a cast.
- The radial nerve can be injured in a humeral fracture or by compression during prolonged lateral recumbency, as in downer cows or during surgery. In **radial nerve paralysis** the animal cannot extend the elbow, carpus or digits. It stands with a dropped elbow and drags the dorsal surface of the hoof.
