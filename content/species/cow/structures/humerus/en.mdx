## Overview

The humerus (*humerus*) forms the skeleton of the arm. It runs obliquely in a caudoventral direction from the shoulder to the elbow and is almost entirely covered by muscles. In cattle it is relatively short and sturdy. The greater tubercle forms the **point of the shoulder**, palpable in the live animal.

## Proximal end

- **Head (*caput humeri*)**: caudal, articulates with the glenoid cavity of the scapula.
- **Greater tubercle (*tuberculum majus*)**: lateral and very large; it is divided into a **cranial part**, which rises above the head, and a **caudal part**. Insertion of the supraspinatus and infraspinatus muscles.
- **Lesser tubercle (*tuberculum minus*)**: medial; insertion of the subscapularis.
- **Intertubercular groove (*sulcus intertubercularis*)**: a single groove between the two tubercles through which the tendon of the biceps brachii runs. Cattle lack the intermediate tubercle of the horse.

## Shaft

- **Deltoid tuberosity (*tuberositas deltoidea*)**: on the lateral surface; insertion of the deltoideus.
- **Teres major tuberosity**: on the medial surface; insertion of the teres major and latissimus dorsi muscles.
- **Brachial groove**: spiral groove on the lateral surface that holds the brachialis muscle and the radial nerve.

## Distal end

- **Humeral condyle (*condylus humeri*)**: articular surface for the radius and ulna at the elbow joint.
- **Medial and lateral epicondyles**: origin of the flexor and extensor muscles of the carpus and digits.
- **Olecranon fossa**: deep, on the caudal surface; receives the anconeal process of the ulna when the elbow extends. There is no supratrochlear foramen, a feature of the dog.

## Clinical relevance

- **Fractures**: uncommon; they mainly affect young animals after trauma. The large muscle mass makes it impossible to immobilise the humerus with a cast.
- **Radial nerve paralysis**: the nerve can be injured in humeral fractures or by compression during prolonged lateral recumbency (downer cows, surgery); the animal cannot extend the elbow, carpus or digits, stands with a dropped elbow and drags the dorsal surface of the hoof.
