## Overview

It fills the angle between the caudal border of the scapula and the humerus.

In cattle it has three heads ending on the olecranon: long, lateral and medial. The **long head** is the bulkiest.

## Origin

- **Long head** (*caput longum*). Caudal border of the scapula. The only head that crosses the shoulder.
- **Lateral head** (*caput laterale*). Tricipital line on the lateral surface of the proximal humerus.
- **Medial head** (*caput mediale*). Medial surface of the humerus.

## Insertion

Olecranon tuber (*tuber olecrani*) of the ulna.

## Action

Extends the elbow and supports body weight when standing. The long head also flexes the shoulder.

## Innervation

Radial nerve (*n. radialis*).

## Clinical relevance

**Radial nerve paralysis** results from humeral fractures or from compression during prolonged lateral recumbency.

The triceps is inactive: the elbow drops, the animal cannot bear weight on the limb and knuckles over onto the dorsal surface of the hoof.
