## Overview

The cow's uterus (*uterus*) is suspended from the roof of the pelvis and abdomen by the **broad ligament** (*mesometrium*).

In the heifer and the non-pregnant cow it lies largely within the pelvic cavity or at the pelvic inlet. During pregnancy it sinks into the abdomen, mainly on the right, because the rumen fills the left.

## Parts

- **Horns** (*cornua uteri*). Long. Caudally they run together, joined by the **intercornual ligaments**. They then diverge and curve in a spiral, first cranially and ventrally and then caudally and dorsally, like a ram's horns.
- **Body** (*corpus uteri*). Short. The external union of the horns makes it look longer than it is.
- **Caruncles** (*carunculae*). Rounded, non-glandular raised areas of the mucosa arranged in rows. In pregnancy they join the cotyledons of the chorion to form the **placentomes** of the cotyledonary placenta.
- **Cervix** (*cervix uteri*). Long, thick-walled and firm. Its **annular folds** close the canal and make it hard to pass. Its firmness makes it the main landmark of the genital tract on rectal palpation.

## Clinical relevance

### Rectal palpation and ultrasound

The cervix is located first and, from it, the body and horns. Pregnancy diagnosis relies on:

- horn asymmetry
- the slip of the fetal membranes
- the amniotic vesicle
- the placentomes
- the fetus
- the fremitus of the uterine artery

Transrectal ultrasound allows an earlier diagnosis.

### Artificial insemination

The catheter is guided through the cervical folds with one hand in the rectum.

### Disorders

- **Puerperal metritis**. Uterine infection in the days after calving, often following retained fetal membranes. It causes a fetid discharge and can affect the general condition.
- **Endometritis and pyometra**. Pyometra comes with a persistent corpus luteum and no signs of oestrus.
- **Uterine prolapse**. The uterus everts through the vulva shortly after calving, often with hypocalcaemia. It is an emergency.
- **Uterine torsion**. At the end of pregnancy or during calving, the uterus rotates on its long axis. It is recognised by spiral folds in the vagina.
- **Freemartinism**. The female twin of a male is usually sterile, with an underdeveloped genital tract, because of the exchange of cells and hormones between the two fetuses.
