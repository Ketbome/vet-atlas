## Overview

It is convex cranially. Its periphery is muscular and its centre is a tendon (*centrum tendineum*).

It has three openings:

- the aortic hiatus
- the esophageal hiatus
- the caval foramen

## Arrangement in cattle

The line of attachment is very oblique. On the ventral midline it attaches far cranially, near the xiphoid process. From there it climbs caudodorsally along the costal arch to the last rib.

As a result the dome reaches far cranially and much of the abdomen lies under cover of the ribs.

The **reticulum** lies directly against the abdominal surface of the ventral diaphragm, with the heart and pericardium on the other side. The liver lies against its right part.

## Origin

- Lumbar part: right and left crura, on the bodies of the cranial lumbar vertebrae.
- Costal part: medial surfaces of the caudal ribs and their cartilages, along the costal arch.
- Sternal part: xiphoid process.

## Insertion

Central tendon.

## Action

Contraction flattens the dome, enlarges the thorax and produces inspiration.

## Innervation

Phrenic nerve (*n. phrenicus*), formed by ventral branches of the caudal cervical nerves.

## Clinical relevance

### Traumatic reticuloperitonitis

An ingested metallic foreign body, such as a wire or a nail, perforates the reticular wall. Because the diaphragm is so close, it can pass through it, reach the pericardium and cause **traumatic pericarditis**.

Oral magnets, which stay in the reticulum, are used to prevent it.

### Diaphragmatic hernia

In cattle it is associated with traumatic reticuloperitonitis. The reticulum passes into the thorax through a defect in the diaphragm and can cause recurrent bloat and indigestion.
