## Overview

The epaxial muscles form three parallel columns, from lateral to medial:

- **Iliocostalis** (*m. iliocostalis*). From the ilium and lumbar vertebrae to the ribs.
- **Longissimus** (*m. longissimus*). The largest, from the ilium to the skull, between the transverse and spinous processes.
- **Transversospinalis** (*m. transversospinalis*). Short bundles next to the spinous processes: spinalis, semispinalis, multifidus and rotatores.

The model shows the three columns as a single mass, from the sacrum to the axis.

Cattle have a well developed **nuchal ligament**. It passively carries the weight of the head and relieves the dorsal neck muscles.

## Meat science

The longissimus thoracis et lumborum, the "longissimus dorsi", is the most valuable cut of the carcass:

- it forms the **rib eye** in the thoracic region
- it forms the **striploin** in the lumbar region

## Origin and insertion

Ilium, sacrum, vertebral processes and ribs. Each bundle spans one or several segments.

## Action

- Extend and stabilise the vertebral column.
- Acting on one side only, they bend it laterally.
- Raise the head and neck.

## Innervation

Dorsal branches of the spinal nerves.

## Clinical relevance

- In **paravertebral anaesthesia** of the flank, spinal nerves T13, L1 and L2 are blocked next to the lumbar transverse processes. The needle passes through these muscles.
- The **inverted-L block** infiltrates along the caudal border of the last rib and ventral to the lumbar transverse processes.
- Rib eye area, measured at the cut between ribs 12 and 13, is used in carcass grading to estimate lean meat yield.
- **Blackleg** (*Clostridium chauvoei*) can affect the large muscle masses of the back and loin in young cattle, as well as those of the hindquarter and shoulder.
