## Overview

The bovine brain (*encephalon*) fills a relatively small cranial cavity, protected by the frontal bone and, in the adult, by the **frontal sinus**, which surrounds it dorsally and caudally. It continues into the spinal cord through the foramen magnum.

## Parts

- **Cerebrum (*cerebrum*)**: two hemispheres with gyri, separated by the longitudinal fissure. The cortex integrates vision, behaviour and voluntary movement.
- **Cerebellum (*cerebellum*)**: caudal to the cerebrum, over the fourth ventricle; it coordinates movement and balance.
- **Brainstem**: midbrain, pons and medulla oblongata, from which most cranial nerves emerge.
- **Blood supply**: in the adult bovine, arterial blood reaches the brain mainly through the **rostral epidural rete mirabile** (*rete mirabile epidurale rostrale*) at the base of the skull.

## Clinical relevance

- **Polioencephalomalacia**: necrosis of the cerebral cortex associated with thiamine deficiency or excess dietary sulphur. It causes cortical blindness, head pressing, opisthotonus and seizures; in the early stages it responds to thiamine.
- **Listeriosis**: *Listeria monocytogenes*, often from poorly fermented silage, forms microabscesses in the brainstem. It causes asymmetric cranial nerve deficits (facial paralysis, head tilt) and circling.
- **Bovine spongiform encephalopathy**: a prion disease spread by meat and bone meal; it causes vacuolation in the brainstem, and the obex is the reference sample for diagnosis. It is now rare thanks to the ban on feeding those meals to ruminants.
- **Rabies**: must be considered in any bovine with rapidly progressive neurological signs; necropsy and sample handling require precautions.
- **Stunning and euthanasia**: the captive bolt is aimed at the crossing of the lines running from the lateral canthus of each eye to the base of the opposite horn.
