## Overview

The udder (*mamma*, *uber*) is a modified skin gland in the inguinal region. It is made of **four quarters**, each with its own glandular tissue, duct system and **one teat**; milk from one quarter does not pass to the others. In a heifer, like the one in the model, the udder is small: the secretory tissue develops during pregnancy and reaches its working size with lactation.

## Suspension

- **Median suspensory ligament**: double, elastic sheet that separates the left and right halves and supports the udder from the ventral abdominal wall.
- **Lateral suspensory laminae**: more fibrous, they wrap each half from the pelvic region. Failure of the suspensory apparatus lets the udder drop and the teats splay apart.

## Teat and cisterns

- **Teat canal (*ductus papillaris*)**: short canal at the tip of the teat, lined with **keratin** that forms a plug between milkings, and closed by the **teat sphincter**. It is the main barrier against bacteria.
- **Fürstenberg's rosette**: mucosal folds where the teat canal joins the cistern.
- **Teat cistern** and **gland cistern**: two parts of the lactiferous sinus that store milk, separated by an annular fold at the base of the teat.
- **Supernumerary teats**: common, usually caudal to the main ones; they are often removed in heifer calves.

## Vessels

The main blood supply arrives through the **external pudendal artery**, which passes through the inguinal canal. Venous blood leaves through the external pudendal vein and the **subcutaneous abdominal vein** ("milk vein"), which runs under the skin of the abdomen and enters the thorax. The **mammary lymph nodes** (superficial inguinal) lie at the caudal base of the udder.

## Clinical relevance

- **Mastitis**: inflammation of the gland, almost always bacterial; it is the most costly disease of dairy cattle. **Clinical mastitis** changes the milk (clots, colour) or the quarter (heat, pain, swelling); **subclinical mastitis** shows no visible signs and is detected by a raised **somatic cell count**.
- **California Mastitis Test**: quick cow-side test that estimates the somatic cells of each quarter by the formation of a gel.
- **Agents**: contagious, spread during milking (*Staphylococcus aureus*, *Streptococcus agalactiae*, *Mycoplasma*), and environmental (*Escherichia coli*, *Streptococcus uberis*).
- **Teat injuries**: wounds from treading or wire, teat-end hyperkeratosis from machine milking and obstructions; all of them favour mastitis.
