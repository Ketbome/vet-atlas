## Overview

The udder (*mamma*, *uber*) is a modified skin gland in the inguinal region.

It is made of **four quarters**. Each has its own glandular tissue, duct system and teat. Milk from one quarter does not pass to the others.

In a heifer, like the one in the model, the udder is small. The secretory tissue develops during pregnancy and reaches its working size with lactation.

## Suspension

- **Median suspensory ligament**. Double, elastic sheet that separates the left and right halves and supports the udder from the ventral abdominal wall.
- **Lateral suspensory laminae**. More fibrous, they wrap each half from the pelvic region.

If the suspensory apparatus fails, the udder drops and the teats splay apart.

## Teat and cisterns

- **Teat canal** (*ductus papillaris*). Short canal at the tip of the teat, closed by the **teat sphincter**. It is lined with keratin, which forms a plug between milkings. It is the **main barrier** against bacteria.
- **Fürstenberg's rosette**. Mucosal folds where the teat canal joins the cistern.
- **Teat cistern and gland cistern**. Two parts of the lactiferous sinus that store milk, separated by an annular fold at the base of the teat.
- **Supernumerary teats**. Common, usually caudal to the main ones. They are often removed in heifer calves.

## Vessels

The main blood supply arrives through the **external pudendal artery**, which passes through the inguinal canal.

Venous blood leaves through the external pudendal vein and the **subcutaneous abdominal vein**, or milk vein, which runs under the skin of the abdomen and enters the thorax.

The **mammary lymph nodes**, or superficial inguinal nodes, lie at the caudal base of the udder.

## Clinical relevance

### Mastitis

Inflammation of the gland, almost always bacterial. It is the most costly disease of dairy cattle.

- **Clinical mastitis** changes the milk, with clots or colour changes, or the quarter, with heat, pain and swelling.
- **Subclinical mastitis** shows no visible signs and is detected by a raised **somatic cell count**.
- The **California Mastitis Test** is a quick cow-side test. It estimates the somatic cells of each quarter by the formation of a gel.

The agents can be:

- contagious, spread during milking: *Staphylococcus aureus*, *Streptococcus agalactiae*, *Mycoplasma*
- environmental: *Escherichia coli*, *Streptococcus uberis*

### Teat injuries

Wounds from treading or wire, teat-end hyperkeratosis from machine milking, and obstructions. All of them favour mastitis.
