## Overview

Cattle have seven cervical vertebrae. The atlas (C1) and the axis (C2) are very different. The third to the seventh share a similar plan and are described here together.

They form a neck that is shorter and more muscular than the horse's and carries a heavy head.

## Key features

- **Body** (*corpus vertebrae*). It has a convex cranial end, the head, and a concave caudal end, the fossa. Its ventral surface carries a **ventral crest**. The intervertebral discs lie between the bodies.
- **Arch and vertebral canal**. Wide. In the caudal neck they house the cervical intumescence of the spinal cord, which gives rise to the nerves of the brachial plexus.
- **Transverse processes and transverse foramen**. From C3 to C6 the base of the transverse process is pierced by the **transverse foramen**. The row of foramina forms the canal for the vertebral artery and vein. On C6 the ventral part of the process forms a wide plate, the **ventral lamina**.
- **Spinous processes**. They grow taller from C3 to C7.
- **Articular processes**. Large, with wide surfaces that allow lateral and dorsoventral movement of the neck.
- **Seventh cervical vertebra** (C7). Transitional towards the thorax. It has no transverse foramen, its spinous process is the tallest of the series and the caudal border of its body carries a costal fovea for the head of the first rib.

## Clinical relevance

- **Intramuscular and subcutaneous injections**. In cattle they are recommended in the side of the neck, within a triangle bounded dorsally by the nuchal ligament, ventrally by the cervical vertebrae and caudally by the scapula. This avoids damage to the valuable cuts of meat.
- **Vertebral osteomyelitis**. In calves, vertebral body abscesses from blood-borne bacterial spread can compress the spinal cord. They cause neck pain, ataxia and paresis.
- **Fractures and luxations**. Uncommon; they follow severe trauma.
