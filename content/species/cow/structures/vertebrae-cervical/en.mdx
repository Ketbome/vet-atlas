summary: The five caudal vertebrae of the neck. Bodies with a convex cranial end and a concave caudal end, transverse processes pierced by the transverse foramen (C3 to C6) and spinous processes that grow taller towards C7.

## Overview

Cattle have seven cervical vertebrae. The atlas (C1) and the axis (C2) are very different; the third to the seventh share a similar plan and are described here together. They form a neck that is shorter and more muscular than the horse's and carries a heavy head.

## Key features

- **Body (*corpus vertebrae*)**: with a convex cranial end (head) and a concave caudal end (fossa); its ventral surface carries a **ventral crest**. The intervertebral discs lie between the bodies.
- **Arch and vertebral canal**: wide; in the caudal neck they house the cervical intumescence of the spinal cord, which gives rise to the nerves of the brachial plexus.
- **Transverse processes and transverse foramen**: from C3 to C6 the base of the transverse process is pierced by the **transverse foramen**; the row of foramina forms the canal for the vertebral artery and vein. On C6 the ventral part of the process forms a wide plate, the **ventral lamina**.
- **Spinous processes**: grow taller from C3 to C7.
- **Articular processes**: large, with wide surfaces that allow lateral and dorsoventral movement of the neck.
- **Seventh cervical (C7)**: transitional towards the thorax. It has no transverse foramen, its spinous process is the tallest of the series and the caudal border of its body carries a costal fovea for the head of the first rib.

## Clinical relevance

- **Intramuscular and subcutaneous injections**: in cattle they are recommended in the side of the neck, within the triangle bounded dorsally by the nuchal ligament, ventrally by the cervical vertebrae and caudally by the scapula; this avoids damage to the valuable cuts of meat.
- **Vertebral osteomyelitis**: in calves, vertebral body abscesses from blood-borne bacterial spread can compress the spinal cord, causing neck pain, ataxia and paresis.
- **Fractures and luxations**: uncommon; they follow severe trauma.
