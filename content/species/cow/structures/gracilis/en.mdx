---
title: Gracilis
summary: Broad, flat muscle of the medial thigh; adductor of the pelvic limb.
---

## Overview

It covers most of the medial surface of the thigh, caudal to the sartorius. The gracilis muscles of both sides arise together on the pelvic symphysis, through the symphyseal tendon.

## Origin

Pelvic symphysis, through the symphyseal tendon.

## Insertion

Medial surface of the proximal tibia, through an aponeurosis continuous with the crural fascia.

## Action

Adducts the pelvic limb, extends the hip and flexes the stifle.

## Innervation

Obturator nerve (*n. obturatorius*).

## Clinical relevance

**Obturator nerve paralysis** occurs mainly after a difficult calving with a large calf, from compression of the nerve against the pelvis. The adductors, the gracilis among them, lose their function: the cow cannot keep its pelvic limbs together and, on slippery floors, splays them sideways and falls.
