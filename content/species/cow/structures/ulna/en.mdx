## Overview

The ulna is the caudolateral bone of the forearm. In cattle it is **complete**, from the elbow to the carpus, unlike in the horse, where its distal part disappears.

Only the proximal end is thick. The shaft is **thin** and joined to the radius along almost its whole length. In the adult it fuses with it.

Only two openings remain between the bones, the **proximal and distal interosseous spaces**.

The ulna bears little weight. Its main role is to act as a lever for the extensors of the elbow.

## Proximal end

- **Olecranon** (*olecranon*). Large, projecting caudally behind the elbow. Its free end, the **olecranon tuber** (*tuber olecrani*), forms the point of the elbow and receives the triceps brachii.
- **Anconeal process** (*processus anconeus*). The proximal edge of the trochlear notch. It fits into the olecranon fossa of the humerus when the elbow extends.
- **Trochlear notch** (*incisura trochlearis*). Concave articular surface that grips the humeral condyle together with the head of the radius.

## Shaft and distal end

- **Shaft**. Slender and triangular in section, applied to the caudolateral surface of the radius. With it, it bounds the interosseous spaces.
- **Lateral styloid process** (*processus styloideus lateralis*). The distal end of the ulna, fused to the radius. It forms the lateral prominence of the distal forearm and articulates with the ulnar carpal bone.

## Clinical relevance

- **Olecranon fractures**. The triceps pulls the proximal fragment away and the animal cannot extend the elbow. It stands with a **dropped elbow**, a sign also seen in radial nerve paralysis.
- **Forearm fractures**. Because the ulna is joined to the radius, fractures of its shaft usually accompany radial fractures.
- **Examination**. The olecranon tuber is palpable and is the landmark for locating the elbow.
