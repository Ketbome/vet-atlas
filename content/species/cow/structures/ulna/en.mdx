## Overview

The ulna is the caudolateral bone of the forearm. In cattle it is **complete**, from the elbow to the carpus, unlike in the horse, where its distal part disappears. Only the proximal end is thick, however: the shaft is **thin** and joined to the radius along almost its whole length, and in the adult it fuses with it. Only two openings remain between the bones, the **proximal and distal interosseous spaces**. The ulna bears little weight; its main role is to act as a lever for the extensors of the elbow.

## Proximal end

- **Olecranon**: large, projecting caudally behind the elbow. Its free end, the **olecranon tuber** (*tuber olecrani*), forms the point of the elbow and receives the triceps brachii.
- **Anconeal process (*processus anconeus*)**: the proximal edge of the trochlear notch; it fits into the olecranon fossa of the humerus when the elbow extends.
- **Trochlear notch (*incisura trochlearis*)**: concave articular surface that grips the humeral condyle together with the head of the radius.

## Shaft and distal end

- **Shaft**: slender and triangular in section, applied to the caudolateral surface of the radius; with it, it bounds the interosseous spaces.
- **Lateral styloid process (*processus styloideus lateralis*)**: the distal end of the ulna, fused to the radius, which forms the lateral prominence of the distal forearm and articulates with the ulnar carpal bone.

## Clinical relevance

- **Olecranon fractures**: the triceps pulls the proximal fragment away and the animal cannot extend the elbow; it stands with a "dropped" elbow, a sign also seen in radial nerve paralysis.
- **Forearm fractures**: because the ulna is joined to the radius, fractures of its shaft usually accompany radial fractures.
- **Examination**: the olecranon tuber is palpable and is the landmark for locating the elbow.
