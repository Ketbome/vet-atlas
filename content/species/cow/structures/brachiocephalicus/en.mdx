---
title: Brachiocephalicus
summary: Muscle joining the arm to the head and neck; in cattle, which have no clavicle, a clavicular intersection crosses it.
---

## Overview

Cattle have no clavicle. Instead, cranial to the shoulder joint, the muscle is crossed by a **clavicular intersection** (*intersectio clavicularis*), a tendinous sheet that divides it into two muscles:

- **Cleidobrachialis (*m. cleidobrachialis*)**: from the clavicular intersection to the arm.
- **Cleidocephalicus (*m. cleidocephalicus*)**: from the clavicular intersection to the head. In cattle it has two parts:
  - **Cleido-occipitalis (*pars occipitalis*)**: the dorsal part, towards the nape.
  - **Cleidomastoideus (*pars mastoidea*)**: the ventral part, towards the mastoid process.

## Origin and insertion

- The cleidobrachialis runs from the clavicular intersection to the crest of the humerus, distal to the deltoid tuberosity, and to the brachial fascia.
- The cleido-occipitalis runs from the clavicular intersection to the funicular part of the nuchal ligament and the occipital bone.
- The cleidomastoideus runs from the clavicular intersection to the mastoid process of the temporal bone.

## Action

The main muscle that advances the thoracic limb (shoulder extension). With the limb fixed, it draws the head and neck ventrally and laterally.

## Innervation

Accessory nerve and ventral branches of the cervical nerves for the cleidocephalicus; axillary nerve for the cleidobrachialis.

## Clinical relevance

- It forms the **dorsal border of the jugular groove**. The external jugular vein, in this groove, is the usual route for drawing blood and giving intravenous medication in cattle.
- Together with the omotransversarius it covers the **superficial cervical (prescapular) lymph nodes**, which are palpated cranial to the shoulder during the clinical examination.
- The side of the neck, cranial to the scapula, is the **recommended injection site** for intramuscular and subcutaneous injections in cattle. Beef Quality Assurance guidelines call for injecting here and not in the hindquarter, to avoid lesions in the most valuable cuts.
