## Overview

Cattle have no clavicle. Instead, cranial to the shoulder joint, the muscle is crossed by a **clavicular intersection** (*intersectio clavicularis*). This tendinous sheet divides it into two muscles:

- **Cleidobrachialis** (*m. cleidobrachialis*). Runs from the clavicular intersection to the arm.
- **Cleidocephalicus** (*m. cleidocephalicus*). Runs from the clavicular intersection to the head. In cattle it has two parts:
  - the cleido-occipitalis (*pars occipitalis*), dorsal, towards the nape
  - the cleidomastoideus (*pars mastoidea*), ventral, towards the mastoid process

## Origin and insertion

- The cleidobrachialis runs from the clavicular intersection to the crest of the humerus, distal to the deltoid tuberosity, and to the brachial fascia.
- The cleido-occipitalis runs from the clavicular intersection to the funicular part of the nuchal ligament and the occipital bone.
- The cleidomastoideus runs from the clavicular intersection to the mastoid process of the temporal bone.

## Action

The main muscle that advances the thoracic limb, by extending the shoulder.

With the limb fixed, it draws the head and neck ventrally and laterally.

## Innervation

The cleidocephalicus is supplied by the accessory nerve and ventral branches of the cervical nerves. The cleidobrachialis is supplied by the axillary nerve.

## Clinical relevance

- It forms the **dorsal border of the jugular groove**. The external jugular vein, in this groove, is the usual route for drawing blood and giving intravenous medication in cattle.
- Together with the omotransversarius it covers the superficial cervical, or prescapular, lymph nodes. They are palpated cranial to the shoulder during the clinical examination.
- The side of the neck, cranial to the scapula, is the **recommended injection site** for intramuscular and subcutaneous injections in cattle. Beef Quality Assurance guidelines call for injecting here and not in the hindquarter, to avoid lesions in the most valuable cuts.
