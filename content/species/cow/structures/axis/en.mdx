## Overview

The axis is the second cervical vertebra.

Its body is long and extends cranially into the **dens**, the pivot around which the atlas rotates.

It articulates cranially with the atlas and caudally with the third cervical vertebra.

## Features

- **Dens**. Cranial projection of the body that rests in the fovea of the dens of the atlas, held by ligaments. In cattle, as in the horse, it is **spout-shaped**, concave dorsally. In the dog and cat it is peg-shaped.
- **Cranial articular surfaces**. On each side of the dens, for the caudal foveae of the atlas.
- **Spinous process**. Long, shaped like a sagittal crest, thicker in its caudal part. Neck muscles such as the obliquus capitis caudalis originate on it.
- **Transverse processes**. Small, directed caudally.
- **Caudal articular processes and fossa of the body**. They articulate with the third cervical vertebra.

## Clinical relevance

- **Atlantoaxial joint**. It concentrates the rotation of the head. Fractures of the dens and atlantoaxial luxations are rare in cattle and follow severe trauma.
- **Vertebral osteomyelitis**. In calves, blood-borne spread of bacteria can form abscesses in the vertebral bodies, including the cervical ones. It causes neck pain, stiffness and ataxia.
- **Congenital malformations** of the occipitoatlantoaxial region. Rare; described in calves.
