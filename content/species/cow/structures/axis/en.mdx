## Overview

The axis is the second cervical vertebra. Its body is long and extends cranially into the **dens**, the pivot around which the atlas rotates. It articulates cranially with the atlas and caudally with the third cervical vertebra.

## Key features

- **Dens**: cranial projection of the body that rests in the fovea of the dens of the atlas, held by ligaments. In cattle, as in the horse, it is **spout-shaped**, concave dorsally, rather than peg-shaped as in the dog and cat.
- **Cranial articular surfaces**: on each side of the dens, for the caudal foveae of the atlas.
- **Spinous process**: long, shaped like a sagittal crest, thicker in its caudal part. Neck muscles such as the obliquus capitis caudalis originate on it.
- **Transverse processes**: small, directed caudally.
- **Caudal articular processes** and **fossa of the body**: articulate with the third cervical vertebra.

## Clinical relevance

- **Atlantoaxial joint**: concentrates the rotation of the head; fractures of the dens and atlantoaxial luxations are rare in cattle and follow severe trauma.
- **Vertebral osteomyelitis**: in calves, blood-borne spread of bacteria can form abscesses in the vertebral bodies, including the cervical ones, with neck pain, stiffness and ataxia.
- **Congenital malformations** of the occipitoatlantoaxial region: rare; described in calves.
