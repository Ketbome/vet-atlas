## Overview

The hip bone (*os coxae*) results from fusion of the **ilium**, **ischium** and **pubis**. The three meet at the **acetabulum**, the socket that receives the head of the femur.

The two hip bones join ventrally at the **pelvic symphysis** and articulate dorsally with the sacrum. With it and the first caudal vertebrae they form the **pelvis**.

In cattle the pelvis is large and angular, and its bony prominences are visible and palpable under the skin.

## Ilium and ischium

- **Tuber coxae**. The lateral angle of the iliac wing, large and prominent. It is the "hook bone" seen on each side of the loin.
- **Tuber sacrale**. The medial angle of the iliac wing, next to the midline, close to its opposite partner.
- **Ischial tuber** (*tuber ischiadicum*). The caudal end of the ischium, on either side of the tail head. In cattle it is triangular, with three prominences, and forms the "pin bone".
- **Ischial arch** (*arcus ischiadicus*). The caudal border between the two ischia.
- **Obturator foramen** (*foramen obturatum*). Large, between the pubis and the ischium.

## Pelvic cavity

- **Pelvic inlet**. **Oval** and oblique.
- **Pelvic floor**. Formed by the pubis and ischium. In the cow it is **concave** and **rises caudally** towards the ischial arch, so the calf must travel upwards during expulsion. In the mare the pelvic floor is flatter.
- **Lateral wall**. Closed by the **broad sacrotuberous ligament**, which relaxes in the days before calving.

## Clinical relevance

- The tuber coxae and the ischial tuber, and the line between them, are basic landmarks for **body condition** scoring in the cow.
- Disproportion between the size of the calf and the pelvic canal is a common cause of **dystocia**, especially in first-calf heifers. **Pelvimetry**, rectal measurement of the height and width of the pelvic inlet, is used as an aid to select heifers for breeding.
- **Fracture of the tuber coxae** follows knocks against doorways or narrow passages. It leaves the rump asymmetric, with the point on the affected side flattened.
- In **calving paralysis**, compression of the obturator and sciatic nerves inside the pelvis during difficult calvings can stop the cow from rising or from adducting the limbs.
