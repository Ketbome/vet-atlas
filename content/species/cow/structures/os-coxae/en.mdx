title: Hip bone
summary: Bone of the hip, formed by the fused ilium, ischium and pubis. Its coxal and ischial tubers are external landmarks, and its shape influences calving in the cow.
---

## Overview

The hip bone (*os coxae*) results from fusion of the **ilium**, **ischium** and **pubis**, which meet at the **acetabulum**, the socket that receives the head of the femur. The two hip bones join ventrally at the **pelvic symphysis** and articulate dorsally with the sacrum; with it and the first caudal vertebrae they form the **pelvis**. In cattle the pelvis is large and angular, and its bony prominences are visible and palpable under the skin.

## Ilium and ischium

- **Tuber coxae**: the lateral angle of the iliac wing, large and prominent; it is the "hook bone" seen on each side of the loin.
- **Tuber sacrale**: the medial angle of the iliac wing, next to the midline, close to its opposite partner.
- **Ischial tuber (*tuber ischiadicum*)**: the caudal end of the ischium, on either side of the tail head; in cattle it is triangular, with three prominences, and forms the "pin bone".
- **Ischial arch (*arcus ischiadicus*)**: the caudal border between the two ischia.
- **Obturator foramen (*foramen obturatum*)**: large, between the pubis and the ischium.

## Pelvic cavity

- **Pelvic inlet**: **oval** and oblique.
- **Pelvic floor**: formed by the pubis and ischium; in the cow it is **concave** and **rises caudally** towards the ischial arch, so the calf must travel upwards during expulsion, unlike in the mare, whose pelvic floor is flatter.
- **Lateral wall**: closed by the **broad sacrotuberous ligament**, which relaxes in the days before calving.

## Clinical relevance

- **Body condition**: the tuber coxae and the ischial tuber, and the line between them, are basic landmarks for body condition scoring in the cow.
- **Dystocia**: disproportion between the size of the calf and the pelvic canal is a common cause of difficult calving, especially in first-calf heifers. **Pelvimetry**, rectal measurement of the height and width of the pelvic inlet, is used as an aid to select heifers for breeding.
- **Fracture of the tuber coxae**: from knocks against doorways or narrow passages; it leaves the rump asymmetric, with the point on the affected side flattened.
- **Calving paralysis**: compression of the obturator and sciatic nerves inside the pelvis during difficult calvings can stop the cow from rising or from adducting the limbs.
