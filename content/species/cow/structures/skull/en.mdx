## Overview

The bovine skull is divided into the **braincase** (*neurocranium*), which protects the brain, and the **facial skeleton** (*viscerocranium*), which forms the nose, the orbits and the mouth. It is long, wide and heavy, with a braincase that is small relative to the size of the head. Its most typical feature is the huge **frontal bone** (*os frontale*), which covers the whole dorsal surface back to the poll and pushes the parietal bones onto the caudal surface of the skull.

## Frontal region and horns

- **Intercornual protuberance (*protuberantia intercornualis*)**: transverse ridge between the horns that forms the poll; it is the highest point of the skull.
- **Cornual process (*processus cornualis*)**: bony extension of the frontal bone that forms the **horn core**, covered by the corium and the horny sheath. Its shape and size vary with breed, sex and age; it is absent in polled breeds such as the Aberdeen Angus.
- **Frontal sinus (*sinus frontalis*)**: very large; in the adult it surrounds the cranial cavity dorsally and caudally and extends into the cornual process.
- **Temporal line (*linea temporalis*)**: ridge running from the orbit to the base of the horn that separates the frontal surface from the **temporal fossa**, which in cattle lies on the lateral side of the skull.

## Face and teeth

- **Orbits**: directed laterally, with a complete bony orbital rim.
- **Facial tuber (*tuber faciale*)**: prominence on the lateral surface of the maxilla, rostral to the orbit and dorsal to the cheek teeth; origin of the masseter muscle.
- **Incisive bone (*os incisivum*)**: has no alveoli. Instead of upper incisors there is a **dental pad** (*pulvinus dentalis*), a horny plate against which the lower incisors press the grass that the tongue wraps around and tears off.
- **Upper teeth**: no incisors or canines; three premolars (P2–P4) and three molars on each side. They are **selenodont**, with crescent-shaped enamel ridges on the occlusal surface. The upper arcades are set wider apart than the lower ones (**anisognathism**), so the animal chews on one side at a time.
- **Adult dental formula**: 2 × (I 0/3, C 0/1, P 3/3, M 3/3) = 32.
- **Temporomandibular joint**: allows the wide side-to-side movements of chewing and rumination.

## Clinical relevance

- **Dehorning**: in older animals the cut opens the frontal sinus that fills the horn core; for this reason **disbudding** of young calves, before the core becomes pneumatised, is preferred.
- **Frontal sinusitis**: a complication of dehorning or horn fractures; it causes nasal discharge, often unilateral, and pain on percussion over the sinus.
- **Cornual nerve block**: the cornual nerve runs along the temporal line, between the lateral canthus of the eye and the base of the horn; it is anaesthetised there before dehorning.
- **Actinomycosis (*lumpy jaw*)**: osteomyelitis caused by *Actinomyces bovis*; it affects the mandible more often, but also the maxilla.
