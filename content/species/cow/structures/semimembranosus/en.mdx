## Overview

It lies medial to the semitendinosus and divides into two bellies, one to the femur and one to the tibia.

In the carcass it is the topside, or inside round.

## Origin

Ischiatic tuberosity (*tuber ischiadicum*), on its ventral surface.

## Insertion

- Cranial belly: medial epicondyle of the femur.
- Caudal belly: medial condyle of the tibia.

## Action

Extends the hip and adducts the limb. Depending on the position of the stifle, it helps extend or flex it.

## Innervation

Sciatic nerve (*n. ischiadicus*).

## Clinical relevance

### Nutritional myodegeneration

Also called white muscle disease. It affects calves and is caused by **selenium and vitamin E** deficiency.

The large muscles of the pelvic limbs, such as this one, show pale streaks of necrosis, often symmetrical on both sides.

The calf is stiff, weak or unable to rise.
