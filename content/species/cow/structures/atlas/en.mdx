## Overview

The atlas is the first cervical vertebra. As in other species it has no vertebral body and no spinous process: it is a ring made of a **dorsal arch** and a **ventral arch**, joined laterally by two masses from which the **wings** (*alae atlantis*) project. It articulates cranially with the occipital condyles and caudally with the axis. In cattle it is a robust bone with wide, thick wings.

## Key features

- **Cranial articular foveae**: two deep cavities that receive the occipital condyles. The atlanto-occipital joint allows flexion and extension of the head (the "yes" movement).
- **Caudal articular foveae**: flatter surfaces for the axis. The atlantoaxial joint allows rotation of the head (the "no" movement).
- **Fovea of the dens (*fovea dentis*)**: on the dorsal surface of the ventral arch, where the dens of the axis rests.
- **Dorsal and ventral tubercles**: small midline prominences on each arch.
- **Wings of the atlas**: wide and palpable on each side of the neck just caudal to the skull. On their ventral surface lies the **atlantal fossa** (*fossa atlantis*).
- **Foramina**: the **lateral vertebral foramen** (*foramen vertebrale laterale*), in the dorsal arch, and the **alar foramen** (*foramen alare*), in the wing, transmit the first cervical nerve and vessels. Unlike the dog, the cat and the horse, cattle **have no transverse foramen**.

## Clinical relevance

- **Cerebellomedullary cistern puncture**: cerebrospinal fluid can be collected between the occipital bone and the dorsal arch of the atlas with the head flexed; the wings and the external occipital protuberance are the landmarks. In standing adult cattle the lumbosacral space is usually preferred.
- **Congenital malformations** of the occipitoatlantoaxial region: rare; described in calves with abnormal head posture and neurological deficits.
- **Fractures and luxations**: uncommon; they follow severe trauma.
