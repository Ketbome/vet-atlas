## Overview

The skin (*cutis*) covers the whole body surface and is continuous with the mucous membranes at the natural openings. It works as a physical, chemical and microbiological barrier and takes part in thermoregulation and in the perception of touch, temperature, pain and itch. In cattle it is **thick**, especially over the dorsal neck and trunk, and beneath it the **cutaneous trunci muscle** (*m. cutaneus trunci*) lets the animal twitch the skin to shake off insects.

## Layers

- **Epidermis**: keratinised stratified squamous epithelium without blood vessels; it is thicker on the muzzle and the skin of the digits.
- **Dermis**: dense connective tissue with collagen and elastic fibres; it holds vessels, nerves, hair follicles, arrector pili muscles and glands. This layer gives bovine skin its thickness.
- **Hypodermis (*tela subcutanea*)**: loose connective tissue with fat.

## Hair and glands

- **Coat**: **simple** hair follicles, with a single hair per opening. Coat length and density vary with breed and season.
- **Sweat glands**: apocrine, associated with the hair follicles. Unlike the dog and cat, cattle lose heat by sweating as well as by breathing.
- **Sebaceous glands**: open into the follicles and lubricate hair and skin.
- **Dewlap**: fold of skin on the ventral neck, very well developed in zebu cattle (*Bos indicus*).

## Specialised regions

- **Muzzle (*planum nasolabiale*)**: hairless area between the nostrils and the upper lip. The **nasolabial glands** in its dermis keep it moist and cool; its pattern of grooves and ridges is unique to each animal and can be used to identify it.
- **Udder, horns and hooves**: modified skin structures described separately.

## Clinical relevance

- **Dermatophytosis (ringworm)**: infection by *Trichophyton verrucosum*, mainly in calves; it causes circular lesions with greyish crusts, often around the eyes. It is a **zoonosis**.
- **Lumpy skin disease**: disease caused by a capripoxvirus and spread by insects; it causes firm skin nodules and fever. It is notifiable.
- **Papillomatosis**: warts caused by bovine papillomavirus (BPV), common in young animals, on the head and neck.
- **Dermatophilosis**: infection by *Dermatophilus congolensis*, favoured by prolonged wetting; it forms thick crusts that pull out tufts of hair.
- **Ectoparasites**: lice and mange. *Chorioptes* mainly affects the tail base, perineum and limbs; *Psoroptes* causes extensive, very itchy lesions on the body; *Sarcoptes* can also affect people.
- **Photosensitisation**: dermatitis of unpigmented skin exposed to sunlight, caused by photodynamic plant substances or, in the hepatogenous form, by liver damage that prevents phylloerythrin from being cleared.
