## Overview

The most superficial muscle of the scapular region. In cattle it has two parts, separated by an aponeurotic strip over the spine of the scapula:

- **Cervical part** (*pars cervicalis*). From the nuchal ligament to the spine of the scapula. Its fibres run caudoventrally.
- **Thoracic part** (*pars thoracica*). From the spinous processes of the cranial thoracic vertebrae to the spine of the scapula. Its fibres run cranioventrally.

## Origin

- Cervical part: funicular part of the nuchal ligament.
- Thoracic part: supraspinous ligament of the thoracic vertebrae.

## Insertion

Spine of the scapula (*spina scapulae*).

## Action

Elevates and abducts the thoracic limb. The cervical part draws the scapula craniodorsally, the thoracic part caudodorsally.

## Innervation

Dorsal branch of the accessory nerve (*n. accessorius*, cranial nerve XI).

## Clinical relevance

The cervical part lies at the dorsal edge of the lateral neck region used for injections in cattle.

Beef quality guidelines recommend injecting in this area of the **neck**, in front of the scapula and below the nuchal ligament, and not in the hindquarter.
