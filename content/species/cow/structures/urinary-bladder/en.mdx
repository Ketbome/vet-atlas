## Overview

The urinary bladder (*vesica urinaria*) has a cranial **apex**, a **body** and a **neck** continuous with the urethra. When empty it lies on the pubis within the pelvic cavity, ventral to the uterus and vagina; when full it extends cranially over the abdominal floor. The ureters enter its dorsal wall obliquely near the neck, so that urine pressure closes their openings and prevents reflux.

## Relations and ligaments

- **Median ligament**: attaches the ventral surface to the floor of the pelvis and abdomen; in the fetus it contains the **urachus**, which connects the bladder with the umbilical cord.
- **Lateral ligaments**: their free edge carries the **round ligament**, the remnant of the umbilical artery.
- **Female urethra**: short; it opens on the floor of the vagina at the external urethral orifice. Just ventral to it opens the **suburethral diverticulum** (*diverticulum suburethrale*), a blind pouch that extends cranially under the urethra.

## Clinical relevance

- **Catheterisation**: a catheter passed blindly tends to enter the suburethral diverticulum. This is avoided by guiding it with a finger placed in the diverticulum to direct it dorsally into the urethral orifice.
- **Cystitis and pyelonephritis**: infection with *Corynebacterium renale* and other bacteria ascends from the bladder to the kidneys.
- **Enzootic haematuria**: long-term intake of bracken fern (*Pteridium aquilinum*) causes bleeding and tumours in the bladder wall, in which bovine papillomavirus is also involved.
- **Bladder eversion or prolapse**: an uncommon complication of straining after calving, through the urethra or through a vaginal tear.
- **Calf**: a patent urachus and navel infections can spread to the apex of the bladder and form abscesses.
- **Obstructive urolithiasis**: a problem of the male, with its long urethra and sigmoid flexure; in the female it is exceptional.
