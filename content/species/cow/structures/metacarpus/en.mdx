title: Metacarpus

## Overview

In cattle the metacarpus is dominated by the **large metacarpal bone** or **cannon bone** (*ossa metacarpalia III et IV*), the result of **fusion of metacarpals III and IV**, which belong to the two weight-bearing digits. The only remnant of the others is a small **metacarpal V** on the lateral side. The cannon bone is straight, flattened dorsopalmarly and covered almost only by skin, tendons and vessels, with no muscle bellies.

## Key features

- **Proximal end**: a flat articular surface for the distal row of the carpus. On the dorsomedial side, the **tuberosity of metacarpal III** receives the extensor carpi radialis.
- **Dorsal longitudinal groove (*sulcus longitudinalis dorsalis*)**: runs down the dorsal surface and marks the line of fusion of the two bones; it carries vessels. The palmar surface has a similar, shallower groove.
- **Proximal and distal metacarpal canals (*canalis metacarpi proximalis* and *distalis*)**: pass through the bone at the ends of the dorsal groove and carry vessels between the dorsal and palmar surfaces.
- **Metacarpal V (*os metacarpale V*)**: a small, rod-like bone applied to the lateral side of the proximal end.
- **Distal end**: two separate **condyles**, each with a sagittal ridge, which articulate with the proximal phalanx and the two proximal sesamoid bones of their digit at the **fetlock**. Between them lies the **intercapital notch** (*incisura intercapitalis*).

## Clinical relevance

- **Fractures in calves**: the metacarpus is one of the bones most often fractured in calves, frequently from **excessive traction during assisted calving**. They may be diaphyseal or run through the distal physis (physeal fractures).
- **Treatment**: closed fractures heal well with external coaptation in a **cast**. Because the bone has little soft tissue cover, open fractures are common and worsen the prognosis.
- **Examination**: being subcutaneous, the outline of the cannon bone and its condyles is easy to palpate.
