## Overview

In cattle the metacarpus is dominated by the **large metacarpal bone** or **cannon bone** (*ossa metacarpalia III et IV*). It results from **fusion of metacarpals III and IV**, which belong to the two weight-bearing digits.

The only remnant of the others is a small **metacarpal V** on the lateral side.

The cannon bone is straight and flattened dorsopalmarly. It is covered almost only by skin, tendons and vessels, with no muscle bellies.

## Key features

- **Proximal end**. A flat articular surface for the distal row of the carpus. On the dorsomedial side, the **tuberosity of metacarpal III** receives the extensor carpi radialis.
- **Dorsal longitudinal groove** (*sulcus longitudinalis dorsalis*). It runs down the dorsal surface and marks the line of fusion of the two bones. It carries vessels. The palmar surface has a similar, shallower groove.
- **Proximal and distal metacarpal canals** (*canalis metacarpi proximalis* and *distalis*). They pass through the bone at the ends of the dorsal groove and carry vessels between the dorsal and palmar surfaces.
- **Metacarpal V** (*os metacarpale V*). A small, rod-like bone applied to the lateral side of the proximal end.
- **Distal end**. Two separate **condyles**, each with a sagittal ridge. They articulate with the proximal phalanx and the two proximal sesamoid bones of their digit at the **fetlock**. Between them lies the **intercapital notch** (*incisura intercapitalis*).

## Clinical relevance

- The metacarpus is one of the bones most often fractured in calves, frequently from **excessive traction during assisted calving**. Fractures may be diaphyseal or run through the distal physis.
- Closed fractures heal well with external coaptation in a **cast**. Because the bone has little soft tissue cover, open fractures are common and worsen the prognosis.
- Being subcutaneous, the outline of the cannon bone and its condyles is easy to palpate.
