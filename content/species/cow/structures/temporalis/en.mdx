---
title: Temporalis
summary: Masticatory muscle that fills the temporal fossa and closes the mouth; in cattle it is much smaller than the masseter.
---

## Overview

Unlike in carnivores, in ruminants it is a relatively small muscle: chewing relies mainly on the masseter. In cattle the temporal fossa lies on the lateral side of the skull, caudal to the orbit and below the temporal line, which separates it from the frontal roof of the skull. The muscle passes medial to the zygomatic arch to the mandible.

## Origin

Temporal fossa, bounded dorsally by the temporal line.

## Insertion

Coronoid process of the mandible.

## Action

Elevates the mandible and closes the mouth.

## Innervation

Mandibular nerve (*n. mandibularis*), a branch of the trigeminal nerve (cranial nerve V).

## Clinical relevance

The **cornual nerve**, which supplies the horn, runs along the temporal line at the dorsal border of the temporal fossa. It is blocked there with local anaesthetic for dehorning.
