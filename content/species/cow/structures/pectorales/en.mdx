## Overview

Veterinary nomenclature distinguishes two groups: the superficial pectorals (*mm. pectorales superficiales*) and the deep pectoral (*m. pectoralis profundus*).

In cattle the superficial pectorals have two parts:

- **Descending pectoral** (*m. pectoralis descendens*). Cranial, runs from the manubrium to the arm.
- **Transverse pectoral** (*m. pectoralis transversus*). Runs from the sternum to the antebrachial fascia.

The **deep pectoral** is much larger. It spreads over the ventral thorax as far as the abdominal wall.

Together with the serratus ventralis they form the **thoracic sling**, which suspends the trunk between the thoracic limbs.

In meat, these muscles make up the brisket.

## Origin

- Descending pectoral: manubrium of the sternum.
- Transverse pectoral: ventral surface of the first sternebrae.
- Deep pectoral: ventral surface of the sternum, costal cartilages and fascia of the ventral abdominal wall.

## Insertion

- Descending pectoral: crest of the humerus, together with the cleidobrachialis.
- Transverse pectoral: medial antebrachial fascia.
- Deep pectoral: greater and lesser tubercles of the humerus.

## Action

Adduct the thoracic limb and support the trunk between the limbs.

The deep pectoral also draws the limb caudally. With the limb planted, it pulls the trunk cranially.

## Innervation

Cranial and caudal pectoral nerves (*nn. pectorales*) from the brachial plexus.
