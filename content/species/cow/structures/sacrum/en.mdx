## Overview

The sacrum (*os sacrum*) results from the fusion of the **five sacral vertebrae** of cattle. It is triangular: the cranial **base** articulates with the last lumbar vertebra, the caudal **apex** with the first caudal vertebra, and the **wings** articulate with the ilia at the sacroiliac joint. It forms the roof of the pelvic cavity.

## Key features

- **Promontory (*promontorium*)**: cranioventral edge of the body of the first sacral vertebra; it marks the dorsal limit of the pelvic inlet.
- **Wings of the sacrum (*alae sacri*)**: their **auricular surface** articulates with the ilium. The sacroiliac joint is almost immobile and is reinforced by strong ligaments.
- **Median sacral crest (*crista sacralis mediana*)**: in cattle the spinous processes fuse into a **continuous crest**, unlike the horse, in which they remain separate.
- **Lateral sacral crests**: formed by the fused transverse processes.
- **Dorsal and ventral sacral foramina**: four pairs of each, between the fused vertebrae; they transmit the branches of the sacral nerves.
- **Pelvic surface**: concave; the rectum lies on it.
- **Broad sacrotuberous ligament (*ligamentum sacrotuberale latum*)**: wide sheet running from the lateral border of the sacrum and the first caudal vertebrae to the ischial spine and the ischial tuber, forming the lateral wall of the pelvis.

## Clinical relevance

- **Signs of impending calving**: in the days before parturition the pelvic ligaments relax and the area on each side of the tail head sinks.
- **Epidural anaesthesia**: **caudal** epidural is given at the sacrococcygeal space or, more often, at the first intercoccygeal space, and anaesthetises the tail, perineum, vagina and rectum without affecting gait; **lumbosacral** epidural, between L6 and the sacrum, reaches more cranial regions.
- **Sacrocaudal injuries**: fractures or luxations of the sacrum and the sacrocaudal junction, typical of cows mounted by others in heat; they cause tail paralysis and sometimes disturbances of urination and defecation.
