## Overview

The tibia is the weight-bearing bone of the leg (*crus*), between the femur and the tarsus.

In cattle the **fibula** is much reduced:

- there is no fibular shaft
- its **proximal end** fuses with the lateral condyle of the tibia
- its **distal end** persists as a separate bone, the **malleolar bone** (*os malleolare*)

The medial surface of the tibia is **subcutaneous** along its whole length.

## Proximal end

- **Medial and lateral condyles** (*condylus medialis* and *lateralis*). Articular surfaces for the femoral condyles, via the menisci.
- **Intercondylar eminence** (*eminentia intercondylaris*). Between the condyles. The cruciate ligaments and the menisci attach to it.
- **Tibial tuberosity** (*tuberositas tibiae*). Large and cranial. It receives the **three patellar ligaments**. It continues distally as the **cranial border** (*margo cranialis*).
- **Fibular remnant**. The head of the fibula forms a small prominence fused with the lateral condyle.

## Shaft and distal end

- **Shaft**. Triangular proximally and more rounded distally. Muscles cover its cranial, lateral and caudal surfaces but not the medial one.
- **Cochlea of the tibia** (*cochlea tibiae*). The distal articular surface, with two grooves separated by an intermediate ridge. The proximal trochlea of the talus fits into it.
- **Medial malleolus** (*malleolus medialis*). The medial prominence of the hock, palpable.
- **Malleolar bone** (*os malleolare*). It takes the place of the lateral malleolus. It articulates with the tibia, the talus and the calcaneus.

## Clinical relevance

- **Tibial fractures**. They occur in calves and adults. Because the medial surface is subcutaneous, many are open. Being high on the limb, a standard cast is not enough: a Thomas splint combined with a cast, external fixators or internal fixation are used.
- **Physeal fractures**. They affect the proximal and distal tibia in calves.
- **Haematogenous osteomyelitis**. In septicaemic calves, the distal metaphysis of the tibia is one of its sites.
