## Overview

The tibia is the weight-bearing bone of the leg (*crus*), between the femur and the tarsus. In cattle the **fibula** is much reduced: there is no fibular shaft, its **proximal end** fuses with the lateral condyle of the tibia and its **distal end** persists as a separate bone, the **malleolar bone** (*os malleolare*). The medial surface of the tibia is **subcutaneous** along its whole length.

## Proximal end

- **Medial and lateral condyles (*condylus medialis* and *lateralis*)**: articular surfaces for the femoral condyles, via the menisci.
- **Intercondylar eminence (*eminentia intercondylaris*)**: between the condyles, attachment of the cruciate ligaments and the menisci.
- **Tibial tuberosity (*tuberositas tibiae*)**: large and cranial; it receives the **three patellar ligaments**. It continues distally as the **cranial border** (*margo cranialis*).
- **Fibular remnant**: the head of the fibula forms a small prominence fused with the lateral condyle.

## Shaft and distal end

- **Shaft**: triangular proximally and more rounded distally; muscles cover its cranial, lateral and caudal surfaces but not the medial one.
- **Cochlea of the tibia (*cochlea tibiae*)**: the distal articular surface, with two grooves separated by an intermediate ridge, into which the proximal trochlea of the talus fits.
- **Medial malleolus (*malleolus medialis*)**: the medial prominence of the hock, palpable.
- **Malleolar bone (*os malleolare*)**: takes the place of the lateral malleolus; articulates with the tibia, the talus and the calcaneus.

## Clinical relevance

- **Tibial fractures**: occur in calves and adults; because the medial surface is subcutaneous, many are open. Being high on the limb, a standard cast is not enough: a Thomas splint combined with a cast, external fixators or internal fixation are used.
- **Physeal fractures** of the proximal and distal tibia in calves.
- **Haematogenous osteomyelitis** in septicaemic calves: the distal metaphysis of the tibia is one of its sites.
