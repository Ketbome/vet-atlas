## Overview

It has a lateral and a medial head. The superficial digital flexor arises between them.

Cattle also have a small **soleus** (*m. soleus*), which joins the gastrocnemius tendon.

The **common calcanean tendon**, the "Achilles tendon", is formed by:

- the tendon of the gastrocnemius
- the tendon of the superficial digital flexor
- the tarsal tendons of the biceps femoris and semitendinosus

## Origin

Lateral and medial supracondylar tuberosities of the femur.

## Insertion

Calcaneal tuber (*tuber calcanei*).

## Action

Extends the tarsus and flexes the stifle.

## Innervation

Tibial nerve (*n. tibialis*).

## Clinical relevance

### Spastic paresis

Also called "Elso heel". It appears in young cattle, from a few weeks to several months old. The gastrocnemius undergoes progressive spastic contraction.

The hock becomes straight and overextended and the limb is held caudally, sometimes without bearing weight.

It has a hereditary component. It is treated by tibial neurectomy or tenotomy, and affected animals should not be used for breeding.

### Rupture and myopathy

- **Gastrocnemius rupture** occurs in downer cows or cows straining to rise. The hock drops and the animal stands with the tarsus close to the ground.
- In downer cow syndrome, prolonged recumbency causes **ischaemic myopathy** of the pelvic limb muscles.
