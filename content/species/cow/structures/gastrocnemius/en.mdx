---
title: Gastrocnemius
summary: Calf muscle that extends the tarsus through the common calcanean tendon.
---

## Overview

It has a lateral and a medial head, between which the superficial digital flexor arises. Cattle also have a small **soleus** (*m. soleus*), which joins the gastrocnemius tendon. The **common calcanean tendon** (the "Achilles tendon") is formed by the tendon of the gastrocnemius, that of the superficial digital flexor and the tarsal tendons of the biceps femoris and semitendinosus.

## Origin

Lateral and medial supracondylar tuberosities of the femur.

## Insertion

Calcaneal tuber (*tuber calcanei*).

## Action

Extends the tarsus and flexes the stifle.

## Innervation

Tibial nerve (*n. tibialis*).

## Clinical relevance

- **Spastic paresis** ("Elso heel"): in young cattle, from a few weeks to several months old, the gastrocnemius undergoes progressive spastic contraction. The hock becomes straight and overextended and the limb is held caudally, sometimes without bearing weight. It has a hereditary component; it is treated by tibial neurectomy or tenotomy, and affected animals should not be used for breeding.
- **Gastrocnemius rupture** occurs in downer cows or cows straining to rise: the hock drops and the animal stands with the tarsus close to the ground.
- In downer cow syndrome, prolonged recumbency causes **ischaemic myopathy** of the pelvic limb muscles.
