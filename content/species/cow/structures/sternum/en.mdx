## Overview

The sternum (*sternum*) forms the floor of the rib cage. In cattle it consists of **seven sternebrae** (*sternebrae*). The first is the **manubrium**, the following ones form the **body** and the last is the **xiphoid process**. Unlike the keel-shaped sternum of the horse, the bovine sternum is **broad and flattened** dorsoventrally and widens caudally.

## Key features

- **Manubrium (*manubrium sterni*)**: first sternebra, at the thoracic inlet; the first pair of ribs articulates with it. In cattle it **does not fuse** with the body: a true joint, the **manubriosternal joint** (*articulatio manubriosternalis*), connects them and allows some movement.
- **Body (*corpus sterni*)**: the intermediate sternebrae, joined by cartilage that ossifies with age. The cartilages of the remaining sternal ribs articulate at the **costal notches**, level with the spaces between sternebrae.
- **Xiphoid process (*processus xiphoideus*)**: last sternebra, extended by the **xiphoid cartilage**, a wide plate on which the linea alba inserts.
- **Muscles**: the pectoral muscles originate on the ventral surface; the transversus thoracis on the dorsal surface.

## Clinical relevance

- **Sternal recumbency**: the normal resting position of the cow, lying on the sternum with the limbs folded. A downer cow should be kept in sternal recumbency, because prolonged lateral recumbency prevents eructation of rumen gas and favours bloat, regurgitation and aspiration pneumonia.
- **Traumatic reticuloperitonitis**: the reticulum rests on the xiphoid region, next to the diaphragm; firm pressure with a fist or a bar under the xiphoid causes pain and a grunt when a metallic foreign body has perforated its wall.
- **Brisket oedema**: in right-sided heart failure (traumatic pericarditis, high-altitude disease) subcutaneous oedema accumulates over the sternal region and in the intermandibular space.
