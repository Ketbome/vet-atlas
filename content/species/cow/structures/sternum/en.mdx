## Overview

The sternum forms the floor of the rib cage. In cattle it consists of **seven sternebrae** (*sternebrae*):

- the first is the **manubrium**
- the following ones form the **body**
- the last is the **xiphoid process**

Unlike the keel-shaped sternum of the horse, the bovine sternum is **broad and flattened** dorsoventrally and widens caudally.

## Parts

- **Manubrium** (*manubrium sterni*). First sternebra, at the thoracic inlet. The first pair of ribs articulates with it. In cattle it **does not fuse** with the body: a true joint, the **manubriosternal joint** (*articulatio manubriosternalis*), connects them and allows some movement.
- **Body** (*corpus sterni*). The intermediate sternebrae, joined by cartilage that ossifies with age. The cartilages of the remaining sternal ribs articulate at the **costal notches**, level with the spaces between sternebrae.
- **Xiphoid process** (*processus xiphoideus*). Last sternebra, extended by the **xiphoid cartilage**, a wide plate on which the linea alba inserts.

The pectoral muscles originate on the ventral surface of the sternum. The transversus thoracis originates on the dorsal surface.

## Clinical relevance

- **Sternal recumbency**. The normal resting position of the cow, lying on the sternum with the limbs folded. A downer cow should be kept in sternal recumbency: prolonged lateral recumbency prevents eructation of rumen gas and favours bloat, regurgitation and aspiration pneumonia.
- **Traumatic reticuloperitonitis**. The reticulum rests on the xiphoid region, next to the diaphragm. When a metallic foreign body has perforated its wall, firm pressure with a fist or a bar under the xiphoid causes pain and a grunt.
- **Brisket oedema**. In right-sided heart failure, as in traumatic pericarditis or high-altitude disease, subcutaneous oedema accumulates over the sternal region and in the intermandibular space.
