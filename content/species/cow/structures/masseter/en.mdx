---
title: Masseter
summary: Large, multilayered masticatory muscle of the cheek; it drives the lateral chewing stroke of ruminants.
---

## Overview

In ruminants it is large and made up of **several layers** separated by tendinous sheets, grouped into a superficial and a deep part. The bovine mandible is narrower than the upper jaw, so the cheek teeth meet on one side at a time. Chewing, including rumination, is a **lateral grinding stroke** in which the masseter plays a leading role.

## Origin

Facial tuber (*tuber faciale*) of the maxilla and zygomatic arch.

## Insertion

Lateral surface of the mandibular ramus and its caudal ventral border, as far as the angle.

## Action

Elevates the mandible and closes the mouth. Contraction on one side shifts the mandible towards that side and produces the grinding stroke.

## Innervation

Masseteric nerve, from the mandibular nerve (cranial nerve V).

## Clinical relevance

- Atrophy limited to one side suggests a lesion of the mandibular nerve on that side.
- In bovine meat inspection the masseters are incised to look for cysts of *Cysticercus bovis*, the larval stage of *Taenia saginata*.
