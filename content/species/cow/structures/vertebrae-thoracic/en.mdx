## Overview

Cattle have **thirteen thoracic vertebrae**. The vertebral formula is C7, T13, L6, S5, Cd18–20. Together with the ribs and the sternum they form the rib cage.

Their bodies are short and their spinous processes long and plate-like. Those of the first thoracic vertebrae form the bony base of the **withers** (*regio interscapularis*), between the dorsal borders of the scapulae.

## Key features

- **Body** (*corpus vertebrae*). Short, with cranial and caudal **costal foveae**. The head of each rib articulates with two consecutive vertebrae. The last thoracic vertebra has cranial foveae only.
- **Transverse processes**. Short, with a **transverse costal fovea** for the tubercle of the rib of the same number. In the last thoracic vertebrae they carry **mamillary processes**.
- **Spinous processes**. Long, broad and flattened, inclined caudally. The longest belong to the first thoracic vertebrae, and they shorten progressively towards the lumbar region. They give attachment to the supraspinous ligament and the epaxial muscles.
- **Articular processes**. Small, with almost horizontal surfaces. In the last thoracic vertebrae they take the lumbar form.
- **Vertebral canal**. Relatively narrow. It houses the thoracic spinal cord.

## Clinical relevance

- **Vertebral body abscesses**. In calves, vertebral osteomyelitis secondary to infections such as omphalitis or pneumonia can compress the spinal cord. It causes pelvic limb paresis, ataxia and pain on palpation of the spine.
- **Withers pinch test**. When the skin over the withers is pinched, a normal cow dips its back. Resistance or a grunt suggests cranial abdominal pain, as in traumatic reticuloperitonitis.
- **Back posture**. An arched back, or kyphosis, is a non-specific sign of abdominal pain or lameness in cattle.
