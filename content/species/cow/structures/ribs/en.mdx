## Overview

Cattle have **thirteen pairs of ribs** (*costae*), one for each thoracic vertebra. Each rib has a dorsal bony part and a ventral **costal cartilage**. The **first eight pairs** are **sternal** (true) ribs: their cartilage articulates directly with the sternum. The **last five** are **asternal** (false) ribs: they do not reach the sternum and their cartilages form the **costal arch** (*arcus costalis*). Compared with those of the dog and cat, bovine ribs are **broad and flat**, and the intercostal spaces are relatively narrow.

## Key features

- **Head (*caput costae*)**: articulates with the costal foveae of two consecutive vertebrae.
- **Neck** and **tubercle**: the tubercle articulates with the transverse process of the vertebra of the same number.
- **Body**: long, curved and broad; the intercostal vein, artery and nerve run along its caudal border.
- **Angle**: point of greatest curvature, where the rib changes direction.
- **Costochondral junction**: between the bony part and the cartilage.
- **Thorax and abdomen**: because the diaphragm attaches far cranially, the last ribs cover abdominal organs: the reticulum and rumen on the left, the liver and omasum on the right.

## Clinical relevance

- **Rib fractures in calves**: common after dystocia with forced traction, especially in large calves; they can cause respiratory distress and injure the lung or heart.
- **Left displaced abomasum**: the characteristic "ping" is heard by simultaneous auscultation and percussion over the last left ribs.
- **Rib resection**: gives access to the thoracic cavity, for example for pericardiotomy in traumatic pericarditis.
