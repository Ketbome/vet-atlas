## Overview

Cattle have **thirteen pairs of ribs** (*costae*), one for each thoracic vertebra. Each rib has a dorsal bony part and a ventral **costal cartilage**.

- **Sternal**, or true, ribs. These are the **first eight pairs**, and their cartilage articulates directly with the sternum.
- **Asternal**, or false, ribs. These are the **last five**. They do not reach the sternum and their cartilages form the **costal arch** (*arcus costalis*).

Compared with those of the dog and cat, bovine ribs are **broad and flat**, and the intercostal spaces are relatively narrow.

## Key features

- **Head** (*caput costae*). Articulates with the costal foveae of two consecutive vertebrae.
- **Neck** and **tubercle**. The tubercle articulates with the transverse process of the vertebra of the same number.
- **Body**. Long, curved and broad. The intercostal vein, artery and nerve run along its caudal border.
- **Angle**. Point of greatest curvature, where the rib changes direction.
- **Costochondral junction**. Between the bony part and the cartilage.

### Thorax and abdomen

The diaphragm attaches far cranially. For this reason the last ribs cover abdominal organs:

- on the left, the reticulum and rumen
- on the right, the liver and omasum

## Clinical relevance

### Rib fractures in calves

Common after dystocia with forced traction, especially in large calves.

They can cause respiratory distress and injure the lung or heart.

### Left displaced abomasum

The characteristic "ping" is heard by simultaneous auscultation and percussion over the last left ribs.

### Rib resection

Gives access to the thoracic cavity, for example for pericardiotomy in traumatic pericarditis.
