## Overview

In cattle, as in the other ruminants, the gluteus superficialis **does not exist as a separate muscle**: it is fused with the biceps femoris and the two form the **gluteobiceps** (*m. gluteobiceps*). It corresponds to the cranial, vertebral part of that muscle, which arises from the sacrum. The model shows it as a piece of its own so it can be located, but in dissection it does not separate from the biceps.

## Origin

Sacrum, broad sacrotuberous ligament and gluteal fascia.

## Insertion

Together with the cranial part of the biceps femoris, on the fascia lata and, through it, the region of the patella.

## Action

Together with the biceps femoris, extends the hip and abducts the pelvic limb.

## Innervation

Caudal gluteal nerve (*n. gluteus caudalis*).
