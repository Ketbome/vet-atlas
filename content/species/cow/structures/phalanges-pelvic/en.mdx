## Overview

As in the forefoot, the bovine hindfoot bears weight on **two main digits**: **III**, medial, and **IV**, lateral. Each has a proximal, middle and distal phalanx.

Digits **II and V** are the **dewclaws**, or accessory digits, on the plantar side of the fetlock. They hold one or two small bones that **do not articulate** with the skeleton.

In the pelvic limb the **lateral claw is larger** and carries more load than the medial, especially during walking. This is why it is the one most often diseased.

## Phalanges

- **Proximal phalanx** (*phalanx proximalis*). Articulates at the fetlock with one condyle of the metatarsus and with the proximal sesamoid bones.
- **Middle phalanx** (*phalanx media*). Forms the pastern joint with the proximal phalanx.
- **Distal phalanx** (*phalanx distalis*). Enclosed in the **claw**. Its **solear surface** rests on the corium of the sole. On it, the **flexor tubercle** receives the tendon of the deep digital flexor. The **extensor process** receives the digital extensor.

## Sesamoid bones

- **Proximal sesamoid bones**. **Four** per limb, two on the plantar side of each fetlock.
- **Distal sesamoid bones**. **One per digit**, plantar to the distal interphalangeal joint.

## Clinical relevance

### Sole ulcer

Appears at the typical site of the sole, near the heel, beneath the **flexor tubercle** of the distal phalanx. It predominates in the **lateral hind claw**.

Sinking of the distal phalanx within the claw compresses the corium between the bone and the sole.

### White line disease and toe necrosis

These also predominate in the lateral hind claw. Toe necrosis affects the tip of the distal phalanx.

### Septic pedal osteitis and septic distal interphalangeal arthritis

They develop when these lesions become deeply infected.

They can be treated by resection of the affected tissue or amputation of the digit, often with a block on the sound claw.

### Fractures of the distal phalanx

Less common than in the thoracic limb.
