title: Phalanges of the pelvic limb
summary: Bones of the two main digits of the hindfoot (III and IV), each with three phalanges, and of the dewclaws. The lateral claw carries more load and concentrates most lesions.

## Overview

As in the forefoot, the bovine hindfoot bears weight on **two main digits**, **III** (medial) and **IV** (lateral), each with a **proximal, middle and distal phalanx**. Digits **II and V** are the **dewclaws** (accessory digits), on the plantar side of the fetlock, with one or two small bones that **do not articulate** with the skeleton. In the pelvic limb the **lateral claw is larger** and carries more load than the medial, especially during walking; this is why it is the one most often diseased.

## Phalanges

- **Proximal phalanx (*phalanx proximalis*)**: articulates at the fetlock with one condyle of the metatarsus and with the proximal sesamoid bones.
- **Middle phalanx (*phalanx media*)**: forms the pastern joint with the proximal phalanx.
- **Distal phalanx (*phalanx distalis*)**: enclosed in the **claw**. Its **solear surface** rests on the corium of the sole; on it the **flexor tubercle** receives the tendon of the deep digital flexor. The **extensor process** receives the digital extensor.

## Sesamoid bones

- **Proximal sesamoid bones**: **four** per limb, two on the plantar side of each fetlock.
- **Distal sesamoid bones**: **one per digit**, plantar to the distal interphalangeal joint.

## Clinical relevance

- **Sole ulcer**: appears at the typical site of the sole, near the heel, beneath the **flexor tubercle** of the distal phalanx, mostly in the **lateral hind claw**. Sinking of the distal phalanx within the claw compresses the corium between the bone and the sole.
- **White line disease** and **toe necrosis**: also predominate in the lateral hind claw; toe necrosis affects the tip of the distal phalanx.
- **Septic pedal osteitis** and **septic distal interphalangeal arthritis**: when these lesions become deeply infected. They can be treated by resection of the affected tissue or amputation of the digit, often with a block on the sound claw.
- **Fractures of the distal phalanx**: less common than in the thoracic limb.
