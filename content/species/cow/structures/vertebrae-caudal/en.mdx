## Overview

Cattle have **18 to 20 caudal vertebrae** (*vertebrae caudales*), which form the skeleton of the tail. They decrease in size and complexity from cranial to caudal: the first ones resemble the other vertebrae, whereas the last are simple bony rods. The first articulates with the apex of the sacrum.

## Key features

- **First caudal vertebrae**: keep a body, vertebral arch, small spinous processes and wide transverse processes.
- **Haemal arches (*arcus haemales*)**: on the ventral surface of the first caudal vertebrae; they form a canal that protects the **median caudal artery and vein**, which run along the ventral midline of the tail.
- **Distal caudal vertebrae**: lose the arch and processes and are reduced to ever smaller cylindrical bodies.
- **Intercoccygeal joints**: the intervertebral discs make the tail very mobile; the first movable space, between Cd1 and Cd2, is located by moving the tail up and down.

## Clinical relevance

- **Blood sampling**: the **median caudal vein** is punctured on the ventral midline of the proximal third of the tail, avoiding the haemal arches; it is the usual route in dairy cattle. The pulse of the median caudal artery is taken at the same site.
- **Caudal epidural anaesthesia**: given at the Cd1–Cd2 space or at the sacrococcygeal space.
- **Tail fractures and trauma**: common from being stepped on, trapped or restrained by the tail with excessive force; they can cause tail deviation, necrosis of the distal tail or paralysis.
