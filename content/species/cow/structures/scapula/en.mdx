## Overview

The scapula (*scapula*) is a flat, roughly triangular bone lying against the lateral surface of the first ribs.

Its dorsal border is extended by the **scapular cartilage**.

Cattle have no clavicle. The thoracic limb is attached to the trunk by muscles only, in a **synsarcosis**: chiefly the serratus ventralis, trapezius, rhomboideus and pectoral muscles.

## Key features

- **Spine of the scapula** (*spina scapulae*). Ridge dividing the lateral surface into two fossae. In cattle it is **displaced towards the cranial border**, so the **infraspinous fossa** is much larger than the **supraspinous fossa**. The muscles of the same names originate in them.
- **Acromion**. Distal end of the spine. It is well developed in cattle and absent in the horse. The acromial part of the deltoideus originates on it.
- **Glenoid cavity** (*cavitas glenoidalis*). Shallow articular surface that receives the head of the humerus.
- **Supraglenoid tubercle**. Cranial to the glenoid cavity. Origin of the biceps brachii muscle. The small **coracoid process** projects from its medial side and gives origin to the coracobrachialis.
- **Neck of the scapula**. Narrowing proximal to the glenoid cavity.
- **Borders**. The cranial border is thin and the caudal border thick.

## Clinical relevance

### Palpation landmarks

The spine and acromion are palpable in the live animal.

The scapula marks the caudal boundary of the **neck triangle** recommended for injections.

### Fractures

Uncommon in cattle. They follow severe trauma such as falls or collisions.
