## Overview

The scapula (*scapula*) is a flat, roughly triangular bone lying against the lateral surface of the first ribs. Its dorsal border is extended by the **scapular cartilage**. Cattle have no clavicle: the thoracic limb is attached to the trunk by muscles only (synsarcosis), chiefly the serratus ventralis, trapezius, rhomboideus and pectoral muscles.

## Key features

- **Spine of the scapula (*spina scapulae*)**: ridge dividing the lateral surface into two fossae. In cattle it is **displaced towards the cranial border**, so the **infraspinous fossa** is much larger than the **supraspinous fossa**; the muscles of the same names originate in them.
- **Acromion**: distal end of the spine, well developed in cattle (absent in the horse); the acromial part of the deltoideus originates on it.
- **Glenoid cavity (*cavitas glenoidalis*)**: shallow articular surface that receives the head of the humerus.
- **Supraglenoid tubercle**: cranial to the glenoid cavity; origin of the biceps brachii muscle. The small **coracoid process** projects from its medial side and gives origin to the coracobrachialis.
- **Neck of the scapula**: narrowing proximal to the glenoid cavity.
- **Borders**: the cranial border is thin; the caudal border is thick.

## Clinical relevance

- **Palpation landmarks**: the spine and acromion are palpable in the live animal; the scapula marks the caudal boundary of the neck triangle recommended for injections.
- **Fractures**: uncommon in cattle; they follow severe trauma such as falls or collisions.
