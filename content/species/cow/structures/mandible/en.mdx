## Overview

The mandible (*mandibula*) is the only movable bone of the skull.

It is formed by two halves joined rostrally at the **mandibular symphysis** (*symphysis mandibulae*). In cattle the symphysis **does not ossify**. It stays fibrocartilaginous throughout life and allows slight movement between the two halves.

Each half has a horizontal **body**, which carries the teeth, and a vertical **ramus**, which articulates with the temporal bone.

## Body

- **Incisive part** (*pars incisiva*). It carries three incisors and the **canine** on each side; in cattle the canine is **incisiform**. The eight teeth form a fan-shaped arcade that works against the dental pad of the upper jaw. In practice they are called centrals, first intermediates, second intermediates and corners.
- **Interalveolar margin** (*margo interalveolaris*). A long toothless gap, or diastema, between the canine and the first premolar.
- **Molar part** (*pars molaris*). Three premolars (P2–P4) and three selenodont molars on each side. The lower arcades are set closer together than the upper ones, so the teeth grind against each other with a side-to-side movement of the mandible.
- **Mental foramen** (*foramen mentale*). On the lateral surface of the body, in the region of the diastema. Exit of the mental nerves and vessels.

## Ramus

- **Condylar process** (*processus condylaris*). It carries the **head of the mandible**, which articulates with the temporal bone at the temporomandibular joint.
- **Coronoid process** (*processus coronoideus*). Tall. The temporalis muscle inserts on it.
- **Angle of the mandible**. Broad. The masseter inserts on its lateral surface and the medial pterygoid on its medial surface.
- **Mandibular foramen** (*foramen mandibulae*). On the medial surface of the ramus. It is the entrance to the mandibular canal, which carries the inferior alveolar nerve and the vessels of the lower teeth.

## Clinical relevance

- **Actinomycosis** (*lumpy jaw*) is a pyogranulomatous osteomyelitis caused by *Actinomyces bovis*. The bacterium enters through wounds of the oral mucosa caused by coarse forage or erupting teeth. It produces a hard, immovable bony swelling with draining tracts that discharge pus with yellowish granules. Affected teeth loosen and chewing becomes difficult.
- **Age** is estimated from the order of eruption of the permanent incisors, from centrals to corners, and from their wear.
- In old cows, **dental wear and loss** impair prehension and chewing and lead to loss of body condition. They affect the incisors and cheek teeth, with irregular or missing molars.
