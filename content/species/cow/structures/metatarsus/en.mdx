---
title: Metatarsus
summary: Large metatarsal bone or cannon bone of the pelvic limb, formed by fusion of metatarsals III and IV. It is longer than the metacarpus and more square in section.
---

## Overview

As in the thoracic limb, the bovine metatarsus is formed mainly by the **large metatarsal bone** or **cannon bone** (*ossa metatarsalia III et IV*), the result of **fusion of metatarsals III and IV**. Compared with the metacarpus it is **longer** and **more square in section**, and its dorsal groove is deeper. It runs from the tarsus to the fetlock, covered only by skin, tendons and vessels.

## Key features

- **Proximal end**: articular surface for the fused second and third tarsal bone and the centroquartal bone.
- **Metatarsal II (*os metatarsale II*)**: may be present as a small, sesamoid-like bone on the plantaromedial side of the proximal end.
- **Dorsal longitudinal groove (*sulcus longitudinalis dorsalis*)**: well marked and deep, it shows the line of fusion and carries vessels. The plantar surface has a similar groove.
- **Proximal and distal metatarsal canals**: connect the dorsal and plantar surfaces at the ends of the dorsal groove and carry vessels.
- **Distal end**: two **condyles** with a sagittal ridge, one for each main digit, separated by the **intercapital notch** (*incisura intercapitalis*); they articulate at the **fetlock** with the proximal phalanx and the proximal sesamoid bones.

## Clinical relevance

- **Fractures in calves**: like metacarpal fractures, they are common and often caused by **excessive traction during assisted calving**. They may be diaphyseal or physeal, through the distal physis.
- **Treatment**: closed fractures usually heal well in a **cast**; open fractures are common because of the scant soft tissue cover, and they worsen the prognosis.
- **Examination**: the cannon bone and its condyles can be palpated under the skin along their whole length.
