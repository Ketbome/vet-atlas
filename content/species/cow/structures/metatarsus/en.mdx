## Overview

As in the thoracic limb, the bovine metatarsus is formed mainly by the **large metatarsal bone** or **cannon bone** (*ossa metatarsalia III et IV*). It results from **fusion of metatarsals III and IV**.

Compared with the metacarpus it is **longer** and **more square in section**, and its dorsal groove is deeper.

It runs from the tarsus to the fetlock, covered only by skin, tendons and vessels.

## Key features

- **Proximal end**. Articular surface for the fused second and third tarsal bone and the centroquartal bone.
- **Metatarsal II** (*os metatarsale II*). It may be present as a small, sesamoid-like bone on the plantaromedial side of the proximal end.
- **Dorsal longitudinal groove** (*sulcus longitudinalis dorsalis*). Well marked and deep. It shows the line of fusion and carries vessels. The plantar surface has a similar groove.
- **Proximal and distal metatarsal canals**. They connect the dorsal and plantar surfaces at the ends of the dorsal groove and carry vessels.
- **Distal end**. Two **condyles** with a sagittal ridge, one for each main digit, separated by the **intercapital notch** (*incisura intercapitalis*). They articulate at the **fetlock** with the proximal phalanx and the proximal sesamoid bones.

## Clinical relevance

- Like metacarpal fractures, **fractures in calves** are common and often caused by **excessive traction during assisted calving**. They may be diaphyseal or physeal, through the distal physis.
- Closed fractures usually heal well in a **cast**. Open fractures are common because of the scant soft tissue cover, and they worsen the prognosis.
- The cannon bone and its condyles can be palpated under the skin along their whole length.
