---
title: External abdominal oblique
summary: The most superficial muscle of the lateral abdominal wall; its fibres run caudoventrally.
---

## Overview

It has a costal and a lumbar part. Its fleshy part covers the lateral abdominal wall and the caudoventral thorax; ventrally it continues as an aponeurosis that reaches the linea alba. The model shows the fleshy part.

In the bovine flank it forms the outermost muscle layer of the **paralumbar fossa**, the hollow bounded by the last rib, the lumbar transverse processes and the tuber coxae. Deep to it lie the internal oblique and the transversus abdominis.

## Origin

- Costal part: lateral surfaces of the caudal ribs, by digitations alternating with those of the serratus ventralis.
- Lumbar part: last rib and thoracolumbar fascia.

## Insertion

Through its aponeurosis: linea alba, prepubic tendon and inguinal ligament.

## Action

Compresses the abdomen (expiration, defecation, urination, parturition) and bends the trunk to the same side.

## Innervation

Ventral branches of the caudal thoracic and cranial lumbar nerves. The skin and muscles of the flank depend mainly on nerves T13, L1 and L2.

## Clinical relevance

- **Flank laparotomy**, in the standing animal, passes in turn through the external oblique, the internal oblique, the transversus abdominis and the peritoneum. Through the **right flank** displaced abomasum (right or left) is corrected with omentopexy; through the **left flank** rumenotomy and abomasopexy for left displacement are performed. Caesarean section is often done through the left flank.
- The flank wall is desensitised with **paravertebral anaesthesia** of nerves T13, L1 and L2 or with an **inverted-L block**.
- Its aponeurosis contains the superficial inguinal ring.
