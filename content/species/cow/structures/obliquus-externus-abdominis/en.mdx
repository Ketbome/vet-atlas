## Overview

It has a costal and a lumbar part. Its fleshy part covers the lateral abdominal wall and the caudoventral thorax. Ventrally it continues as an aponeurosis that reaches the linea alba. The model shows the fleshy part.

In the bovine flank it forms the outermost muscle layer of the **paralumbar fossa**. This is the hollow bounded by the last rib, the lumbar transverse processes and the tuber coxae.

Deep to it lie the internal oblique and the transversus abdominis.

## Origin

- Costal part. Lateral surfaces of the caudal ribs, by digitations alternating with those of the serratus ventralis.
- Lumbar part. Last rib and thoracolumbar fascia.

## Insertion

Through its aponeurosis, on the linea alba, the prepubic tendon and the inguinal ligament.

## Action

Compresses the abdomen in expiration, defecation, urination and parturition.

Bends the trunk to the same side.

## Innervation

Ventral branches of the caudal thoracic and cranial lumbar nerves.

The skin and muscles of the flank depend mainly on nerves T13, L1 and L2.

## Clinical relevance

- **Flank laparotomy**, in the standing animal, passes in turn through the external oblique, the internal oblique, the transversus abdominis and the peritoneum.
- Through the **right flank**, displaced abomasum, to the right or to the left, is corrected with omentopexy.
- Through the **left flank**, rumenotomy and abomasopexy for left displacement are performed. Caesarean section is often done through the left flank.
- The flank wall is desensitised with **paravertebral anaesthesia** of nerves T13, L1 and L2 or with an **inverted-L block**.
- Its aponeurosis contains the superficial inguinal ring.
