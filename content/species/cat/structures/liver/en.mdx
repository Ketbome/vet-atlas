## Topography

Its convex diaphragmatic surface fits the dome of the diaphragm.

Its concave visceral surface contacts:

- the stomach, on the left
- the duodenum and pancreas, on the right
- the right kidney, which sits in the renal impression of the caudate process

The caudal vena cava runs along its dorsal border. The portal vein, hepatic artery and bile duct enter and leave at the hilus (*porta hepatis*).

## Lobes and biliary tract

### Lobes

- Left lateral and left medial lobes.
- Quadrate lobe, between the medial lobes.
- Right medial and right lateral lobes.
- Caudate lobe, with the papillary and caudate processes.

### Biliary tract

- **Gallbladder** (*vesica fellea*). It lies in a fossa between the quadrate and right medial lobes. Bilobed or double gallbladders are congenital anomalies seen more often in the cat than in other domestic species. They are usually of no clinical importance.
- **Bile duct** (*ductus choledochus*). In the cat it joins the main pancreatic duct before opening at the major duodenal papilla. Bile and pancreatic juice thus share the final segment.

## Clinical relevance

### Hepatic lipidosis

It is the most common liver disease of the cat. It develops when a cat, often obese, stops eating for several days.

It causes jaundice and an enlarged, hyperechoic liver. Treatment is **assisted feeding**, usually through an esophagostomy tube.

### Other diseases

- **Cholangitis and triaditis**. The common opening of the bile and pancreatic ducts makes it easier for inflammation to involve the biliary tract, pancreas and small intestine together.
- **Feline infectious peritonitis**. Pyogranulomas on the liver surface and, in the effusive form, protein-rich ascites.
- **Portosystemic shunts**. Microhepatica, ptyalism and neurological signs after meals.

### Drug metabolism

The cat has a very limited capacity for **glucuronidation**. Paracetamol is therefore toxic, and other drugs need adjusted doses or intervals.
