## Overview

The metacarpus (*ossa metacarpalia*) is made of **five metacarpal bones**, numbered I (medial) to V (lateral). The cat is **digitigrade**: it stands on its digits only, with the metacarpus nearly vertical and raised off the ground, which functionally lengthens the limb.

## Key features

- **Metacarpal I**: short, carries the first digit (dewclaw), which does not touch the ground.
- **Metacarpals II to V**: long and parallel; **III** and **IV** are the longest and most central and bear most of the load.
- **Base**: the proximal end, which articulates with the distal carpal bones and with neighbouring metacarpals.
- **Head**: rounded distal end with a sagittal ridge, articulating with the proximal phalanx. On its palmar side lie two **proximal sesamoid bones** per digit, in the tendons of the interosseous muscles.
- **Metacarpal pad**: a single, three-lobed pad lying palmar to the metacarpophalangeal joints.

## Clinical relevance

- **Metacarpal fractures**: after trauma and crushing injuries; when one or two bones are involved they are often managed with a splint, while fractures of the central metacarpals or of several bones may need fixation.
- **Polydactyly**: extra digits, mainly on the medial side of the forepaw, inherited; it may come with extra or fused metacarpal bones.
