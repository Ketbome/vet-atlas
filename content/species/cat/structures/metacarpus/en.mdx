## Overview

The metacarpus (*ossa metacarpalia*) is made of **five metacarpal bones**, numbered I (medial) to V (lateral).

The cat is **digitigrade**. It stands on its digits only, with the metacarpus nearly vertical and raised off the ground, which functionally lengthens the limb.

## Key features

- **Metacarpal I**. Short. It carries the first digit, the dewclaw, which does not touch the ground.
- **Metacarpals II to V**. Long and parallel. III and IV are the longest and most central and bear most of the load.
- **Base**. The proximal end. It articulates with the distal carpal bones and with neighbouring metacarpals.
- **Head**. Rounded distal end with a sagittal ridge, articulating with the proximal phalanx. On its palmar side lie two **proximal sesamoid bones** per digit, in the tendons of the interosseous muscles.
- **Metacarpal pad**. A single, three-lobed pad, palmar to the metacarpophalangeal joints.

## Clinical relevance

### Metacarpal fractures

They follow trauma and crushing injuries.

- When one or two bones are involved, they are often managed with a splint.
- Fractures of the central metacarpals or of several bones may need fixation.

### Polydactyly

Inherited extra digits, mainly on the medial side of the forepaw. It may come with extra or fused metacarpal bones.
