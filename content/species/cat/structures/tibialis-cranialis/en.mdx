---
title: Tibialis cranialis
summary: The most cranial muscle of the leg; flexes the tarsus.
keyPoints:
  - It inserts on the medial distal tarsus, on the rudimentary **metatarsal bone I**.
  - It is supplied by the **common fibular nerve**.
  - The **cranial tibial reflex** is elicited by tapping its belly and tests the fibular nerve.
  - In feline **aortic thromboembolism** it becomes firm and painful from ischaemia.
---

## Origin

- proximal end of the tibia, on the cranial border and lateral surface
- head of the fibula
- interosseous membrane

## Insertion

Medial side of the distal tarsus, on the rudimentary metatarsal bone I.

## Action

Flexes the tarsus.

## Innervation

Common fibular or common peroneal nerve (*n. fibularis communis*).

## Clinical relevance

In the neurological examination its belly is tapped to elicit tarsal flexion. This is the **cranial tibial reflex**, which tests the fibular nerve.

In feline aortic thromboembolism, this muscle and the gastrocnemius become firm and painful from ischaemia.
