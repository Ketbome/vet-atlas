## Overview

The humerus is the bone of the arm. In the cat it is long, slender and relatively straight compared with the dog's.

- Proximally it forms the **shoulder** with the glenoid cavity of the scapula.
- Distally it forms the **elbow** with the radius and ulna.

## Proximal end

- **Head** (*caput humeri*). Convex articular surface facing caudally, larger than the glenoid cavity.
- **Greater tubercle** (*tuberculum majus*). Lateral. The supraspinatus and infraspinatus insert on it. It is palpable and forms the "point of the shoulder".
- **Lesser tubercle** (*tuberculum minus*). Medial, where the subscapularis inserts.
- **Intertubercular groove**. Between the two tubercles. It carries the tendon of origin of the biceps brachii.

## Shaft

- **Deltoid tuberosity** and **crest of the humerus**. On the lateral and cranial surfaces. The deltoideus and the pectoral muscles insert on them.
- **Groove for the brachialis**. It spirals along the lateral surface. It holds the brachialis muscle and the radial nerve, which crosses the bone in its distal third.

## Distal end

- **Humeral condyle**. Formed by the trochlea, medial, which articulates with the ulna, and the capitulum, lateral, which articulates with the radius.
- **Olecranon fossa**. Caudal, it receives the olecranon of the ulna when the elbow extends. Unlike the dog, the cat has **no supratrochlear foramen**.
- **Supracondylar foramen** (*foramen supracondylare*). An opening on the medial side of the distal end, proximal to the medial epicondyle, characteristic of the cat. The **median nerve** and the **brachial artery** pass through it.
- **Medial and lateral epicondyles**. Origin of the flexor and extensor muscles of the carpus and digits, respectively.

## Clinical relevance

### Fractures

They are diaphyseal and supracondylar, after trauma.

- In the medial approach to the distal humerus and when placing implants, the median nerve and brachial artery must be protected where they pass through the supracondylar foramen.
- The radial nerve is at risk in diaphyseal fractures.

### Osteosarcoma

The proximal humerus is one of the appendicular sites in the cat.

Feline osteosarcoma metastasises far less often than canine osteosarcoma. Amputation can therefore give long survival.

### Osteoarthritis

The elbow is, with the hip, the joint most affected by degenerative joint disease in older cats.

Signs are often subtle: the cat stops jumping and grooms less.
