title: Sternum

---

## Overview

The sternum forms the floor of the rib cage. In the cat it consists of **eight sternebrae**, joined by intersternebral cartilages that ossify with age. The first is the **manubrium** and the last the **xiphoid process**.

## Key features

- **Manubrium (*manubrium sterni*)**: the first sternebra, elongated and slightly compressed laterally; it projects cranially at the thoracic inlet. The first pair of costal cartilages articulates with it, and the pectoral muscles and the sternocephalicus arise from it.
- **Body (*corpus sterni*)**: sternebrae 2 to 7. The cartilages of pairs 2 to 9 articulate at the **costal notches**, at the level of the intersternebral spaces.
- **Xiphoid process (*processus xiphoideus*)**: the last sternebra, extended by the **xiphoid cartilage**, a flexible plate on which the linea alba inserts.
- **Muscles**: the pectoral muscles arise from the ventral surface; the transversus thoracis from the dorsal surface.

## Clinical relevance

- **Pectus excavatum**: dorsal displacement of the caudal sternebrae in kittens, which narrows the thorax; severe cases are corrected with external splints or surgery while the cartilages are still pliable.
- **Median sternotomy**: approach to the cranial mediastinum (for example to remove a thymoma) and to both hemithoraces.
- **Radiography**: lateral views show the sternebrae and the costal cartilages; mineralisation of these cartilages is a normal finding in older cats.
