---
title: Trapezius
summary: Thin muscular sheet joining the dorsal midline of the neck and thorax to the spine of the scapula.
---

## Overview

The most superficial muscle of the scapular region. Classic feline anatomy texts describe three trapezius muscles: clavotrapezius, acromiotrapezius and spinotrapezius. The clavotrapezius is the cleidocervicalis, which veterinary nomenclature places in the brachiocephalicus. The other two are the parts of the trapezius:

- **Cervical part (*pars cervicalis*)**: the acromiotrapezius, a thin sheet from the dorsal neck and withers to the metacromion and spine of the scapula.
- **Thoracic part (*pars thoracica*)**: the spinotrapezius, from the thoracic spinous processes to the spine of the scapula.

## Origin

Median fibrous raphe of the neck and spinous processes of the thoracic vertebrae.

## Insertion

Spine of the scapula (*spina scapulae*), including the metacromion.

## Action

Elevates and abducts the thoracic limb. The cervical part draws the scapula craniodorsally, the thoracic part caudodorsally.

## Innervation

Dorsal branch of the accessory nerve (*n. accessorius*, cranial nerve XI).

## Clinical relevance

It lies under the skin of the interscapular region, for years the usual site for vaccines and injections in the cat. Injection-site sarcomas here are hard to remove with wide margins, so current guidelines recommend vaccinating in the distal limbs or the tail.
