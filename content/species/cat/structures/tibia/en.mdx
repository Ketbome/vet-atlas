## Overview

The tibia is the medial, weight-bearing bone of the leg. The fibula runs alongside it laterally.

In the cat it is long and slender. Its shaft has little muscle on the medial surface, where it can be felt directly under the skin.

## Proximal end

- **Medial and lateral condyles**. Articular surfaces for the femoral condyles, separated by the **intercondylar eminence**. The menisci rest on them.
- **Tibial tuberosity**. Insertion of the patellar ligament. It continues distally as the **cranial border**.
- **Articular surface for the fibula**. On the caudolateral side of the lateral condyle.

## Shaft

It is triangular proximally and more cylindrical distally.

The medial surface is subcutaneous. The lateral surface is covered by the tibialis cranialis muscle.

## Distal end

- **Cochlea of the tibia** (*cochlea tibiae*). Grooved articular surface that fits the trochlea of the talus.
- **Medial malleolus**. Palpable projection on the medial side of the tarsus. It is the origin of the medial collateral ligament.

## Clinical relevance

- **Tibial fractures**. Common in cats. Because the medial surface has hardly any muscle cover, many are open. They are usually treated with plates or external fixators.
- **Cranial cruciate ligament rupture**. In the cat it usually affects overweight animals, often without clear trauma. It frequently comes with meniscal injury.
- **Meniscal ossicle**. A small mineralisation in the medial meniscus is a normal finding on feline stifle radiographs. It must not be mistaken for a fragment.
- **Patellar luxation**. May come with rotation of the tibial tuberosity.
- **Osteosarcoma**. The proximal tibia is one of its appendicular sites in the cat.
