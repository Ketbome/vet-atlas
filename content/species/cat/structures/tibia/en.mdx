title: Tibia
summary: Main bone of the leg. It articulates with the femur at the stifle and with the talus at the tarsus, and carries almost all the weight of the segment.

## Overview

The tibia is the medial, weight-bearing bone of the leg; the fibula runs alongside it laterally. In the cat it is long and slender, with a shaft covered by little muscle on its medial surface, where it can be felt directly under the skin.

## Proximal end

- **Medial and lateral condyles**: articular surfaces for the femoral condyles, separated by the **intercondylar eminence**; the **menisci** rest on them.
- **Tibial tuberosity**: insertion of the patellar ligament; it continues distally as the **cranial border**.
- **Articular surface for the fibula**: on the caudolateral side of the lateral condyle.

## Shaft

Triangular proximally and more cylindrical distally. Its medial surface is subcutaneous; the lateral surface is covered by the tibialis cranialis muscle.

## Distal end

- **Cochlea of the tibia (*cochlea tibiae*)**: grooved articular surface that fits the trochlea of the talus.
- **Medial malleolus**: palpable projection on the medial side of the tarsus, origin of the medial collateral ligament.

## Clinical relevance

- **Tibial fractures**: common in cats; because the medial surface has hardly any muscle cover, many are open. They are usually treated with plates or external fixators.
- **Cranial cruciate ligament rupture**: in the cat it usually affects overweight animals, often without clear trauma, and frequently comes with meniscal injury.
- **Meniscal ossicle**: a small mineralisation in the medial meniscus is a normal finding on feline stifle radiographs and must not be mistaken for a fragment.
- **Patellar luxation**: may come with rotation of the tibial tuberosity.
- **Osteosarcoma**: the proximal tibia is one of the appendicular sites in the cat.
