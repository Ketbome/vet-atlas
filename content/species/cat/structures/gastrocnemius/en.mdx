---
title: Gastrocnemius
summary: Calf muscle that extends the tarsus through the common calcanean tendon.
---

## Overview

It has a lateral and a medial head. Unlike the dog, the cat has a soleus (*m. soleus*), which arises from the head of the fibula and joins the gastrocnemius tendon. Their tendons form the main part of the common calcanean tendon.

## Origin

Lateral and medial supracondylar tuberosities of the femur.

## Insertion

Calcaneal tuber (*tuber calcanei*).

## Action

Extends the tarsus and flexes the stifle.

## Innervation

Tibial nerve (*n. tibialis*).

## Clinical relevance

- In feline aortic thromboembolism, almost always secondary to cardiomyopathy, a thrombus lodges at the aortic trifurcation. The pelvic limbs become paretic, cold and pulseless at the femoral artery, with pale or cyanotic pads; the gastrocnemius and cranial tibial muscles become firm and painful from ischaemic neuromyopathy. Reperfusion can cause hyperkalaemia.
- Diabetic neuropathy in the cat causes a plantigrade stance: the cat walks with its hocks on the ground.
- Rupture or laceration of the common calcanean tendon also makes the hock drop.
