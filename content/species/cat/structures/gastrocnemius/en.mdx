## Overview

It has a lateral and a medial head.

Unlike the dog, the cat has a soleus (*m. soleus*). It arises from the head of the fibula and joins the gastrocnemius tendon. Their tendons form the main part of the common calcanean tendon.

## Origin

Lateral and medial supracondylar tuberosities of the femur.

## Insertion

Calcaneal tuber (*tuber calcanei*).

## Action

Extends the tarsus and flexes the stifle.

## Innervation

Tibial nerve (*n. tibialis*).

## Clinical relevance

### Feline aortic thromboembolism

It is almost always secondary to cardiomyopathy. A thrombus lodges at the aortic trifurcation.

- The pelvic limbs become paretic, cold and pulseless at the femoral artery, with pale or cyanotic pads.
- The gastrocnemius and cranial tibial muscles become firm and painful from ischaemic neuromyopathy.
- Reperfusion can cause **hyperkalaemia**.

### Plantigrade stance

Diabetic neuropathy in the cat causes a plantigrade stance: the cat walks with its hocks on the ground.

Rupture or laceration of the common calcanean tendon also makes the hock drop.
