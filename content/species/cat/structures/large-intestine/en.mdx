## Parts

- **Caecum**. Short, comma-shaped diverticulum next to the ileocolic orifice, on the right side of the abdomen.
- **Ascending colon**. Very short, on the right.
- **Transverse colon**. Crosses from right to left, caudal to the stomach.
- **Descending colon**. The longest segment. It runs along the left dorsal abdomen to the pelvic inlet.
- **Rectum and anal canal**. In the pelvic canal, ventral to the sacrum and the first caudal vertebrae. The anal sacs open on either side of the anus.

## Clinical relevance

### Megacolon

Chronic dilation of the colon with severe constipation, or obstipation.

- The most common form is **idiopathic**.
- Other causes are a narrowed pelvic canal after malunited pelvic fractures, and sacrocaudal injuries.
- On the lateral radiograph, a ratio of colon diameter to the length of the L5 vertebral body above about **1.5** suggests megacolon.

### Other diseases

- **Colitis**. Large-bowel diarrhoea, with tenesmus, mucus and fresh blood. In young cattery cats, consider *Tritrichomonas blagburni*, formerly *T. foetus*.
- **Constipation**. Common in older cats dehydrated by chronic kidney disease.
- **Neoplasia**. Adenocarcinoma and lymphoma. The ileocolic region is a common site.
