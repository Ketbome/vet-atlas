title: Large intestine
summary: Final part of the digestive tube, from the ileocolic orifice to the anus. In the cat it is short and simple, shaped like a question mark, and made up of a small caecum, the colon and the rectum.
---

## Parts

- **Caecum**: short, comma-shaped diverticulum next to the ileocolic orifice on the right side of the abdomen.
- **Ascending colon**: very short, on the right.
- **Transverse colon**: crosses from right to left caudal to the stomach.
- **Descending colon**: the longest segment; it runs along the left dorsal abdomen to the pelvic inlet.
- **Rectum and anal canal**: in the pelvic canal, ventral to the sacrum and the first caudal vertebrae. The anal sacs open on either side of the anus.

## Clinical relevance

- **Megacolon**: chronic dilation of the colon with severe constipation (obstipation). The most common form is idiopathic; other causes are a narrowed pelvic canal after malunited pelvic fractures and sacrocaudal injuries. On the lateral radiograph, a ratio of colon diameter to the length of the L5 vertebral body above about 1.5 suggests megacolon.
- **Colitis**: large-bowel diarrhoea (tenesmus, mucus, fresh blood). In young cattery cats, *Tritrichomonas blagburni* (formerly *T. foetus*) should be considered.
- **Constipation**: common in older cats dehydrated by chronic kidney disease.
- **Neoplasia**: adenocarcinoma and lymphoma; the ileocolic region is a common site.

## 3D model

The colon comes from the CT of a cat (pixelbeaker model), simplified and registered to the atlas skeleton; the rectum, outside the CT, is modelled through the pelvic canal to the anus (modelled fidelity).
