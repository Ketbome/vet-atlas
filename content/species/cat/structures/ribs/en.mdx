## Overview

The cat has **thirteen pairs of ribs** (*costae*), one for each thoracic vertebra. Each rib has a dorsal bony part and a ventral **costal cartilage**.

- **Sternal**, or true, ribs. The **first nine pairs**. Their cartilage articulates directly with the sternum.
- **Asternal**, or false, ribs. The **last four**. The cartilages of pairs 10 to 12 join the cartilage in front and form the **costal arch**. Pair 13 ends freely in the muscles as a **floating rib**.

## Key features

- **Head** (*caput costae*). Articulates with the costal foveae of two consecutive vertebrae.
- **Neck** and **tubercle**. The tubercle articulates with the transverse process of the vertebra of the same number. In the last ribs, head and tubercle come close together.
- **Body**. Long, curved and narrow. The intercostal vein, artery and nerve run along its caudal border.
- **Angle**. Point of greatest curvature, where the rib changes direction.
- **Costochondral junction**. Between the bony part and the cartilage. Palpable in the live cat.
- **Costal cartilages**. Long and flexible. They make the rib cage of the young cat very elastic.

## Clinical relevance

- **Flail chest**. Fractures of several consecutive ribs in two places, typically from dog bites or road accidents. The free segment moves paradoxically during breathing.
- **Pectus excavatum** and **flat-chested kitten syndrome**. Chest wall deformities of kittens that flatten the thorax dorsoventrally and can impair breathing. Burmese cats are overrepresented in the latter.
- **Thoracocentesis**. Performed in the ventral third of intercostal spaces 7–8, at the cranial border of the rib to avoid the intercostal vessels.
- **Radiography**. Counting ribs helps to locate vertebral lesions and to assess heart size by the number of intercostal spaces it spans.
