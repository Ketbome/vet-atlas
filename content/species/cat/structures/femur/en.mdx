## Overview

The femur is the longest bone of the cat. It is long, slender and fairly straight.

It forms the skeleton of the thigh and transmits the thrust of the pelvic limbs, which in the cat power the jump. It articulates:

- at the **hip**, with the acetabulum
- at the **stifle** (*articulatio genus*), with the tibia and patella

## Proximal end

- **Head** (*caput ossis femoris*). Spherical, sits in the acetabulum. The fovea of the head receives the ligament of the femoral head.
- **Neck** (*collum ossis femoris*). Joins the head to the shaft.
- **Capital physis**. The growth plate between the head and the neck. In cats neutered early it closes later.
- **Greater trochanter**. Lateral and palpable. The gluteal muscles insert on it.
- **Lesser trochanter**. Medial. The iliopsoas inserts on it.
- **Trochanteric fossa**. Between the greater trochanter and the neck. It is the entry point for intramedullary pins and for bone marrow aspiration.

## Shaft

The diaphysis is cylindrical. On its caudal surface the *facies aspera*, bounded by medial and lateral lips, receives the adductor muscles.

## Distal end

- **Trochlea**. Cranial groove in which the patella glides.
- **Medial and lateral condyles**. Caudal articular surfaces in contact with the menisci and tibia.
- **Intercondylar fossa**. Houses the cruciate ligaments.
- **Sesamoid bones**. Two fabellae in the heads of origin of the gastrocnemius and a small sesamoid in the tendon of the popliteus. All show on a lateral stifle radiograph.

## Clinical relevance

- **Femoral capital physeal dysplasia**. Spontaneous separation, without trauma, of the femoral head through the capital physis. It affects young cats, mainly overweight neutered males. It is sometimes bilateral.
- **Fractures**. Common after trauma. Diaphyseal fractures are often comminuted.
- **Hip dysplasia and patellar luxation**. Often found together in the cat. Patellar luxation is linked to a shallow trochlea.
- **Osteosarcoma**. The distal femur is one of the most frequent appendicular sites of feline osteosarcoma, which metastasises less than in the dog.
