## Overview

The cat skull is divided into the **braincase** (*neurocranium*), which protects the brain, and the **facial skeleton** (*viscerocranium*), which forms the nose, the orbits and the mouth. Compared with the dog it is short and rounded, with a brief face and a proportionally large braincase. Breed variation is smaller than in the dog, but **brachycephalic** breeds (Persian, Exotic Shorthair, Himalayan) have a markedly shortened face.

## Key features

- **Orbits**: very large and directed rostrally, giving a wide binocular field of view. The orbital rim is incomplete: the zygomatic process of the frontal bone and the frontal process of the zygomatic bone almost meet, and the **orbital ligament** closes the gap.
- **Zygomatic arches**: wide and strongly curved. With the **external sagittal crest** and the temporal lines they give origin and insertion to the temporalis and masseter, powerful muscles in a predator.
- **Occipital bone**: carries the **foramen magnum** and the **occipital condyles**, which articulate with the atlas.
- **Tympanic bulla (*bulla tympanica*)**: large and rounded. In the cat a **bony septum** (*septum bullae*) divides it into a large ventromedial compartment and a small dorsolateral one, joined by a narrow opening.
- **Maxilla and incisive bone**: hold the upper teeth. The **infraorbital foramen** is the exit of a **very short infraorbital canal**.
- **Upper teeth**: three incisors, one canine, three premolars (P2–P4) and one tiny molar on each side. The **upper fourth premolar** (108/208 in the modified Triadan system) is the upper **carnassial tooth**, which shears like scissors against the lower first molar.
- **Temporomandibular joint**: the mandibular fossa of the temporal bone, limited caudally by the retroarticular process, holds the mandibular condyle and allows almost only hinge movement.

## Clinical relevance

- **High-rise syndrome**: cats falling from several floors strike the chin and face; separation of the mandibular symphysis, fractures of the hard palate and dental injuries are typical.
- **Middle ear**: nasopharyngeal polyps of young cats arise from the middle ear or the auditory tube. Ventral bulla osteotomy must open both compartments; the sympathetic fibres running through the middle ear explain Horner's syndrome after surgery.
- **Dental nerve blocks**: because the infraorbital canal is so short, a needle advanced into it can reach the orbit and puncture the globe.
- **Brachycephaly**: stenotic nares, angled nasolacrimal ducts (epiphora) and dental malocclusions.
- **Dental disease**: tooth resorption and feline chronic gingivostomatitis also affect the teeth and gingiva of the upper jaw.
