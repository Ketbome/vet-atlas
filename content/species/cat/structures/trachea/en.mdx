## Topography

- It begins at the cricoid cartilage.
- It descends along the ventral midline of the neck. The esophagus lies dorsal to it and, in the caudal neck, slightly to the left. The thyroid lobes sit on either side of its first rings.
- It enters the thorax through the cranial thoracic aperture and runs through the cranial mediastinum.
- It divides over the heart base into the right and left principal bronchi.

## Structure

- **Tracheal cartilages**. C-shaped rings of hyaline cartilage, open dorsally.
- **Trachealis muscle**. Joins the ends of the cartilages. In carnivores it attaches to their outer surface.
- **Mucosa**. Ciliated pseudostratified epithelium with goblet cells, part of the mucociliary apparatus.

## Clinical relevance

### Intubation

The feline larynx is prone to **laryngospasm**, so topical lidocaine is usually applied before intubation.

An overinflated endotracheal tube cuff, or moving the intubated cat, can **tear the trachea**. Subcutaneous emphysema and pneumomediastinum appear days after anaesthesia.

### Feline asthma and chronic bronchitis

Tracheal or bronchoalveolar lavage through the endotracheal tube confirms eosinophilic or neutrophilic inflammation.

### Tracheal obstruction

Tracheal collapse is rare in the cat, unlike the dog.

Obstructions are mostly due to:

- intraluminal masses, such as lymphoma or carcinoma
- foreign bodies

### Brachycephalic syndrome

In Persians and other brachycephalic breeds the main problem is stenotic nares and nasal conformation. Tracheal involvement is far less relevant than in the dog.
