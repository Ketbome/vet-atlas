---
title: Sternocephalicus
summary: Long muscle of the ventrolateral neck, from the sternum to the head.
---

## Overview

Classic feline anatomy texts call it sternomastoid. The muscles of both sides arise together at the midline and diverge towards the head, forming a V on the ventral surface of the neck.

## Origin

Manubrium of the sternum.

## Insertion

Mastoid process of the temporal bone and nuchal crest of the occipital bone.

## Action

Draws the head and neck ventrally or to the same side.

## Innervation

Ventral branch of the accessory nerve (*n. accessorius*).

## Clinical relevance

- It forms the ventral border of the jugular groove, used to locate and puncture the external jugular vein, a routine site for drawing blood in the cat.
- When examining a cat for suspected hyperthyroidism, the fingertips are slid along both sides of the trachea, from the larynx towards the thoracic inlet, to detect an enlarged thyroid lobe.
