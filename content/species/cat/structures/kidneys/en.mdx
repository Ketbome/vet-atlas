---
title: Kidneys
summary: Bean-shaped retroperitoneal organs on either side of the aorta and caudal vena cava. In the cat they are relatively large, mobile and easy to palpate, with capsular veins visible on their surface.
---

## Topography

- **Right kidney**: roughly ventral to the transverse processes of L1 to L4; its cranial pole contacts the renal impression of the caudate process of the liver.
- **Left kidney**: somewhat more caudal, roughly L2 to L5.
- Both lie dorsally in the abdomen, outside the peritoneum, wrapped in perirenal fat. They are loosely attached, so they move and can be palpated in most cats.

## Structure

- **Capsular veins**: run under the capsule across the surface of the kidney and converge towards the hilus; they are a characteristic feature of the feline kidney.
- **Cortex and medulla**: the cortex is often pale or yellowish because of lipid in the tubular epithelium. As in the dog, the pyramids merge into a common renal crest.
- **Renal pelvis** and **hilus**, where the renal artery enters and the renal vein and ureter leave.

## Clinical relevance

- **Size**: on a ventrodorsal radiograph each kidney measures about 2 to 3 times the length of the body of L2; on ultrasound, about 3–4.5 cm long.
- **Chronic kidney disease**: very common in older cats; small, irregular kidneys. It is staged with creatinine and SDMA and is associated with hypertension and hypokalaemia.
- **Polycystic kidney disease**: inherited (autosomal dominant, *PKD1* gene) in Persians and related breeds; multiple cysts detectable by ultrasound and a genetic test.
- **Ureteral obstruction**: calcium oxalate ureteroliths are common in cats; the obstructed kidney enlarges while the other one is often already atrophied.
- **Renal lymphoma**: bilateral, often irregular renomegaly.
- **Feline infectious peritonitis**: pyogranulomas on the renal surface.
- **Perinephric pseudocysts**: fluid collecting between the capsule and the parenchyma in older cats.
