## Overview

It has a cervical and a thoracic part. Classic feline anatomy texts call the cervical part levator scapulae and the thoracic part serratus magnus.

It is the main component of the thoracic limb **synsarcosis**. The cat's clavicle does not articulate with the sternum or the scapula, so it does not brace the shoulder. The trunk hangs between the two scapulae, held by these muscles.

## Origin

- Cervical part: transverse processes of the last five cervical vertebrae.
- Thoracic part: first ribs, by digitations.

## Insertion

Serrated face (*facies serrata*), on the dorsal part of the medial surface of the scapula.

## Action

Supports the trunk and moves the scapula.

- The cervical part draws the dorsal angle of the scapula cranially.
- The thoracic part draws it caudally. With the limb fixed, it assists inspiration.

## Innervation

- Cervical part: ventral branches of the cervical nerves.
- Thoracic part: long thoracic nerve (*n. thoracicus longus*).
