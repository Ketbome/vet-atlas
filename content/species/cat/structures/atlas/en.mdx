## Overview

The atlas is the first cervical vertebra. Unlike the others, it has no vertebral body and no spinous process.

It is a ring made of a **dorsal arch** and a **ventral arch**, joined laterally by two masses from which the wings (*alae atlantis*) project. It articulates cranially with the occipital condyles and caudally with the axis.

## Key features

- **Cranial articular foveae**. Two deep cavities that receive the occipital condyles. The atlanto-occipital joint allows flexion and extension of the head, the "yes" movement.
- **Caudal articular foveae**. Flatter surfaces for the axis. The atlantoaxial joint allows rotation of the head, the "no" movement.
- **Fovea of the dens** (*fovea dentis*). On the dorsal surface of the ventral arch, where the dens of the axis rests.
- **Dorsal and ventral tubercles**. Small midline prominences on each arch.
- **Wings of the atlas**. Wide horizontal plates, easily palpable on each side of the neck just caudal to the skull.
- **Foramina**. The lateral vertebral foramen transmits the first cervical nerve. The transverse foramen, at the base of the wing, transmits the vertebral artery.

## Clinical relevance

- **Cerebellomedullary cistern puncture**. Cerebrospinal fluid is collected between the occipital bone and the dorsal arch of the atlas, with the head flexed. The wings and the external occipital protuberance are the landmarks.
- **Atlantoaxial instability**. Much less common than in toy-breed dogs. In cats it is described after trauma and, rarely, with malformations of the dens of the axis.
- **Fractures**. Uncommon, and associated with road accidents and falls.
