## Overview

It has a large part (*m. adductor magnus et brevis*) and a small cranial part (*m. adductor longus*). It lies deep to the gracilis, between the pectineus cranially and the semimembranosus caudally.

## Origin

Pelvic symphysis, through the symphyseal tendon, and the ventral surface of the pubis and ischium.

## Insertion

Caudal surface of the femur, along most of its length.

## Action

Adducts the limb and extends the hip.

## Innervation

Obturator nerve (*n. obturatorius*).

## Clinical relevance

With the pectineus and gracilis it depends on the obturator nerve, which can be injured in pelvic fractures. The limb then tends to slide outward on slippery floors.
