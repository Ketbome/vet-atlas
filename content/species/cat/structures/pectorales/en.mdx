---
title: Pectoral muscles
summary: Muscles of the ventral thorax joining the sternum to the thoracic limb; adductors of the limb.
---

## Overview

Veterinary nomenclature distinguishes two groups, the superficial pectorals (*mm. pectorales superficiales*) and the deep pectoral (*m. pectoralis profundus*). Classic feline anatomy texts describe four muscles in the cat, from superficial to deep:

- **Pectoantebrachialis (*m. pectoantebrachialis*)**: a thin band from the manubrium to the antebrachial fascia.
- **Pectoralis major**: from the cranial sternum to the humerus.
- **Pectoralis minor**: deep to the former and, despite its name, larger in the cat.
- **Xiphihumeralis (*m. xiphihumeralis*)**: the most caudal, from the xiphoid process to the humerus.

The first two correspond to the superficial pectorals and the last two to the deep pectoral.

## Origin

- Superficial pectorals: manubrium and first sternebrae.
- Deep pectoral: body of the sternum and xiphoid process.

## Insertion

- Superficial pectorals: cranial surface of the humerus; the pectoantebrachialis continues into the antebrachial fascia.
- Deep pectoral: proximal humerus.

## Action

Adduct the thoracic limb. The deep pectoral also draws the limb caudally and, with the limb planted, pulls the trunk cranially.

## Innervation

Cranial and caudal pectoral nerves (*nn. pectorales*) from the brachial plexus.
