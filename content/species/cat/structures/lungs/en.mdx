## Topography

Each lung fills its pleural cavity, from the pleural cupula at the level of the first rib to the diaphragm. The mediastinum between them holds the heart, trachea and esophagus.

The feline mediastinum is thin and often incomplete. Pneumothorax and pleural effusions are therefore usually bilateral.

Caudal to the basal border of the lungs, the costodiaphragmatic recess of the pleura contains no lung.

## Lobes

- **Left lung**. Cranial lobe, with cranial and caudal parts, and caudal lobe.
- **Right lung**. Cranial, middle, caudal and accessory lobes. The accessory lobe sits in the mediastinal recess, around the caudal vena cava.

## Clinical relevance

### Feline asthma

Eosinophilic inflammation of the lower airways with bronchoconstriction.

Radiographs show:

- a bronchial pattern
- hyperinflation with a flattened diaphragm
- sometimes, collapse of the right middle lobe

### Pleural effusion

Common feline causes:

- heart failure
- pyothorax
- feline infectious peritonitis
- mediastinal lymphoma

Thoracocentesis is done at about the **7th–8th intercostal space**.

### Other diseases

- **Heart failure**. In the cat pulmonary oedema has a patchy distribution and often comes with pleural effusion.
- **High-rise syndrome**. Falls from height cause pulmonary contusion and pneumothorax.
- **Aspiration pneumonia**. It typically affects the cranioventral lobes.
- **Pulmonary carcinoma**. In the cat it can metastasise to the digits (lung-digit syndrome) and present as lameness.
- **Parasites**. *Aelurostrongylus abstrusus* causes a bronchointerstitial pattern that can be mistaken for asthma.
