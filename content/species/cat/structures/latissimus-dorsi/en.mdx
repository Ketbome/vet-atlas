---
title: Latissimus dorsi
summary: Large triangular muscle of the thoracic wall that draws the thoracic limb caudally.
---

## Overview

A broad, flat muscle caudal to the scapula. Its fibres converge cranioventrally, pass medial to the long head of the triceps and end in a tendon shared with the teres major.

In the cat, the epitrochlearis (*m. tensor fasciae antebrachii*) leaves its deep surface and ventral border and accompanies the triceps to the olecranon.

## Origin

Thoracolumbar fascia, which attaches it to the spinous processes of the caudal thoracic and lumbar vertebrae.

## Insertion

Teres major tuberosity (*tuberositas teres major*) on the medial surface of the humerus.

## Action

Flexes the shoulder and draws the limb caudally. With the limb planted, it pulls the trunk cranially, as when climbing.

## Innervation

Thoracodorsal nerve (*n. thoracodorsalis*).

## Clinical relevance

- Feline injection-site sarcoma on the lateral thoracic wall or in the interscapular region can infiltrate this muscle, which then has to be partly removed.
- Its size and constant vascular pedicle make it a useful muscle flap for reconstructing defects of the thoracic wall, including those left by resection of these sarcomas.
