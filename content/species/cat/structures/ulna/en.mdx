## Overview

The ulna is the caudal bone of the forearm.

It is longer than the radius, because its proximal end projects behind the elbow as the **olecranon**.

In the cat it keeps a complete, well-developed body down to the carpus. The radius turns around it in pronation and supination.

## Features

- **Olecranon and olecranon tuberosity**. The lever of the elbow, where the triceps brachii and anconeus insert. It forms the palpable point of the elbow.
- **Trochlear notch** (*incisura trochlearis*). Half-moon articular surface that grips the humeral trochlea.
- **Anconeal process**. The proximal end of the trochlear notch. It fits into the olecranon fossa of the humerus when the elbow is extended.
- **Medial and lateral coronoid processes**. The distal ends of the trochlear notch, which also support the humerus.
- **Radial notch**. Articulates with the articular circumference of the head of the radius.
- **Head of the ulna and styloid process**. At the lateral distal end. They articulate with the radius and with the ulnar and accessory carpal bones.

## Clinical relevance

- **Olecranon fractures**. The pull of the triceps distracts the fragments. They are repaired with pins and a tension-band wire.
- **Radius and ulna fractures** after trauma.
- **Elbow osteoarthritis**. Very common in older cats. Osteophytes form on the anconeal process and the margins of the trochlear notch.
