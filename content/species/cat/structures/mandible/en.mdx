## Overview

The cat mandible is made of two **hemimandibles** joined rostrally by the **mandibular symphysis**, a fibrous joint that never fuses. Each half has a horizontal **body**, holding the teeth, and a vertical **ramus**, where the masticatory muscles insert and which articulates with the skull. It is short and sturdy, built for a strong bite and for cutting meat rather than grinding.

## Key features

- **Body (*corpus mandibulae*)**: holds three incisors, the canine, two premolars (P3 and P4, 307–308 and 407–408 in the modified Triadan system) and one molar. The permanent dental formula of the cat is 2 × (I 3/3, C 1/1, P 3/2, M 1/1) = 30 teeth.
- **Lower first molar (309/409)**: the lower **carnassial tooth**; its cutting edge works against the upper fourth premolar.
- **Mental foramina**: on the lateral surface of the body, exit of the mental nerve.
- **Mandibular foramen**: on the medial surface of the ramus, entry of the inferior alveolar nerve (mandibular nerve block).
- **Coronoid process**: tall and wide, insertion of the temporalis, lodged in the temporal fossa.
- **Masseteric fossa**: deep depression on the lateral surface of the ramus for the masseter.
- **Condylar process**: elongated transversely; it fits in the mandibular fossa of the temporal bone and restricts movement almost to a hinge, with very little side-to-side shift.
- **Angular process**: caudal projection where the masseter and medial pterygoid insert.

## Clinical relevance

- **Symphyseal separation**: the most common mandibular injury of the cat, typical of high-rise falls and road accidents. It is stabilised with a cerclage wire around both hemimandibles.
- **Mandibular fractures**: of the body and ramus, and pathological fractures in bone weakened by periodontal disease.
- **Tooth resorption**: very common in adult cats; the mandibular third premolars (307/407) are the most frequently affected teeth. Diagnosis needs intraoral dental radiographs.
- **Feline chronic gingivostomatitis**: severe inflammation of the gingiva and the caudal oral mucosa; it often requires multiple extractions.
- **Squamous cell carcinoma**: the most common malignant oral tumour of the cat; it invades and destroys mandibular bone.
