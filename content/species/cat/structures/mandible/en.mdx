## Overview

The cat mandible is made of two **hemimandibles** joined rostrally by the **mandibular symphysis**, a fibrous joint that never fuses.

Each half has two parts:

- a horizontal **body**, holding the teeth
- a vertical **ramus**, where the masticatory muscles insert and which articulates with the skull

It is short and sturdy, built for a strong bite and for cutting meat rather than grinding.

## Key features

- **Body** (*corpus mandibulae*). It holds three incisors, the canine, two premolars and one molar. The premolars are P3 and P4, 307–308 and 407–408 in the modified Triadan system. The permanent dental formula of the cat is 2 × (I 3/3, C 1/1, P 3/2, M 1/1) = 30 teeth.
- **Lower first molar** (309/409). The lower **carnassial tooth**. Its cutting edge works against the upper fourth premolar.
- **Mental foramina**. On the lateral surface of the body. The mental nerve exits through them.
- **Mandibular foramen**. On the medial surface of the ramus. The inferior alveolar nerve enters here, and it is the site of the mandibular nerve block.
- **Coronoid process**. Tall and wide, lodged in the temporal fossa. The temporalis inserts on it.
- **Masseteric fossa**. Deep depression on the lateral surface of the ramus for the masseter.
- **Condylar process**. Elongated transversely. It fits in the mandibular fossa of the temporal bone and restricts movement almost to a hinge, with very little side-to-side shift.
- **Angular process**. Caudal projection where the masseter and medial pterygoid insert.

## Clinical relevance

### Symphyseal separation

It is the most common mandibular injury of the cat, typical of high-rise falls and road accidents. It is stabilised with a cerclage wire around both hemimandibles.

### Other conditions

- **Mandibular fractures**. Of the body and ramus. Pathological fractures also occur in bone weakened by periodontal disease.
- **Tooth resorption**. Very common in adult cats. The mandibular third premolars (307/407) are the most frequently affected teeth. Diagnosis needs intraoral dental radiographs.
- **Feline chronic gingivostomatitis**. Severe inflammation of the gingiva and the caudal oral mucosa. It often requires multiple extractions.
- **Squamous cell carcinoma**. The most common malignant oral tumour of the cat. It invades and destroys mandibular bone.
