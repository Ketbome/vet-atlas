## Overview

Its four heads converge on the patella:

- rectus femoris
- vastus lateralis
- vastus medialis
- vastus intermedius

The model shows the first three. The vastus intermedius lies deep, on the cranial surface of the femur.

## Origin

- **Rectus femoris** (*m. rectus femoris*). Body of the ilium, cranial to the acetabulum. The only head that crosses the hip.
- **Vasti** (*m. vastus lateralis, medialis, intermedius*). Proximal femur.

## Insertion

Tibial tuberosity, through the patella and the patellar ligament.

## Action

Extends the stifle. The rectus femoris also flexes the hip.

## Innervation

Femoral nerve (*n. femoralis*).

## Clinical relevance

- **Patellar reflex**. Tapping the patellar ligament extends the stifle. It tests the femoral nerve and spinal cord segments L4–L6.
- **Intramuscular injections**. Its craniolateral part is, together with the lumbar epaxial muscles, the preferred site in the cat.
- **Medial patellar luxation**. The pull of the quadriceps is displaced medially. In the cat it is often bilateral and is described in breeds such as the Devon Rex and the Abyssinian.
