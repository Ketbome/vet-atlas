---
title: Quadriceps femoris
summary: Large extensor of the stifle covering the cranial surface and sides of the femur.
---

## Overview

Its four heads converge on the patella: rectus femoris, vastus lateralis, vastus medialis and vastus intermedius. The model shows the first three; the vastus intermedius lies deep, on the cranial surface of the femur.

## Origin

- **Rectus femoris (*m. rectus femoris*)**: body of the ilium, cranial to the acetabulum. The only head that crosses the hip.
- **Vasti (*m. vastus lateralis, medialis, intermedius*)**: proximal femur.

## Insertion

Tibial tuberosity, through the patella and the patellar ligament.

## Action

Extends the stifle. The rectus femoris also flexes the hip.

## Innervation

Femoral nerve (*n. femoralis*).

## Clinical relevance

- Tapping the patellar ligament extends the stifle: the patellar reflex tests the femoral nerve and spinal cord segments L4–L6.
- Its craniolateral part is, together with the lumbar epaxial muscles, the preferred site for intramuscular injections in the cat.
- In medial patellar luxation, which in the cat is often bilateral and is described in breeds such as the Devon Rex and the Abyssinian, the pull of the quadriceps is displaced medially.
