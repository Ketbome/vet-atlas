## Overview

It is convex cranially. Its periphery is muscular and its centre is a tendon (*centrum tendineum*).

It has three openings:

- the aortic hiatus
- the esophageal hiatus
- the caval foramen

## Origin

- **Lumbar part**. Right and left crura, on the bodies of the cranial lumbar vertebrae.
- **Costal part**. Medial surfaces of the last ribs and their cartilages, along the costal arch.
- **Sternal part**. Xiphoid process.

## Insertion

Central tendon.

## Action

Contraction flattens the dome, enlarges the thorax and produces inspiration.

## Innervation

Phrenic nerve (*n. phrenicus*), formed by ventral branches of the caudal cervical nerves.

## Clinical relevance

- **Traumatic diaphragmatic hernia**. Occurs in the cat after road accidents and falls from height. Liver, small intestine and stomach move into the thorax. Signs may appear weeks after the trauma.
- **Peritoneopericardial diaphragmatic hernia**. Congenital, and the most common pericardial anomaly in the cat. It is often an incidental finding. It is described more often in Persian, Himalayan and long-haired cats.
