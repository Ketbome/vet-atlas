title: Phalanges of the thoracic limb
summary: Bones of the five digits of the forepaw. The distal phalanx carries the claw and, at rest, is held retracted over the middle phalanx by dorsal elastic ligaments.
---

## Overview

The cat's forepaw has **five digits**. **Digit I** (the dewclaw) is short, does not touch the ground and has **two phalanges**; **digits II to V** have **three phalanges**: proximal, middle and distal. The cat bears weight on digits II to V through the digital pads.

## Key features

- **Proximal phalanx (*phalanx proximalis*)**: the longest; articulates with the head of the metacarpal and with the proximal sesamoid bones.
- **Middle phalanx (*phalanx media*)**: shorter and asymmetrical. At rest the distal phalanx folds dorsally alongside it.
- **Distal phalanx (*phalanx distalis*)**: carries the **claw**. It has:
  - **Ungual process (*processus unguicularis*)**: curved and laterally compressed, it fits inside the horny sheath of the claw.
  - **Ungual crest (*crista unguicularis*)**: a bony fold that wraps the base of the claw like a sheath; the germinal tissue that produces the claw lies beneath it.
  - **Flexor tubercle**: on the palmar surface, insertion of the deep digital flexor tendon.
- **Dorsal elastic ligaments**: run from the middle phalanx to the ungual crest of the distal phalanx and hold the claw **retracted** with no muscular effort. When the **deep digital flexor** contracts, the distal phalanx rotates palmarly, the ligaments stretch and the claw comes out (**protraction**).
- **Sesamoid bones**: proximal (two per digit, at the metacarpophalangeal joint) and dorsal.

## Clinical relevance

- **Onychectomy ("declawing")**: it is not a nail trim but the **amputation of the distal phalanx** of each digit. If germinal tissue of the ungual crest is left behind, the claw can regrow abnormally. It causes pain, lameness and retained bone fragments; it is banned in many countries and discouraged by the main veterinary associations.
- **High-rise syndrome**: the distal limbs are among the regions injured, together with the face and thorax.
- **Lung-digit syndrome**: primary lung carcinoma of the cat can metastasise to the distal phalanges, with swelling and bone lysis in one or several digits.
- **Polydactyly**: inherited extra digits, with a higher risk of ingrown claws.
