## Overview

Several long muscles run along the tail, each made of segments that end on the caudal vertebrae by long tendons:

- **Dorsal sacrocaudal muscles** (*mm. sacrocaudales dorsales medialis et lateralis*), the continuation of the epaxial muscles.
- **Ventral sacrocaudal muscles** (*mm. sacrocaudales ventrales medialis et lateralis*).
- **Intertransversarii** (*mm. intertransversarii dorsales et ventrales caudae*), between the transverse processes.

The tail is important to the cat for balance and as a signal of mood.

## Origin and insertion

Sacrum and caudal vertebrae; each segment ends on a vertebra further along the tail.

## Action

The dorsal muscles raise the tail, the ventral ones lower it, and those of one side bend it to that side.

## Innervation

Dorsal and ventral branches of the caudal nerves.

## Clinical relevance

When the tail is caught or pulled, the sacrocaudal region can be fractured or luxated and the nerves of the cauda equina stretched. The tail then hangs limp and is insensitive, and there may be urinary and faecal incontinence.
