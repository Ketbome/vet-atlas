## Overview

The eye (*oculus*) consists of the eyeball (*bulbus oculi*) and its accessory organs: eyelids, conjunctiva, lacrimal apparatus and extrinsic muscles. The cat's eyes are very large relative to the skull and face forward, giving a wide binocular field that helps to judge distance when hunting. The orbit is not closed by bone: the **orbital ligament** completes the ring.

## What is visible from outside

- **Cornea**: transparent and very large; it covers almost the whole visible surface of the eye, so the sclera barely shows.
- **Iris and pupil**: the pupil constricts to a **vertical slit** in bright light and dilates until almost round in the dark. This shape allows a very wide range in the amount of light entering the eye.
- **Eyelids (*palpebrae*)**: skin folds with tarsal glands; the cat has no true eyelashes.
- **Third eyelid (*plica semilunaris conjunctivae*)**: a conjunctival fold at the medial angle with a T-shaped cartilage and a gland that supplies part of the tear film.

## Inside the eye

- **Tapetum lucidum**: a reflective cellular layer of the choroid in the dorsal half of the fundus, yellow-green in colour. It sends light back through the retina and improves vision in dim light; it causes the cat's eyeshine in torchlight or a flash.
- **Retina**: rich in rods, adapted to low-light vision.

## Clinical relevance

- **Feline herpesvirus keratoconjunctivitis (FHV-1)**: causes conjunctivitis and dendritic corneal ulcers, recurring with stress.
- **Corneal ulcer**: confirmed with fluorescein. In brachycephalic cats it can progress to a **corneal sequestrum**, a brown or black necrotic plaque typical of the cat.
- **Entropion**: common at the medial angle of the lower eyelid in Persians.
- **Feline infectious peritonitis**: the dry form causes uveitis and retinal lesions.
- **Hypertensive retinopathy**: systemic hypertension, associated with chronic kidney disease and hyperthyroidism, causes retinal haemorrhage and detachment with sudden blindness and dilated pupils.
- **Taurine-deficiency retinal degeneration**: in cats fed inadequate diets.
- **Bilateral protrusion of the third eyelid**: may signal systemic or gastrointestinal illness; if unilateral, Horner's syndrome.
