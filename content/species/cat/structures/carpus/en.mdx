## Overview

The carpus (*ossa carpi*) is the group of short bones between the radius and ulna proximally and the metacarpal bones distally. In the cat, as in the dog, it has **seven bones** in **two rows**. The carpal joint allows very wide flexion and, together with rotation of the forearm, gives the cat's paw great handling ability.

## Bones

- **Proximal row**:
  - **Radial carpal bone (*os carpi intermedioradiale*)**: the largest, formed by fusion of the radial and intermediate bones; articulates with the radius.
  - **Ulnar carpal bone (*os carpi ulnare*)**: lateral, articulates with the ulna.
  - **Accessory carpal bone (*os carpi accessorium*)**: projects palmarly as a lever; the flexor carpi ulnaris and the extensor carpi ulnaris insert on it.
- **Distal row**: **carpal bones I to IV**, from medial to lateral, which articulate with the metacarpal bones. Bone IV is the largest.

## Associated structures

- **Carpal pad (*torus carpeus*)**: on the palmar surface, over the accessory carpal bone; it does not touch the ground at a normal walk.
- **Carpal vibrissae**: a tuft of long tactile hairs proximal to the carpal pad, characteristic of the cat. They signal contact of prey with the paw.
- **Palmar ligaments** and **palmar fibrocartilage**: resist hyperextension of the carpus.

## Clinical relevance

- **Carpal hyperextension**: rupture of the palmar ligaments after a fall or a jump lets the carpus sink to the ground; it is treated by arthrodesis.
- **Luxations and fractures** of the carpal bones after trauma.
- **Osteoarthritis** in older cats.
