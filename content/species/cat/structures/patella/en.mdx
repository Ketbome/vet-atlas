## Overview

The patella is a sesamoid bone in the tendon of insertion of the **quadriceps femoris**. It glides in the femoral trochlea and increases the lever arm of the quadriceps when the stifle extends. Distally it continues as the **patellar ligament**, which inserts on the tibial tuberosity.

## Key features

- **Base**: the proximal edge, where the quadriceps tendon inserts.
- **Apex**: the distal end, from which the patellar ligament arises.
- **Articular surface**: smooth, in contact with the femoral trochlea.
- **Parapatellar fibrocartilages**: widen the gliding surface on each side of the patella.
- **Medial and lateral retinacula**: fascial bands that keep it centred in the trochlea.

## Clinical relevance

- **Patellar luxation**: in the cat it is almost always **medial** and often bilateral. The Devon Rex and Abyssinian are predisposed, and it frequently coexists with hip dysplasia. Many cats tolerate it with few signs; severe cases cause lameness or a crouched gait and are corrected surgically.
- **Spontaneous patellar fractures**: transverse fatigue fractures without previous trauma in young adult cats; they may be bilateral and associated with retained deciduous teeth (patellar fracture and dental anomaly syndrome).
- **Extensor mechanism**: rupture of the patellar ligament prevents extension of the stifle.
