## Overview

The fibula is the lateral bone of the leg. It is slender and runs the whole length of the tibia, separated from it by the **interosseous space**. It carries virtually no weight; its role is to give attachment to muscles and ligaments and to stabilise the tarsal joint laterally.

## Key features

- **Head (*caput fibulae*)**: the proximal end, articulates with the lateral condyle of the tibia; the lateral collateral ligament of the stifle inserts on it.
- **Body**: thin, joined to the tibia by the interosseous membrane.
- **Lateral malleolus (*malleolus lateralis*)**: the distal end, palpable on the lateral side of the tarsus; it articulates with the talus and gives origin to the lateral collateral ligament of the tarsus.
- **Muscles**: the fibular (peroneal) muscles and the lateral digital extensor arise from it.

## Clinical relevance

- **Fractures**: usually accompany tibial fractures; an isolated fibular fracture rarely needs treatment.
- **Lateral malleolus**: its fractures or rupture of the lateral collateral ligament destabilise the tarsus and need repair.
- **Common fibular nerve**: crosses the lateral surface superficially near the head of the fibula and can be injured by trauma or compression.
