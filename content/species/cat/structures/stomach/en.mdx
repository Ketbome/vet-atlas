## Topography

It rests on the visceral surface of the liver, mostly left of the median plane and within the costal arch.

- The fundus lies dorsal and to the left.
- The body descends ventrally.
- The pyloric part crosses to the right.

In the cat the **pylorus** lies close to the midline, more medial than in the dog. This gives the stomach its J shape on the ventrodorsal view.

The greater curvature carries the greater omentum. The spleen is attached to it by the gastrosplenic ligament.

## Parts

- **Cardia**. Entrance of the esophagus.
- **Fundus** and **body**. The most capacious part, with gastric folds in the mucosa.
- **Pyloric part**. Antrum and pyloric canal, ending at the **pylorus** and the duodenum.

## Clinical relevance

- **Trichobezoars**. Hairballs formed by grooming. Vomiting them frequently is not normal and can point to underlying gastrointestinal disease.
- **Linear foreign bodies**. Threads and ribbons anchored at the pylorus or under the tongue. The base of the tongue should always be checked in a vomiting cat.
- **Gastric lymphoma**. The most common gastric neoplasm of the cat.
- **Chronic vomiting**. In the cat it is often due to small intestinal disease, such as chronic enteropathy or lymphoma, rather than primary gastric disease.
- **Gastric dilatation-volvulus**. Exceptional in the cat, unlike the dog.
