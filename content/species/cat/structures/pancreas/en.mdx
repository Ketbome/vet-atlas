## Topography

- **Right lobe**: in the mesoduodenum, along the descending duodenum.
- **Body**: at the angle between the two lobes, near the pylorus.
- **Left lobe**: in the deep leaf of the greater omentum, caudal to the greater curvature of the stomach, towards the spleen and the left kidney.

## Ducts and islets

- **Main pancreatic duct**: in the cat it joins the bile duct and both open together at the major duodenal papilla.
- **Accessory pancreatic duct**: present in only about 20% of cats; it opens at the minor duodenal papilla. In the dog it is the other way round: the accessory duct is usually the main one.
- **Pancreatic islets**: the endocrine part; beta cells produce insulin.

## Clinical relevance

- **Pancreatitis**: in the cat it is usually chronic with vague signs (lethargy, anorexia, weight loss); vomiting and abdominal pain are less obvious than in the dog. Diagnosis relies on feline pancreas-specific lipase and ultrasound, and it is often part of triaditis with cholangitis and enteropathy.
- **Diabetes mellitus**: resembles human type 2 diabetes, with insulin resistance (obesity, inactivity) and amyloid deposition in the islets. With early glycaemic control many cats go into remission. Diabetic neuropathy causes a typical plantigrade stance.
- **Exocrine pancreatic insufficiency**: in the cat intrinsic factor is produced only by the pancreas, so exocrine insufficiency comes with cobalamin deficiency.
