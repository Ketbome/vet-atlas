## Topography

- **Right lobe**. In the mesoduodenum, along the descending duodenum.
- **Body**. At the angle between the two lobes, near the pylorus.
- **Left lobe**. In the deep leaf of the greater omentum, caudal to the greater curvature of the stomach, towards the spleen and the left kidney.

## Ducts and islets

- **Main pancreatic duct**. In the cat it joins the bile duct and both open together at the major duodenal papilla.
- **Accessory pancreatic duct**. Present in only about 20% of cats. It opens at the minor duodenal papilla. In the dog it is the other way round: the accessory duct is usually the main one.
- **Pancreatic islets**. The endocrine part. Beta cells produce insulin.

## Clinical relevance

### Pancreatitis

In the cat it is usually **chronic**, with vague signs: lethargy, anorexia and weight loss. Vomiting and abdominal pain are less obvious than in the dog.

Diagnosis relies on feline pancreas-specific lipase and ultrasound.

It is often part of triaditis, together with cholangitis and enteropathy.

### Diabetes mellitus

It resembles human type 2 diabetes, with insulin resistance from obesity and inactivity, and amyloid deposition in the islets.

- With early glycaemic control many cats go into **remission**.
- Diabetic neuropathy causes a typical plantigrade stance.

### Exocrine pancreatic insufficiency

In the cat intrinsic factor is produced only by the pancreas. Exocrine insufficiency therefore comes with **cobalamin deficiency**.
