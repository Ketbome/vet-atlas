## Overview

The epaxial muscles form three parallel columns, from lateral to medial:

- **Iliocostalis** (*m. iliocostalis*). From the ilium and lumbar vertebrae to the ribs.
- **Longissimus** (*m. longissimus*). The largest. It runs from the ilium to the skull, between the transverse and spinous processes.
- **Transversospinalis** (*m. transversospinalis*). Short bundles next to the spinous processes: spinalis, semispinalis, multifidus and rotatores.

The cat has no nuchal ligament, so these muscles alone hold up the head.

## Origin and insertion

Ilium, sacrum, vertebral processes and ribs. Each bundle spans one or several segments.

## Action

Extend and stabilise the vertebral column. Acting on one side only, they bend it laterally.

In leaping and galloping, extension of the spine lengthens each stride.

## Innervation

Dorsal branches of the spinal nerves.

## Clinical relevance

- **Intramuscular injection**. The lumbar epaxial muscles are, with the quadriceps, a common injection site in the cat.
- **Injection-site sarcoma**. It can infiltrate these muscles along fascial planes. Surgery needs wide margins, about 5 cm laterally and two fascial planes deep, sometimes including the dorsal spinous processes.
- **Muscle condition**. Their bulk is palpated for muscle condition scoring. Atrophy is an early sign of chronic disease, such as chronic kidney disease or hyperthyroidism, and of sarcopenia in older cats.
- **Ventroflexion of the neck**. The way generalised muscle weakness shows in the cat, for example in hypokalaemic myopathy, thiamine deficiency or myasthenia gravis.
