summary: Extensor of the carpus on the cranial surface of the forearm.
keyPoints:
  - In the cat it is two muscles, **longus and brevis**; in the dog, a single belly.
  - It arises from the **lateral supracondylar crest** of the humerus.
  - The longus ends on metacarpal II and the brevis on **metacarpal III**.
  - It is supplied by the **radial nerve**.
  - Tapping its belly extends the carpus; this is the **extensor carpi radialis reflex**.

## Overview

In the cat it is described as two muscles, the extensor carpi radialis longus and brevis. Each has its own belly and tendon, which cross the dorsal surface of the carpus.

In the dog it forms a single belly with two tendons.

## Origin

Lateral supracondylar crest of the humerus.

## Insertion

- **Extensor carpi radialis longus**. Base of metacarpal bone II.
- **Extensor carpi radialis brevis**. Base of metacarpal bone III.

## Action

Extends the carpus.

## Innervation

Radial nerve (*n. radialis*).

## Clinical relevance

It is used in the neurological examination. Tapping its belly extends the carpus (extensor carpi radialis reflex), which tests the radial nerve.
