## Overview

The tarsus (*ossa tarsi*) is the group of short bones of the hock, between the tibia and fibula proximally and the metatarsal bones distally. The cat has **seven tarsal bones** arranged in several rows. In the normal digitigrade stance the tarsus is raised off the ground and the joint is held at an angle.

## Bones

- **Talus**: proximal row, medial. Its **trochlea** fits the cochlea of the tibia and forms the tarsocrural joint, where almost all tarsal movement takes place.
- **Calcaneus**: proximal row, lateral. Its **tuber** (*tuber calcanei*) forms the point of the hock and receives the **common calcaneal tendon** (gastrocnemius, superficial digital flexor and others), which extends the tarsus. The **sustentaculum tali** supports the talus medially and guides the tendon of the deep digital flexor.
- **Central tarsal bone**: between the proximal and distal rows, on the medial side.
- **Tarsal bones I to IV**: distal row; bone IV is the largest and spans two levels on the lateral side.

## Clinical relevance

- **Plantigrade stance**: resting the tarsus and metatarsus on the ground is a classic sign of **diabetic neuropathy** in the cat; it also appears with injuries of the common calcaneal tendon and the sciatic nerve.
- **High-rise syndrome**: the tarsus is among the limb regions injured.
- **Scottish Fold osteochondrodysplasia**: exostoses on the plantar side of the tarsus and metatarsus, with stiffness, pain and lameness.
- **Fractures, luxations and shearing injuries** of the tarsus after road accidents, often open.
