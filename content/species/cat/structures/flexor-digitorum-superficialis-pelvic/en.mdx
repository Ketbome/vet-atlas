---
title: Flexor digitorum superficialis (pelvic limb)
summary: Flexor of the toes that arises between the heads of the gastrocnemius and caps the point of the hock.
---

## Overview

Classic feline anatomy texts call it plantaris (*m. plantaris*). It arises deep, between the two heads of the gastrocnemius. Its tendon winds around the gastrocnemius tendon, becomes superficial, forms a cap over the calcaneal tuber and continues along the plantar surface of the metatarsus, where it splits into four tendons.

## Origin

Lateral supracondylar tuberosity of the femur.

## Insertion

Middle phalanges of digits II to V.

## Action

Flexes the digits and extends the tarsus.

## Innervation

Tibial nerve (*n. tibialis*).

## Clinical relevance

- It is part of the common calcanean tendon: its laceration or rupture makes the hock drop.
- In feline aortic thromboembolism the caudal leg muscles become firm and painful from ischaemia.
