## Overview

The cat's hind paw normally has four weight-bearing digits, II to V. Digit I is absent or reduced to a vestige.

Each digit has **three phalanges**: proximal, middle and distal. The cat stands on the digits through the digital pads, with the proximal phalanges nearly horizontal.

## Key features

- **Proximal phalanx**. The longest. It articulates with the head of the metatarsal and with the proximal sesamoid bones.
- **Middle phalanx**. Shorter and asymmetrical. At rest, the distal phalanx folds dorsally alongside it.
- **Distal phalanx**. Carries the claw. It has the ungual process, inside the horny sheath; the ungual crest, wrapping the base of the claw; and the flexor tubercle, for the deep digital flexor tendon.
- **Dorsal elastic ligaments**. Hold the claw retracted at rest. Contraction of the deep digital flexor protrudes it.

The mechanism is the same as in the forepaw, although the hind claws are used less for gripping and climbing.

## Clinical relevance

- **Scottish Fold osteochondrodysplasia**. Short, deformed, irregular phalanges, with exostoses and progressive ankylosis of the distal limb joints.
- Digital fractures and luxations after trauma or entrapment.
- **Onychectomy**. Involves amputation of the distal phalanx. It is performed less often on the hind paw, but the consequences are the same.
- Polydactyly, with extra digits, more frequent on the forepaw than on the hind paw.
