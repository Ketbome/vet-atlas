title: Phalanges of the pelvic limb
summary: Bones of the four digits of the hind paw (II to V), each with three phalanges. The distal phalanx carries a retractile claw, as in the forepaw.
---

## Overview

The cat's hind paw normally has **four weight-bearing digits**, **II to V**; digit I is absent or reduced to a vestige. Each digit has **three phalanges**: proximal, middle and distal. The cat stands on the digits through the digital pads, with the proximal phalanges nearly horizontal.

## Key features

- **Proximal phalanx**: the longest; articulates with the head of the metatarsal and with the proximal sesamoid bones.
- **Middle phalanx**: shorter and asymmetrical; at rest the distal phalanx folds dorsally alongside it.
- **Distal phalanx**: carries the **claw**, with the **ungual process** inside the horny sheath, the **ungual crest** wrapping the base of the claw and the **flexor tubercle** for the deep digital flexor tendon.
- **Dorsal elastic ligaments**: hold the claw retracted at rest; contraction of the deep digital flexor protrudes it. The mechanism is the same as in the forepaw, although the hind claws are used less for gripping and climbing.

## Clinical relevance

- **Scottish Fold osteochondrodysplasia**: short, deformed, irregular phalanges, with exostoses and progressive ankylosis of the distal limb joints.
- **Digital fractures and luxations** after trauma or entrapment.
- **Onychectomy**: involves amputation of the distal phalanx; it is performed less often on the hind paw, but the consequences are the same.
- **Polydactyly**: extra digits, more frequent on the forepaw than on the hind paw.
