## Overview

The skin (*cutis*) covers the whole body surface and is continuous with the mucous membranes at the natural openings.

Functions:

- physical, chemical and microbiological barrier
- thermoregulation
- perception of touch, temperature, pain and itch
- storage of water, electrolytes and fat

In the cat it is **thin** and **elastic**. It is very loosely attached to the underlying tissue, especially over the dorsal neck, the "scruff".

## Layers

- **Epidermis**. Keratinised stratified squamous epithelium without blood vessels. In the haired skin of the cat it is thin. It becomes much thicker on the pads and the nasal planum.
- **Dermis**. Connective tissue with collagen and elastic fibres. It holds vessels, nerves, hair follicles, arrector pili muscles and glands.
- **Hypodermis** (*tela subcutanea*). Loose connective tissue with fat, which lets the cat's skin slide widely.

## Hair and glands

### Hair

- **Compound hair follicles**. One primary hair and several secondary hairs leave through the same opening. Grooming with the tongue, which is covered in horny papillae, removes dead hair.
- **Vibrissae** (*pili tactiles*). Long tactile hairs with a venous sinus around the follicle and a rich nerve supply. On the face there are muzzle (whiskers), supraorbital, genal and mandibular vibrissae. On the thoracic limb are the **carpal vibrissae**, on the palmar side of the carpus.

### Glands

- **Sebaceous**. Open into the follicles. They are well developed on the **chin** and lips, the eyelids and the **dorsal tail base**, the tail gland or supracaudal organ. The cat uses their secretion for scent marking when it rubs.
- **Sweat**. Apocrine in all haired skin. **Eccrine only in the pads**, which sweat with heat and stress. The cat does not lose heat by sweating, so panting is a warning sign.
- Cats **lack the circumanal** (hepatoid) glands of the dog.
- The **anal sacs** open on each side of the anus.
- Females have **four pairs of mammary glands**.

## Specialised regions

- **Nasal planum**. Hairless and heavily keratinised.
- **Pads** (*tori*). Digital pads, one metacarpal or metatarsal pad and, on the thoracic limb, a carpal pad.

## Clinical relevance

- **Injection-site sarcoma**. Aggressive mesenchymal tumour arising where vaccines or other injectable drugs were given. Vaccination in the distal limbs or tail rather than the interscapular region is recommended.
- **3-2-1 rule**. Any mass that persists for more than three months, is larger than 2 cm or is still growing one month after injection should be biopsied.
- **Dermatophytosis**. Infection by *Microsporum canis*, common in kittens and long-haired cats such as Persians. It is a zoonosis.
- **Eosinophilic granuloma complex**. Includes the indolent ulcer of the upper lip, the eosinophilic plaque and the eosinophilic granuloma. It often has an allergic basis.
- **Others**:
  - chin acne
  - tail gland hyperplasia ("stud tail") in intact males
  - squamous cell carcinoma on the ears, eyelids and nasal planum of white cats exposed to sun
  - mammary tumours, most of which are malignant
- **Routes of administration**. Subcutaneous injections are given into the loose skin. The site of each vaccine should be recorded.
