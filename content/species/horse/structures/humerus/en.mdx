## Overview

The humerus (*humerus*) is the bone of the arm. It articulates:

- proximally with the scapula, at the **shoulder joint**
- distally with the radius and ulna, at the **elbow joint**

In the horse it is **short and thick**. Its shaft looks twisted because of the spiral groove of the brachialis, and it is almost entirely covered by muscle.

## Proximal end

- **Head** (*caput humeri*). Caudal, articulates with the glenoid cavity.
- **Greater and lesser tubercles**. Lateral and medial, each divided into a cranial and a caudal part. The caudal part of the greater tubercle forms the palpable **point of the shoulder**.
- **Intermediate tubercle** (*tuberculum intermedium*). Unique to the horse, between the other two. It divides the **intertubercular groove** into two channels through which the tendon of origin of the biceps passes, cushioned by the intertubercular bursa (*bursa intertubercularis*).

## Shaft and distal end

- **Deltoid tuberosity** (*tuberositas deltoidea*). Large and palpable on the lateral surface of the shaft. The deltoideus inserts here.
- **Teres major tuberosity**. On the medial surface. The teres major and latissimus dorsi insert here.
- **Brachial groove**. Spirals across the lateral surface and holds the brachialis and the radial nerve.
- **Condyle** (*condylus humeri*). Trochlea-shaped distal articular surface for the radius and ulna.
- **Olecranon fossa**. Caudal and deep. It receives the anconeal process of the ulna when the elbow is extended.
- **Lateral and medial epicondyles**. Origin of the extensors and flexors of the carpus and digits, respectively.

## Clinical relevance

- **Stress fractures**. They affect the neck and shaft of the humerus in young racehorses in training. If missed, they can become complete.
- **Complete fractures**. They follow falls or kicks. The limb takes on a **dropped elbow** posture, also seen in radial nerve paralysis and olecranon fractures.
- **Intertubercular bursitis**. Inflammation or infection of the bursa of the biceps tendon. It causes weight-bearing lameness and pain on pressure over the point of the shoulder.
