## Overview

The humerus (*humerus*) is the bone of the arm. It articulates proximally with the scapula at the **shoulder joint** and distally with the radius and ulna at the **elbow joint**. In the horse it is **short and thick**, with a shaft that looks twisted because of the spiral groove of the brachialis, and it is almost entirely covered by muscle.

## Proximal end

- **Head (*caput humeri*)**: caudal, articulates with the glenoid cavity.
- **Greater and lesser tubercles**: lateral and medial, each divided into a cranial and a caudal part. The caudal part of the greater tubercle forms the palpable **point of the shoulder**.
- **Intermediate tubercle (*tuberculum intermedium*)**: unique to the horse, between the other two; it divides the **intertubercular groove** into two channels through which the tendon of origin of the biceps passes, cushioned by the **intertubercular bursa** (*bursa intertubercularis*).

## Shaft and distal end

- **Deltoid tuberosity (*tuberositas deltoidea*)**: large and palpable on the lateral surface of the shaft; insertion of the deltoideus.
- **Teres major tuberosity**: on the medial surface, insertion of the teres major and latissimus dorsi.
- **Brachial groove**: spirals across the lateral surface and holds the brachialis and the radial nerve.
- **Condyle (*condylus humeri*)**: trochlea-shaped distal articular surface for the radius and ulna.
- **Olecranon fossa**: caudal and deep, receives the anconeal process of the ulna when the elbow is extended.
- **Lateral and medial epicondyles**: origin of the extensors and flexors of the carpus and digits, respectively.

## Clinical relevance

- **Stress fractures**: of the neck and shaft of the humerus in young racehorses in training; if missed they can become complete.
- **Complete fractures**: from falls or kicks; the limb takes on a **dropped elbow** posture, also seen in radial nerve paralysis and olecranon fractures.
- **Intertubercular bursitis**: inflammation or infection of the bursa of the biceps tendon, with weight-bearing lameness and pain on pressure over the point of the shoulder.
