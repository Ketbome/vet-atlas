---
title: Infraspinatus
summary: Fills the infraspinous fossa of the scapula; lateral rotator and stabiliser of the shoulder.
---

## Overview

It fills the infraspinous fossa, caudal to the scapular spine, and is partly covered by the deltoideus. Its belly is crossed by tendinous sheets. Its tendon glides over the **infraspinatus bursa**, on the caudal part of the greater tubercle, before inserting.

## Origin

Infraspinous fossa (*fossa infraspinata*) of the scapula and scapular cartilage.

## Insertion

Lateral surface of the caudal part of the greater tubercle of the humerus, distal to the supraspinatus insertion.

## Action

Abducts the arm and rotates it laterally. The horse has no true collateral ligaments at the shoulder: this muscle acts as a contractile **lateral collateral ligament** of the joint.

## Innervation

Suprascapular nerve (*n. suprascapularis*).

## Clinical relevance

**Sweeny** is injury to the suprascapular nerve where it winds around the cranial border of the scapula, from collisions or, in draught horses, from collar pressure. The supraspinatus and infraspinatus waste, the scapular spine stands out and the shoulder shifts laterally when the limb bears weight.
