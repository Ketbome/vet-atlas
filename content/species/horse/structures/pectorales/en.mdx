---
title: Pectoral muscles
summary: Muscles of the ventral thorax joining the sternum to the thoracic limb; adductors of the limb and part of the thoracic sling.
---

## Overview

Veterinary nomenclature distinguishes two groups, the superficial pectorals (*mm. pectorales superficiales*) and the deep pectoral (*m. pectoralis profundus*). In the horse the superficial pectorals have two parts:

- **Descending pectoral (*m. pectoralis descendens*)**: cranial, from the manubrium to the arm. It forms the bulge of the chest seen between the forelimbs.
- **Transverse pectoral (*m. pectoralis transversus*)**: from the sternum to the antebrachial fascia.

The **deep pectoral** (ascending pectoral) is much larger and spreads over the ventral thorax as far as the abdominal wall. The horse also has a prominent **subclavius** (*m. subclavius*), which climbs along the cranial border of the supraspinatus. Together with the serratus ventralis, the pectorals form the **thoracic sling**, which suspends the trunk between the thoracic limbs.

Between the descending pectoral and the brachiocephalicus lies the **lateral pectoral groove**, in which the **cephalic vein** runs up, visible under the skin.

## Origin

- Descending pectoral: manubrium of the sternum.
- Transverse pectoral: ventral surface of the sternum.
- Deep pectoral: ventral surface of the sternum, costal cartilages and fascia of the ventral abdominal wall.

## Insertion

- Descending pectoral: crest of the humerus, together with the cleidobrachialis.
- Transverse pectoral: medial antebrachial fascia.
- Deep pectoral: lesser and greater tubercles of the humerus.

## Action

Adduct the thoracic limb and support the trunk between the limbs. The deep pectoral also draws the limb caudally and, with the limb planted, pulls the trunk cranially.

## Innervation

Cranial and caudal pectoral nerves (*nn. pectorales*) from the brachial plexus.

## Clinical relevance

- The pectoral mass is one of the horse's **intramuscular injection sites**; local reactions can make limb movement painful.
- Infection with *Corynebacterium pseudotuberculosis* causes large **abscesses in the pectoral region** ("pigeon fever"), mainly in dry areas and in autumn.
