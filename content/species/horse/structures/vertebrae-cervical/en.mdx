## Overview

The horse has **seven cervical vertebrae**. Its vertebral formula is C7, T18, L6, S5, Cd15–21. The typical ones, the third to seventh, are described here.

They are **long and robust**. They form the base of the long, mobile neck that the horse uses for grazing and for balancing the body in motion.

The cervical spine describes an S-shaped curve: it descends from the poll and rises again at the thoracic inlet.

## Key features

- **Body** (*corpus vertebrae*). Long, with a convex cranial head and a concave caudal fossa, joined by intervertebral discs. Ventrally it carries a marked ventral crest.
- **Spinous processes**. **Almost absent** from C3 to C6, reduced to a low ridge. The nuchal ligament takes over their role in supporting the head. C7 has a short but distinct spinous process.
- **Transverse processes**. Divided into two tubercles and pierced by the **transverse foramen**, through which the vertebral artery and vein run. On C6 they carry a broad **ventral lamina**, sometimes palpable at the base of the neck.
- **Articular processes**. Large, with wide, almost horizontal surfaces that allow great lateral and dorsoventral mobility.
- **C7**. Lacks a transverse foramen and has a caudal costal fovea for the head of the first rib.

## Clinical relevance

- **Cervical stenotic myelopathy** (wobbler syndrome). Compression of the spinal cord by stenosis of the vertebral canal, vertebral malformation or osteoarthritis of the articular processes. It causes **symmetrical ataxia**, worse in the pelvic limbs. It is most common in fast-growing young males, such as Thoroughbreds and sport horses. In the youngest animals compression is usually dynamic at the cranial segments (C3–C5); in somewhat older ones, static at the caudal segments (C5–C7).
- **Osteoarthritis of the articular processes**. In adult horses, mainly between C5 and C7. It causes neck stiffness and pain and can compress nerve roots.
- **Intramuscular injections in the neck**. Given in the triangle bounded by the nuchal ligament dorsally, the cervical vertebrae ventrally and the scapula caudally. This avoids the vertebrae and the ligament.
