## Overview

It is the most cranial and bulkiest muscle of the equine forearm.

Its belly narrows in the distal half of the forearm. Its tendon crosses the dorsal surface of the carpus inside a synovial sheath.

In the proximal forearm it receives the **lacertus fibrosus** of the biceps brachii.

## Origin

Lateral supracondylar crest of the humerus and radial fossa.

## Insertion

Tuberosity of the third metacarpal bone (*tuberositas ossis metacarpalis III*), on the dorsal surface of the proximal cannon bone.

## Action

Extends the carpus and flexes the elbow.

## Innervation

Radial nerve (*n. radialis*).

## Clinical relevance

- **Stay apparatus**. Through the lacertus fibrosus it is part of the stay apparatus of the thoracic limb. The tension of the biceps, passed on to this tendon, helps keep the carpus extended without muscular effort.
- **Wounds over the dorsal carpus**. They can open its tendon sheath or the joint capsule. Any wound in this area calls for ruling out contamination of synovial structures.
