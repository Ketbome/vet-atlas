title: Metacarpus
summary: The cannon of the thoracic limb, formed by the large third metacarpal bone, flanked by the two vestigial second and fourth metacarpal bones or splint bones.
---

## Overview

The horse stands on **a single digit**, the **third**, and its metacarpus is dominated by the **third metacarpal bone** (*os metacarpale III*) or **cannon bone**, a long, straight and very strong bone, oval in cross-section. On its palmar side lie the **second** (medial) and **fourth** (lateral) **metacarpal bones**, called **splint bones**, which do not reach the fetlock. The first and fifth metacarpal bones are absent.

## Key features

- **Proximal end of Mc III**: a broad articular surface for the distal row of the carpus. On the dorsomedial side, the **metacarpal tuberosity** receives the extensor carpi radialis.
- **Mc II and Mc IV**: proximally they articulate with the carpus and bear some weight; their shaft tapers and ends in a small **button** at the distal third of the cannon. An **interosseous ligament** binds them to Mc III.
- **Palmar surface**: between the two splint bones lies a channel filled by the **suspensory ligament** (*m. interosseus medius*), which arises from the proximal palmar surface of Mc III.
- **Distal end**: a single **condyle** with a **sagittal ridge** that articulates with the proximal phalanx and the two proximal sesamoid bones at the **fetlock**.
- **Dorsal surface**: subcutaneous, crossed by the extensor tendons; the cannon can be palpated along its whole length.

## Clinical relevance

- **Splints**: exostoses between a splint bone and the cannon, from inflammation of the interosseous ligament or the periosteum. They are most common on **Mc II** of the thoracic limb in young horses, and usually stop hurting once they have consolidated.
- **Splint bone fractures**: of the distal end, often associated with suspensory desmitis; proximal ones are usually caused by kicks.
- **Dorsal metacarpal disease (bucked shins)**: pain and inflammation of the dorsal surface of Mc III in **young racehorses** early in training, from insufficient adaptation of the bone to load.
- **Condylar fractures**: in racehorses, mainly of the **lateral condyle**; they start at the articular surface of the fetlock and are repaired with screws.
