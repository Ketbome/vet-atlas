## Overview

The horse stands on **a single digit**, the third. Its metacarpus is dominated by the **third metacarpal bone** (*os metacarpale III*) or **cannon bone**, a long, straight and very strong bone, oval in cross-section.

On its palmar side lie the second (medial) and fourth (lateral) metacarpal bones. These are the **splint bones**, and they do not reach the fetlock.

The first and fifth metacarpal bones are absent.

## Key features

- **Proximal end of Mc III**. A broad articular surface for the distal row of the carpus. On the dorsomedial side, the **metacarpal tuberosity** receives the extensor carpi radialis.
- **Mc II and Mc IV**. Proximally they articulate with the carpus and bear some weight. Their shaft tapers and ends in a small button at the distal third of the cannon. An interosseous ligament binds them to Mc III.
- **Palmar surface**. Between the two splint bones lies a channel filled by the **suspensory ligament** (*m. interosseus medius*). It arises from the proximal palmar surface of Mc III.
- **Distal end**. A single condyle with a sagittal ridge. It articulates at the **fetlock** with the proximal phalanx and the two proximal sesamoid bones.
- **Dorsal surface**. Subcutaneous and crossed by the extensor tendons. The cannon can be palpated along its whole length.

## Clinical relevance

- **Splints**. Exostoses between a splint bone and the cannon, from inflammation of the interosseous ligament or the periosteum. They are most common on **Mc II** of the thoracic limb in young horses. They usually stop hurting once they have consolidated.
- **Splint bone fractures**. Those of the distal end are often associated with suspensory desmitis. Proximal ones are usually caused by kicks.
- **Dorsal metacarpal disease** (bucked shins). Pain and inflammation of the dorsal surface of Mc III in **young racehorses** early in training. It comes from insufficient adaptation of the bone to load.
- **Condylar fractures**. In racehorses, mainly of the **lateral condyle**. They start at the articular surface of the fetlock and are repaired with screws.
