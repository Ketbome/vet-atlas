## Overview

They are two fibrous bands that tie the digital flexor tendons to the skeleton of the thoracic limb.

They fix the tendon below the muscle belly. The horse's weight then tenses the tendon without the muscle having to contract.

- **Accessory ligament of the superficial digital flexor**. Also called the radial or superior check ligament.
- **Accessory ligament of the deep digital flexor**. Also called the carpal or inferior check ligament. It continues the **palmar carpal ligament**.

In the pelvic limb the superficial digital flexor has no accessory ligament, and that of the deep flexor is thin or absent.

## Origin

- Radial check ligament: caudomedial surface of the distal radius.
- Carpal check ligament: palmar carpal ligament.

## Insertion

- Radial check ligament: superficial digital flexor tendon, proximal to the carpus.
- Carpal check ligament: deep digital flexor tendon, at about mid-metacarpus.

## Action

They passively transfer the load on the fetlock to the radius and carpus, and limit stretching of the muscle bellies.

## Innervation

They have no motor innervation, as they are ligaments.

## Clinical relevance

- **Stay apparatus**. With the digital flexors and the interosseus medius they form the stay apparatus of the thoracic limb. It lets the horse stand for hours with little muscular effort.
- **Desmitis of the accessory ligament of the deep digital flexor**. It causes swelling at the back of the proximal third of the cannon. It is more common in ponies and older horses. It is diagnosed by ultrasonography.
- **Desmotomy**. Desmotomy of either ligament is used to treat flexural deformities in foals. The distal one for club foot; the proximal one for some fetlock deformities.
