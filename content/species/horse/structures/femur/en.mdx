## Overview

The femur (*os femoris*) forms the skeleton of the thigh. It articulates:

- at the hip, with the acetabulum
- at the **stifle** (*articulatio genus*), the anatomical knee, with the tibia and patella

It is wrapped in large muscle masses, so only the greater trochanter and some distal landmarks can be palpated.

Unlike cattle and the dog, the horse has a **third trochanter**.

## Proximal end

- **Head** (*caput ossis femoris*). Hemispherical, with a deep medial **notch** rather than a simple fovea. The ligament of the femoral head and the **accessory ligament**, unique to the horse, attach in it.
- **Neck** (*collum ossis femoris*). Short and poorly defined.
- **Greater trochanter** (*trochanter major*). Divided into a cranial and a higher caudal part. The caudal part rises above the head and receives the gluteus medius.
- **Lesser trochanter** (*trochanter minor*). Medial. The iliopsoas inserts on it.
- **Third trochanter** (*trochanter tertius*). A prominent crest on the lateral surface, at the junction of the proximal and middle thirds. The **gluteus superficialis** inserts on it.

## Shaft and distal end

- **Supracondylar fossa** (*fossa supracondylaris*). Deep, on the caudal surface. Origin of the superficial digital flexor.
- **Trochlea** (*trochlea ossis femoris*). The **medial ridge** is much wider and higher than the lateral. It has a proximal thickening, the **tubercle of the medial ridge**, over which the patella hooks to lock the stifle in the stay apparatus.
- **Medial and lateral condyles**. Caudal articular surfaces in contact with the menisci and tibia. Between them lies the intercondylar fossa.
- **Extensor fossa**. Between the lateral condyle and the trochlea. Origin of the long digital extensor and the peroneus tertius.

## Clinical relevance

- **Subchondral bone cysts**. Lesions of the **medial femoral condyle** in young horses that cause pelvic limb lameness. Treated arthroscopically or by injection into the lesion.
- **Osteochondrosis of the stifle**. Mainly affects the lateral trochlear ridge. It causes distension of the femoropatellar joint in foals and young horses.
- **Femoral fractures**. Mainly in **foals**, from trauma. In the adult the body weight makes repair very difficult.
- **Coxofemoral luxation**. Rare in the horse thanks to the accessory ligament. When it occurs, it usually comes with rupture of that ligament or acetabular fractures.
