## Overview

The eye (*oculus*) consists of the eyeball (*bulbus oculi*) and its accessory organs: eyelids, conjunctiva, lacrimal apparatus and extrinsic muscles. In the horse the eyeball is **very large** and sits on the side of the head in an **orbit closed by bone**. This position gives a very wide visual field, covering almost all its surroundings, with a narrow zone of binocular vision in front and **blind spots** just in front of the muzzle and behind the body.

## What is visible from outside

- **Cornea (*cornea*)**: transparent anterior part of the fibrous tunic, horizontally oval.
- **Pupil**: **horizontal** and elliptical in bright light.
- **Iridic granules (*granula iridica*)**: pigmented masses on the pupillary margin, mainly the upper one; they shade the pupil.
- **Sclera (*sclera*)**: white part of the fibrous tunic; little of it shows except at the lateral angle or in poorly pigmented eyes.
- **Eyelids (*palpebrae*)**: with lashes on the upper lid and long tactile hairs around them.
- **Third eyelid (*plica semilunaris conjunctivae*)**: a conjunctival fold with a cartilage at the medial angle; it slides across the cornea when the globe is retracted.
- **Nasal opening of the nasolacrimal duct**: visible on the floor of the nasal vestibule, near the junction of skin and mucosa; the duct can be flushed through it.

## Clinical relevance

- **Corneal ulcer**: common because of the eye's prominent position; confirmed with fluorescein. In the horse it can become infected by **fungi** and worsen quickly.
- **Equine recurrent uveitis**: repeated episodes of inflammation of the uvea; it is the **most common cause of blindness** in the horse. It has been associated with *Leptospira* infection.
- **Squamous cell carcinoma**: the most common ocular tumour; it affects the third eyelid, eyelids and limbus, mainly in horses with little pigment around the eye.
- **Entropion in foals**: common in weak or dehydrated newborns; the lower lid margin rolls inwards and the hair irritates the cornea.
- **Examination**: blocking the **auriculopalpebral nerve** stops the horse from squeezing its eyelids shut and makes examining and treating the eye easier.
