---
title: Brachiocephalicus
summary: Long muscle joining the arm to the head that forms the dorsal border of the jugular groove; it is the main protractor of the thoracic limb.
---

## Overview

The horse has no clavicle. Instead, cranial to the shoulder joint, the muscle is crossed by a **clavicular intersection** (*intersectio clavicularis*), a faint tendinous sheet that divides it into two muscles:

- **Cleidobrachialis (*m. cleidobrachialis*)**: from the clavicular intersection to the arm.
- **Cleidocephalicus (*m. cleidocephalicus*)**: from the clavicular intersection to the head. In the horse it has only a **mastoid part** (cleidomastoideus); there is no cleido-occipitalis.

In the equine neck the brachiocephalicus is partly fused with the omotransversarius, which lies deep to it.

## Origin and insertion

- The cleidobrachialis runs from the clavicular intersection to the crest of the humerus, distal to the deltoid tuberosity, and to the brachial fascia.
- The cleidomastoideus runs from the clavicular intersection to the mastoid process of the temporal bone.

## Action

It is the main muscle that advances the thoracic limb (shoulder extension). With the limb fixed, it draws the head and neck ventrally and laterally.

## Innervation

Accessory nerve and ventral branches of the cervical nerves for the cleidomastoideus; axillary nerve for the cleidobrachialis.

## Clinical relevance

- It forms the **dorsal border of the jugular groove**. The external jugular vein, in this groove, is the usual route for blood sampling, intravenous medication and catheter placement in the horse.
- Its action sets the length of the cranial phase of the forelimb stride. A shortened cranial phase can reflect pain in the neck or shoulder region, as well as distal lameness.
