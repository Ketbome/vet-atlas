## Overview

The horse has no clavicle. Cranial to the shoulder joint, the muscle is crossed by a **clavicular intersection** (*intersectio clavicularis*), a faint tendinous sheet. It divides the muscle into two:

- **Cleidobrachialis** (*m. cleidobrachialis*). Runs from the clavicular intersection to the arm.
- **Cleidocephalicus** (*m. cleidocephalicus*). Runs from the clavicular intersection to the head. In the horse it has only a **mastoid part** (cleidomastoideus); there is no cleido-occipitalis.

In the neck, the brachiocephalicus is partly fused with the omotransversarius, which lies deep to it.

## Origin and insertion

- The cleidobrachialis runs from the clavicular intersection to the crest of the humerus, distal to the deltoid tuberosity, and to the brachial fascia.
- The cleidomastoideus runs from the clavicular intersection to the mastoid process of the temporal bone.

## Action

- It is the main muscle that advances the thoracic limb, by extending the shoulder.
- With the limb fixed, it draws the head and neck ventrally and laterally.

## Innervation

- Cleidomastoideus: accessory nerve and ventral branches of the cervical nerves.
- Cleidobrachialis: axillary nerve.

## Clinical relevance

### Jugular groove

It forms the **dorsal border of the jugular groove**. The external jugular vein, in this groove, is the usual route for blood sampling, intravenous medication and catheter placement in the horse.

### Stride length

Its action sets the length of the cranial phase of the forelimb stride. A shortened cranial phase can reflect pain in the neck or shoulder region, as well as distal lameness.
