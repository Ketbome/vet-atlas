---
title: Gastrocnemius
summary: Calf muscle that extends the hock through the common calcanean tendon.
---

## Overview

It has a lateral and a medial head, between which the superficial digital flexor arises. Its bellies contain much tendinous tissue and continue, at mid-crus, into a strong tendon. The horse also has a small **soleus** (*m. soleus*) that joins the gastrocnemius tendon. The **common calcanean tendon** ("Achilles tendon") is made up of the gastrocnemius tendon, the superficial digital flexor tendon and the tarsal tendons of the biceps femoris and semitendinosus.

## Origin

Lateral and medial supracondylar tuberosities of the femur.

## Insertion

Calcaneal tuber (*tuber calcanei*).

## Action

Extends the hock and flexes the stifle. It is the main driver of the hock during propulsion.

## Innervation

Tibial nerve (*n. tibialis*).

## Clinical relevance

- **Rupture of the gastrocnemius** or its tendon is uncommon but is described after falls, violent effort or a rough recovery from anaesthesia: the hock drops and flexes when the limb bears weight.
- The **tibial nerve** runs between the two heads of the gastrocnemius and on the caudal crus; it is blocked proximal to the hock in diagnostic anaesthesia of the pelvic limb.
