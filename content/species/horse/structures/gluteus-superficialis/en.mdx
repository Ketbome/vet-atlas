---
title: Gluteus superficialis
summary: V-shaped croup muscle that in the horse is independent and ends on the third trochanter of the femur.
---

## Overview

Unlike in ruminants, in the horse the superficial gluteal is a **separate muscle**, independent of the biceps femoris. It is fairly thin and V-shaped, with two heads: a cranial head from the coxal tuber, joined to the tensor fasciae latae, and a caudal head from the gluteal fascia. Both converge on a tendon that ends on the **third trochanter** (*trochanter tertius*), a prominence on the lateral surface of the femur that is typical of the horse.

## Origin

Coxal tuber (*tuber coxae*) and gluteal fascia.

## Insertion

Third trochanter of the femur (*trochanter tertius*).

## Action

Flexes the hip, abducts the pelvic limb and tenses the fascia lata.

## Innervation

Cranial and caudal gluteal nerves (*nn. glutei*).
