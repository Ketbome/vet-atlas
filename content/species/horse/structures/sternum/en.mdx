---
title: Sternum
summary: Unpaired bone that closes the thorax ventrally and receives the cartilages of the eight sternal ribs. In the horse it is keel-shaped, with a manubrial cartilage projecting cranially.
---

## Overview

The sternum (*sternum*) forms the floor of the rib cage. It is made up of a series of **sternebrae** joined by cartilage, which ossify only partly with age. In the horse it is shaped like a **boat's keel**: compressed laterally in its cranial part, with a prominent ventral crest, and flattened dorsoventrally in its caudal part, where it widens.

## Key features

- **Manubrium (*manubrium sterni*)**: the first sternebra, extended cranially by the **manubrial cartilage** (*cartilago manubrii*), which projects between the pectoral muscles and is palpable at the point of the breast.
- **Sternal crest**: a cartilaginous ventral edge running along the cranial part of the bone, giving attachment to the pectoral muscles.
- **Body (*corpus sterni*)**: formed by the middle sternebrae; on its sides, the **costal notches** receive the cartilages of the sternal ribs.
- **Xiphoid process and xiphoid cartilage**: the caudal end, broad and spade-shaped; it gives attachment to the diaphragm and the linea alba.

## Clinical relevance

- **Bone marrow aspiration**: the sternebrae, on the ventral midline between the thoracic limbs, are a usual site for collecting bone marrow in the adult horse.
- **Pectoral abscesses**: in the region ventral to the sternum, infection with *Corynebacterium pseudotuberculosis* produces large abscesses in the pectoral muscles.
- **Traumatic injuries**: the sternum and its cartilages can be injured in horses that hit fences or obstacles with their chest.
