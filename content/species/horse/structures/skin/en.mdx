## Overview

The skin (*cutis*) covers the whole surface of the body and is continuous with the mucous membranes at the natural openings.

It acts as a physical, chemical and microbiological barrier. It takes part in thermoregulation and in the perception of touch, temperature, pain and itch.

In the horse it is relatively **thin** and very sensitive. Beneath it, the **cutaneous trunci muscle** lets the animal twitch the skin to drive off insects.

## Layers

- **Epidermis**. Keratinised stratified squamous epithelium without vessels. It thickens on the nostrils, lips and the skin of the distal limbs.
- **Dermis**. Dense connective tissue with collagen and elastic fibres. It holds vessels, nerves, hair follicles, arrector pili muscles and glands.
- **Hypodermis** (*tela subcutanea*). Loose connective tissue with fat. Almost absent on the distal limbs, where the skin lies directly on tendons and bone.

## Hair and glands

- **Coat**. Simple follicles, one hair per opening. The winter coat is long and dense and is shed in spring.
- **Long hairs**. Thick, long hairs of the mane on the neck, the forelock on the forehead and the tail. Draught horses also have tufts of feather on the palmar and plantar fetlocks.
- **Tactile hairs**. Long and stiff, around the nostrils, lips and eyes.
- **Sweat glands**. Apocrine and very abundant. The horse is one of the few domestic animals that loses most of its heat by **sweating**. Its sweat contains a protein, **latherin**, which makes it foamy and helps it wet the coat.
- **Sebaceous glands**. Open into the follicles and lubricate hair and skin.

## Special structures

- **Chestnuts** (*castanea*). Horny plates on the medial forearm, proximal to the carpus, and on the medial tarsus.
- **Ergots** (*calcar metacarpeum* and *metatarseum*). Small horny projections on the palmar or plantar side of the fetlock.
- **Hoof**. A modified skin structure described separately.

## Clinical relevance

- **Equine sarcoid**. The most common skin tumour of the horse, associated with bovine papillomavirus types 1 and 2. It is locally aggressive and often recurs after removal.
- **Melanoma**. Very common in older grey horses, at the tail base, perineum and parotid region.
- **Insect bite hypersensitivity** (sweet itch). Hypersensitivity to biting midges (*Culicoides*), with intense itching of the mane and tail base.
- **Dermatophilosis and pastern dermatitis**. Crusts and exudate on the back after prolonged rain, or on the distal limbs in wet, muddy conditions.
- **Dermatophytosis** (ringworm). Caused by *Trichophyton equinum* and *Microsporum*. It produces circular areas of hair loss, often in the girth and saddle area. It is contagious and can spread to people.
- **Anhidrosis**. Loss of the ability to sweat in hot, humid climates, with a risk of heat stroke.
