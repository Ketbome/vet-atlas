---
title: Common digital extensor
summary: Digital extensor on the craniolateral forearm; its tendon runs down the cannon to the third phalanx.
---

## Overview

Its belly lies lateral to the extensor carpi radialis. The tendon crosses the dorsal carpus in a synovial sheath and runs down the dorsal surface of the third metacarpal bone. Since the horse has a single functional digit, it reaches the third phalanx undivided. At the level of the first phalanx it receives, on each side, the **extensor branches of the interosseus medius** (suspensory ligament).

## Origin

Lateral epicondyle of the humerus and adjacent parts of the radius and ulna.

## Insertion

Extensor process of the third phalanx (*processus extensorius*), with minor attachments on the dorsal surface of the first and second phalanges.

## Action

Extends the joints of the digit and the carpus.

## Innervation

Radial nerve (*n. radialis*).

## Clinical relevance

- With radial nerve paralysis the horse cannot extend the digit: it stands with the fetlock knuckled forward.
- **Rupture of the common digital extensor tendon** occurs in newborn foals, often together with flexural deformities: there is a swelling on the dorsolateral carpus and the foal tends to knuckle at the fetlock.
