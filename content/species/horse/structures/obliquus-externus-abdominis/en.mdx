---
title: External abdominal oblique
summary: The most superficial muscle of the lateral abdominal wall; its fibres run caudoventrally.
---

## Overview

It has a costal part and a lumbar part. Its fleshy part covers the lateral abdominal wall and the caudoventral thorax; ventrally it continues as a broad aponeurosis that reaches the linea alba. The model shows the fleshy part. In the horse, the outer surface of the muscle is reinforced by the **tunica flava** (*tunica flava abdominis*), a sheet of yellow elastic tissue that helps carry the weight of the abdominal viscera.

Caudally its aponeurosis splits into an **abdominal tendon** and a **pelvic tendon**; the **superficial inguinal ring** lies between them.

## Origin

- Costal part: lateral surfaces of the caudal ribs, by slips that interdigitate with those of the serratus ventralis.
- Lumbar part: last rib and thoracolumbar fascia.

## Insertion

Through its aponeurosis: linea alba, prepubic tendon, coxal tuber and inguinal ligament.

## Action

Compresses the abdomen (expiration, defecation, urination, parturition) and flexes the trunk to the same side.

## Innervation

Ventral branches of the caudal thoracic and cranial lumbar nerves.

## Clinical relevance

- **Inguinal hernia** in stallions passes through the inguinal canal, whose outer opening lies in this aponeurosis. It can strangulate a loop of small intestine and cause acute colic that needs surgery.
- Colic surgery is almost always done through the ventral midline, but some standing procedures, such as laparoscopy or ovariectomy, go through the **flank**, across the oblique and transverse abdominal muscles.
