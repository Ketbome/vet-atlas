## Overview

It has a costal part and a lumbar part.

Its fleshy part covers the lateral abdominal wall and the caudoventral thorax. Ventrally it continues as a broad aponeurosis that reaches the linea alba. The model shows the fleshy part.

In the horse, the outer surface of the muscle is reinforced by the **tunica flava** (*tunica flava abdominis*), a sheet of yellow elastic tissue that helps carry the weight of the abdominal viscera.

Caudally, the aponeurosis splits into an abdominal tendon and a pelvic tendon. The **superficial inguinal ring** lies between them.

## Origin

- Costal part: lateral surfaces of the caudal ribs, by slips that interdigitate with those of the serratus ventralis.
- Lumbar part: last rib and thoracolumbar fascia.

## Insertion

Through its aponeurosis: linea alba, prepubic tendon, coxal tuber and inguinal ligament.

## Action

Compresses the abdomen (expiration, defecation, urination, parturition) and flexes the trunk to the same side.

## Innervation

Ventral branches of the caudal thoracic and cranial lumbar nerves.

## Clinical relevance

- **Inguinal hernia**. In stallions it passes through the inguinal canal, whose outer opening lies in this aponeurosis. It can strangulate a loop of small intestine and cause acute colic that needs surgery.
- **Flank approach**. Colic surgery is almost always done through the ventral midline. Some standing procedures, such as laparoscopy or ovariectomy, go through the flank, across the oblique and transverse abdominal muscles.
