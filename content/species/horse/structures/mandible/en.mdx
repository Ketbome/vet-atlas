---
title: Mandible
summary: The bone of the lower jaw, formed by two halves fused at the symphysis. In the horse it has a wide interdental space where the bit rests and a vascular notch where the pulse is taken.
---

## Overview

The mandible (*mandibula*) is the only movable bone of the skull. It is made of two halves that fuse at the **mandibular symphysis** during the first months of life, so in the adult it is a single bone. Each half has an **incisive part**, which holds the incisors and the canine, a **body** carrying the cheek teeth and a vertical **ramus** that articulates with the skull. In the horse the ramus is tall and the **mandibular angle** very wide, for the attachment of the powerful masseter.

## Key features

- **Interalveolar margin (*margo interalveolaris*)**: the **interdental space** (the "bars") between the canine or incisors and the first cheek tooth. It is covered only by gum and is where the **bit** rests.
- **Notch for the facial vessels (*incisura vasorum facialium*)**: on the ventral border, rostral to the angle; the facial artery and vein and the parotid duct cross it onto the face. The horse's **pulse is taken** here.
- **Mental foramen (*foramen mentale*)**: on the lateral surface of the incisive part; exit of the mental nerve.
- **Mandibular foramen (*foramen mandibulae*)**: on the medial surface of the ramus; entry of the inferior alveolar nerve into the mandibular canal.
- **Coronoid process**: tall and narrow; it rises into the temporal fossa and receives the temporalis.
- **Condylar process**: articulates with the temporal bone at the **temporomandibular joint**, which has an articular disc and allows the wide side-to-side movements of chewing.

## Clinical relevance

- **Injuries to the bars**: a poorly fitted bit or rough use of the reins can injure the gum and the bone of the interdental space.
- **Fractures of the incisive part**: common when the horse bites a fixed object and pulls back; they usually have a good prognosis with wire fixation.
- **Eruption bumps**: in young horses, firm swellings on the ventral border below the erupting premolars; they normally resolve on their own.
- **Nerve blocks**: the mental and inferior alveolar nerves are anaesthetised for dental procedures.
