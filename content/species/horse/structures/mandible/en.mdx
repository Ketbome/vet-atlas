## Overview

The mandible (*mandibula*) is the only movable bone of the skull. Its two halves fuse at the **mandibular symphysis** during the first months of life. In the adult it is a single bone.

Each half has:

- an incisive part, which holds the incisors and the canine
- a body, carrying the cheek teeth
- a vertical ramus, which articulates with the skull

In the horse the ramus is tall and the **mandibular angle** very wide, for the attachment of the powerful masseter.

## Key features

- **Interalveolar margin** (*margo interalveolaris*). The **interdental space**, or "bars", between the canine or incisors and the first cheek tooth. It is covered only by gum. The bit rests here.
- **Notch for the facial vessels** (*incisura vasorum facialium*). On the ventral border, rostral to the angle. The facial artery and vein and the parotid duct cross it onto the face. The horse's **pulse is taken** here.
- **Mental foramen** (*foramen mentale*). On the lateral surface of the incisive part. The mental nerve exits through it.
- **Mandibular foramen** (*foramen mandibulae*). On the medial surface of the ramus. The inferior alveolar nerve enters the mandibular canal through it.
- **Coronoid process**. Tall and narrow. It rises into the temporal fossa and receives the temporalis.
- **Condylar process**. Articulates with the temporal bone at the **temporomandibular joint**. The joint has an articular disc and allows the wide side-to-side movements of chewing.

## Clinical relevance

- **Injuries to the bars**. A poorly fitted bit or rough use of the reins can injure the gum and the bone of the interdental space.
- **Fractures of the incisive part**. Common when the horse bites a fixed object and pulls back. They usually have a good prognosis with wire fixation.
- **Eruption bumps**. Firm swellings on the ventral border in young horses, below the erupting premolars. They normally resolve on their own.
- **Nerve blocks**. The mental and inferior alveolar nerves are anaesthetised for dental procedures.
