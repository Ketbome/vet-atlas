## Overview

The atlas (*atlas*) is the first cervical vertebra. It has no body and no spinous process. It is a **bony ring** formed by a dorsal and a ventral arch, with two large lateral **wings**.

It articulates at two points:

- cranially with the occipital condyles, at the **atlanto-occipital joint**, which allows the nodding movement
- caudally with the axis, at the **atlantoaxial joint**, which allows rotation of the head

## Key features

- **Dorsal arch** (*arcus dorsalis*). It carries a **dorsal tubercle** in place of a spinous process. The nuchal ligament runs over it, separated from the bone by the **cranial nuchal bursa**.
- **Ventral arch** (*arcus ventralis*). It carries a **ventral tubercle** for the longus colli. Its dorsal surface forms the fovea for the dens of the axis.
- **Wings** (*alae atlantis*). Broad and flat, easily palpated caudal to the base of the ear. Ventrally, the guttural pouch reaches the level of the wing.
- **Cranial articular foveae**. Deep and cup-shaped, for the occipital condyles.

### Foramina

The horse has **three on each side**:

- the **lateral vertebral foramen**, which transmits the first cervical nerve
- the **alar foramen**, at the cranial border of the wing
- the **transverse foramen**, in the caudal part of the wing

The dog has an alar notch instead of an alar foramen, and cattle lack the transverse foramen.

## Clinical relevance

- **Occipitoatlantoaxial malformation**. A congenital anomaly, described mainly in **Arabian** horses. The atlas fuses with the occipital bone or takes on an occipital shape. It causes ataxia or an abnormal neck posture in foals.
- **Atlanto-occipital puncture**. Under general anaesthesia or deep sedation, cerebrospinal fluid is collected from the **cisterna magna**, between the occipital bone and the dorsal arch of the atlas.
- **Nuchal bursitis** ("poll evil"). Inflammation or infection of the cranial nuchal bursa over the dorsal arch of the atlas, with swelling and pain at the poll.
