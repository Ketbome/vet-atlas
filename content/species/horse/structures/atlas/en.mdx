## Overview

The atlas (*atlas*) is the first cervical vertebra. It has no body and no spinous process: it is a **bony ring** formed by a dorsal and a ventral arch, with two large lateral **wings**. It articulates cranially with the occipital condyles (**atlanto-occipital joint**, which allows the nodding movement) and caudally with the axis (**atlantoaxial joint**, which allows rotation of the head).

## Key features

- **Dorsal arch (*arcus dorsalis*)**: with a **dorsal tubercle** in place of a spinous process. The nuchal ligament runs over it, separated from the bone by the **cranial nuchal bursa**.
- **Ventral arch (*arcus ventralis*)**: with a **ventral tubercle** for the longus colli; its dorsal surface forms the fovea for the dens of the axis.
- **Wings (*alae atlantis*)**: broad and flat, easily palpated caudal to the base of the ear. Ventrally, the guttural pouch reaches the level of the wing.
- **Foramina**: the horse has **three on each side**: the **lateral vertebral foramen**, which transmits the first cervical nerve; the **alar foramen**, at the cranial border of the wing; and the **transverse foramen**, in the caudal part of the wing. The dog has an alar notch instead of an alar foramen, and cattle lack the transverse foramen.
- **Cranial articular foveae**: deep and cup-shaped, for the occipital condyles.

## Clinical relevance

- **Occipitoatlantoaxial malformation**: a congenital anomaly, described mainly in **Arabian** horses, in which the atlas fuses with the occipital bone or takes on an occipital shape; it causes ataxia or an abnormal neck posture in foals.
- **Atlanto-occipital puncture**: under general anaesthesia or deep sedation, cerebrospinal fluid is collected from the **cisterna magna**, between the occipital bone and the dorsal arch of the atlas.
- **Nuchal bursitis ("poll evil")**: inflammation or infection of the cranial nuchal bursa over the dorsal arch of the atlas, with swelling and pain at the poll.
