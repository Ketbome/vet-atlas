## Overview

The equine lungs (*pulmones*) are long and extend caudodorsally under the dome of the diaphragm.

The interlobar fissures are shallow, so each lung looks almost like a single piece. The interlobular septa are incomplete and allow some collateral ventilation.

The **mediastinum** is thin and often incomplete. That is why pleural effusions and pneumothorax are usually bilateral.

## Lobes and bronchi

- **Left lung**. Cranial lobe and caudal lobe, separated by the cardiac notch.
- **Right lung**. Cranial lobe, caudal lobe and **accessory lobe**, which fills the mediastinal recess between the heart and the diaphragm. There is no middle lobe.
- **Bronchi**. The two principal bronchi arise at the tracheal bifurcation and branch into lobar and segmental bronchi.

### Auscultation and percussion area

The caudal border of the lung field runs roughly:

- from the 16th intercostal space, at the level of the tuber coxae
- through the 11th, at the level of the shoulder joint
- to the 6th, at the level of the elbow

## Clinical relevance

- **Equine asthma**. Lower airway inflammation linked to inhaled dust and moulds from hay and bedding. The severe form, formerly "recurrent airway obstruction", causes cough, nostril flaring and an abdominal **heave line** on expiration. The mild form reduces performance.
- **Exercise-induced pulmonary haemorrhage**. Common in racehorses. It mainly affects the caudodorsal regions of the lungs and is diagnosed by endoscopy or bronchoalveolar lavage.
- **Pleuropneumonia**. Associated with long transport with the head tied high. *Streptococcus equi* subsp. *zooepidemicus* and anaerobes cause pneumonia and pleural effusion, which is drained by thoracocentesis.
- **Rhodococcosis**. *Rhodococcus equi* causes pyogranulomatous bronchopneumonia with abscesses in foals a few months old.
- **Examination**. Ultrasound shows effusion, pleural irregularities and peripheral consolidation. Bronchoalveolar lavage gives the lung cytology.
