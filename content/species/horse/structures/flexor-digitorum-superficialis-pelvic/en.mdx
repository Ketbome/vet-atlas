## Overview

It arises between the two heads of the gastrocnemius. In the horse it is **almost entirely tendinous**: it keeps very few muscle fibres and works as a cable between the femur and the digit.

### Course of the tendon

- It winds around the gastrocnemius tendon and becomes superficial.
- It forms a **cap over the calcaneal tuber**, attached to it on both sides.
- It continues down the plantar surface of the metatarsus.
- At the fetlock it forms a sleeve, the *manica flexoria*, for the deep digital flexor tendon.
- It ends on the first and second phalanges.

Unlike in the thoracic limb, it **has no accessory ligament**. Its own attachment to the femur and calcaneus does that job.

## Origin

Supracondylar fossa of the femur (*fossa supracondylaris*).

## Insertion

Distal end of the first phalanx and proximal end of the second phalanx.

## Action

Flexes the digit and extends the hock. Supports the fetlock when standing.

## Innervation

Tibial nerve (*n. tibialis*).

## Clinical relevance

- **Reciprocal apparatus**. It is the caudal part; the peroneus tertius is the cranial one. It couples stifle and hock so that one cannot flex without the other. Together with the patellar lock, it lets the horse sleep standing up.
- **Luxation of the tendon**. The tendon slips off the calcaneal tuber, almost always laterally. It causes swelling at the point of the hock and acute lameness.
- **Subcutaneous calcaneal bursitis** ("capped hock"). It results from knocks against stable or trailer walls.
