---
title: Semimembranosus
summary: Bulky muscle of the caudomedial thigh; in the horse it has a small vertebral head.
---

## Overview

It lies medial to the semitendinosus and forms the medial part of the caudal thigh. In the horse, besides the pelvic head, it has a small **vertebral head** that climbs towards the tail base and adds to the rounded outline of the buttock.

## Origin

- Vertebral head: caudal border of the broad sacrotuberous ligament and caudal fascia.
- Pelvic head: ventral surface of the ischial tuberosity (*tuber ischiadicum*).

## Insertion

Medial epicondyle of the femur.

## Action

Extends the hip and adducts the limb.

## Innervation

Sciatic nerve (*n. ischiadicus*), through the tibial nerve.

## Clinical relevance

- Together with the semitendinosus it is the usual site for **muscle biopsy** in the diagnosis of polysaccharide storage myopathy (PSSM) and other equine myopathies.
- The caudal thigh muscles are used for **intramuscular injections**, especially in foals. An abscess here drains distally more easily than one in the croup.
- **Fibrotic myopathy** can involve it together with the semitendinosus.
