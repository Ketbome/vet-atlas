## Overview

The large colon (*colon ascendens*) is, with the caecum, the horse's great fermentation chamber. It forms two stacked U-shaped loops, one ventral and one dorsal, that fill much of the abdomen. The ventral parts have four taeniae and sacculations; the pelvic flexure and left dorsal colon have a single taenia and a smooth wall; the right dorsal colon has three taeniae. The diameter changes greatly: it narrows sharply at the pelvic flexure and widens in the right dorsal colon.

## Parts

- **Right ventral colon**: leaves the caecocolic orifice and runs cranially along the right abdominal floor.
- **Sternal flexure**: crosses from right to left on the floor, caudal to the xiphoid cartilage.
- **Left ventral colon**: runs caudally along the left floor.
- **Pelvic flexure**: at the pelvic inlet, often slightly to the left; it is the narrowest segment.
- **Left dorsal colon**: runs back cranially, dorsal to the left ventral colon and joined to it by a short mesocolon.
- **Diaphragmatic flexure**: against the diaphragm and the liver.
- **Right dorsal colon**: runs caudally on the right and widens into an **ampulla** before continuing as the transverse colon, cranial to the cranial mesenteric artery.

## Clinical relevance

- **Pelvic flexure impaction**: the most common cause of impaction colic; the firm mass is felt on rectal palpation. It is treated with fluids by tube and intravenously and, if it does not resolve, by pelvic flexure enterotomy.
- **Left dorsal displacement (nephrosplenic entrapment)**: the left colon rises between the spleen and the body wall and hangs over the **nephrosplenic ligament**. It is diagnosed by rectal palpation and ultrasound (the left kidney can no longer be seen) and corrected with phenylephrine and exercise, by rolling the anaesthetised horse or surgically.
- **Large colon volvulus**: typical of postpartum broodmares; very severe pain and rapid deterioration. It is a surgical emergency.
- **Enteroliths and sand**: enteroliths form mainly in the right dorsal and transverse colon and cause obstruction when they move into the small colon; sand accumulates in the ventral colon.
- **Colitis**: *Salmonella*, clostridia and *Neorickettsia risticii* cause severe acute diarrhoea; non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs such as phenylbutazone can cause ulcerative **right dorsal colitis**.
