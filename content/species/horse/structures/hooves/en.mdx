## Overview

The horse stands on a single digit per limb, the **third**, which ends in the **hoof** (*ungula*): a horn capsule that encloses the distal phalanx (coffin bone), the navicular bone, part of the middle phalanx, the ungular cartilages and the **digital cushion**. The **front hooves** are wider and rounder, and the **hind hooves** narrower and more pointed, with a steeper dorsal wall. The hoof spreads load, absorbs impact and, as the heels expand with each step, helps pump blood back from the foot.

## Horn capsule

- **Periople**: band of soft horn at the proximal edge, next to the haired skin; it covers the wall with a thin layer that limits water loss.
- **Wall (*paries*)**: hard horn forming the visible part of the hoof; it is divided into **toe** (dorsal), **quarters** (sides) and **heels**. At the heels it turns inwards to form the **bars**.
- **Sole (*solea*)**: a concave plate of horn on the ground surface, between the wall and the frog.
- **Frog (*cuneus ungulae*)**: a wedge of soft, elastic horn between the bars, with a central groove; it rests on the digital cushion.
- **White line**: the junction between wall and sole; it is the landmark for placing horseshoe nails and a weak point of the capsule.

## Dermis (corium)

Between bone and horn lies the **dermis**, highly vascular and innervated, which produces and nourishes the horn. At the **coronary band** it forms most of the wall, which grows distally and takes **about a year** to be replaced at the toe. Under the wall the dermis is **laminar**: some **six hundred primary laminae**, each carrying many secondary laminae, interlock with the epidermal laminae of the wall and suspend the coffin bone inside the hoof. Under the sole and frog it is papillary.

## Clinical relevance

- **Laminitis**: failure of the laminar bond between coffin bone and wall. Most cases are **endocrine**, linked to **equine metabolic syndrome** and **pituitary pars intermedia dysfunction** with hyperinsulinaemia; it also follows severe systemic disease or overloading of one limb. It is very painful, and in severe cases the coffin bone rotates or sinks.
- **Subsolar abscess**: infection under the sole or wall, a very common cause of **severe acute lameness**; it is located with hoof testers and drained.
- **Thrush**: infection of the central and collateral grooves of the frog, with black, foul-smelling horn, favoured by wet conditions and poor hoof care.
- **White line disease** and **quarter cracks**: separation of the wall from the white line and vertical cracks in the wall, respectively.
- **Shoeing and hoof balance**: trimming every few weeks keeps the hoof–pastern axis aligned and spreads the load.
