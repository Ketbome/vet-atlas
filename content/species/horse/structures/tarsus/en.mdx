## Overview

The tarsus (*ossa tarsi*) forms the **hock**, between the tibia and the metatarsus. The horse has **six tarsal bones** in three rows.

They form four joints:

- the **tarsocrural** joint, between tibia and talus, where practically all flexion and extension happens
- the proximal intertarsal joint
- the distal intertarsal joint
- the tarsometatarsal joint

The last three barely move and absorb load.

Unlike ruminants, the equine talus has only one trochlea, the proximal one.

## Bones

- **Talus** (*talus*). Medial in the proximal row. Its trochlea has two tall ridges, set in a spiral and oblique to the limb axis, that fit the cochlea of the tibia. As a result the foot moves slightly laterally as the hock flexes.
- **Calcaneus** (*calcaneus*). Lateral in the proximal row. Its tuberosity (*tuber calcanei*) forms the **point of the hock** and receives the **common calcaneal tendon**. The superficial digital flexor tendon forms a cap over it. The sustentaculum tali guides the deep digital flexor tendon medially.
- **Central tarsal bone** (*os tarsi centrale*). Middle row, distal to the talus.
- **First and second tarsal bones**. Fused into a single small bone on the plantaromedial side.
- **Third tarsal bone**. Distal row, between the central tarsal bone and Mt III.
- **Fourth tarsal bone**. Lateral. It spans the height of the two distal rows.

## Clinical relevance

- **Bone spavin**. Osteoarthritis of the distal intertarsal and tarsometatarsal joints. It is one of the most common causes of pelvic limb lameness in sport horses. It is diagnosed by intra-articular anaesthesia and radiography.
- **Osteochondrosis of the tarsus**. Found at the distal intermediate ridge of the tibia and the lateral trochlear ridge of the talus. It causes distension of the tarsocrural joint (bog spavin).
- **Subcutaneous calcaneal bursitis** (capped hock). Soft swelling over the point of the hock, almost always from knocks against walls or the trailer.
- **Plantar desmitis** (curb). Thickening of the plantar aspect of the tarsus, distal to the point of the hock. It comes from injury to the long plantar ligament or the neighbouring tissues.
