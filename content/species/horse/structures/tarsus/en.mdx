## Overview

The tarsus (*ossa tarsi*) is the group of short bones of the **hock**, between the tibia proximally and the metatarsus distally. The horse has **six tarsal bones** in three rows. They form four joints: the **tarsocrural**, between tibia and talus, where practically all flexion and extension happens; and the **proximal intertarsal**, **distal intertarsal** and **tarsometatarsal** joints, which barely move and absorb load. Unlike ruminants, the equine talus has only **one trochlea**, the proximal one.

## Bones

- **Talus (*talus*)**: medial in the proximal row. Its **trochlea** has **two tall ridges** set in a spiral, oblique to the limb axis, that fit the cochlea of the tibia; as a result the foot moves slightly laterally as the hock flexes.
- **Calcaneus (*calcaneus*)**: lateral in the proximal row. Its **tuberosity** (*tuber calcanei*) forms the **point of the hock** and receives the **common calcaneal tendon**; the superficial digital flexor tendon forms a cap over it. The **sustentaculum tali** guides the deep digital flexor tendon medially.
- **Central tarsal bone (*os tarsi centrale*)**: middle row, distal to the talus.
- **First and second tarsal bones**: fused into a single small bone on the plantaromedial side.
- **Third tarsal bone**: in the distal row, between the central tarsal bone and Mt III.
- **Fourth tarsal bone**: lateral, spanning the height of the two distal rows.

## Clinical relevance

- **Bone spavin**: **osteoarthritis of the distal intertarsal and tarsometatarsal joints**, one of the most common causes of pelvic limb lameness in sport horses. It is diagnosed by intra-articular anaesthesia and radiography.
- **Osteochondrosis of the tarsus**: at the distal intermediate ridge of the tibia and the **lateral trochlear ridge of the talus**; it causes distension of the tarsocrural joint (bog spavin).
- **Subcutaneous calcaneal bursitis (capped hock)**: soft swelling over the point of the hock, almost always from knocks against walls or the trailer.
- **Plantar desmitis (curb)**: thickening of the plantar aspect of the tarsus, distal to the point of the hock, from injury to the long plantar ligament or the neighbouring tissues.
