## Overview

The equine skull is divided into the **braincase** (*neurocranium*), which protects the brain, and the **facial skeleton** (*viscerocranium*), which forms the nose, the orbits and the mouth. It is very **elongated**: the face takes up most of its length, mainly to hold the long arcades of cheek teeth. On the dorsal surface of the braincase the parietal bones form an **external sagittal crest** that ends caudally at the **nuchal crest**, where the nuchal ligament attaches.

## Orbit and face

- **Orbit**: **closed by bone**. The zygomatic process of the frontal bone joins the zygomatic arch to form a complete postorbital bar, unlike the dog. Dorsal to it, the **supraorbital foramen** transmits the nerve of the same name.
- **Facial crest (*crista facialis*)**: longitudinal ridge on the lateral surface of the maxilla that continues the zygomatic arch rostrally; origin of the masseter. It is a palpable landmark for locating the maxillary sinuses.
- **Infraorbital foramen (*foramen infraorbitale*)**: rostral to the facial crest; the infraorbital nerve emerges here and can be blocked at this point.
- **Nasoincisive notch (*incisura nasoincisiva*)**: wide cleft between the nasal and incisive bones that gives the nostrils their mobility.

## Paranasal sinuses

- **Conchofrontal sinus (*sinus conchofrontalis*)**: formed by the frontal sinus and the dorsal conchal sinus, which communicate widely. It drains into the caudal maxillary sinus.
- **Rostral and caudal maxillary sinuses**: separated by a bony septum. The roots of the last upper cheek teeth project into them.

## Teeth

- **Adult dental formula**: 2 × (I 3/3, C 1/1, P 3–4/3, M 3/3) = 36–44. Canines are usually absent in mares, and the first premolar (**wolf tooth**) is small and inconstant.
- **Hypsodont cheek teeth**: very long crowns that keep erupting throughout life and wear at the occlusal surface.
- **Anisognathism**: the upper arcade is wider than the lower, so **enamel points** form on the buccal edge of the upper teeth and the lingual edge of the lower teeth.
- **Age**: eruption and wear of the incisors are used, with a wide margin of error, to estimate age.

## Clinical relevance

- **Sinusitis**: primary or secondary to **apical infection of the upper cheek teeth** whose roots lie in the maxillary sinuses; it causes **unilateral**, foul-smelling nasal discharge.
- **Dental points**: enamel points injure the mucosa of the cheeks and tongue; they are rasped down periodically.
- **Skull fractures**: a horse that rears and falls over backwards can fracture the base of the skull (basisphenoid bone), with neurological signs and bleeding from the nose or ears.
- **Nerve blocks**: the infraorbital and supraorbital nerves are anaesthetised where they leave their foramina.
