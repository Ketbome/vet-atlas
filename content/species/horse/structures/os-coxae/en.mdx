## Overview

Each hip bone (*os coxae*) results from the fusion of three bones, the **ilium**, the **ischium** and the **pubis**, which meet at the **acetabulum**.

The two hip bones form the **pelvis**:

- ventrally they join each other at the pelvic symphysis
- dorsally they join the sacrum at the sacroiliac joints

In the horse the ilium is broad and slopes craniolaterally. Its landmarks shape the outline of the croup.

## Ilium

- **Wing of the ilium** (*ala ossis ilii*). Broad and laterally concave. The gluteal muscles, very large in the horse, arise from its gluteal surface.
- **Tuber coxae** (*tuber coxae*). The lateral angle of the wing. It forms the visible, palpable **point of the hip**.
- **Tuber sacrale** (*tuber sacrale*). The medial angle of the wing. It rises beside the sacral spinous processes and forms the highest point of the croup.
- **Body**. Narrow, joining the wing to the acetabulum.

## Ischium and pubis

- **Ischial tuberosity** (*tuber ischiadicum*). Forms the **point of the buttock**. It gives origin to the hamstring muscles: biceps femoris, semitendinosus and semimembranosus.
- **Ischial arch**. The caudal border of the two ischia, between the ischial tuberosities.
- **Pubis**. Forms the cranial part of the pelvic floor. The prepubic tendon attaches to its cranial border.
- **Obturator foramen** (*foramen obturatum*). Large, between pubis and ischium. It is closed by muscles and a membrane.

## Acetabulum

A deep cavity that holds the head of the femur. Two ligaments pass through its acetabular notch:

- the ligament of the head of the femur
- the **accessory ligament of the femur**, unique to the horse, which arises from the prepubic tendon and limits abduction of the limb

## Clinical relevance

- **Fracture of the tuber coxae** ("knocked-down hip"). From hitting door frames or falling sideways. The point of the hip is flattened and asymmetric. Most heal with rest, although the asymmetry remains.
- **Stress fractures of the ilium**. In **racehorses**, in the wing or body of the ilium. They cause pelvic limb lameness and can become complete and injure the iliac artery.
- **Pelvic fractures**. From falls. They are examined by **rectal palpation** and ultrasound, looking for crepitus, asymmetry or haematomas.
