---
title: Masseter
summary: Large, multilayered chewing muscle of the cheek; it shapes the lateral surface of the equine head.
---

## Overview

In the horse it is very large and forms the rounded surface of the cheek. It is made up of **several layers** separated by tendinous sheets, grouped into a superficial and a deep part. The equine mandible is narrower than the maxilla (**anisognathia**), so the cheek teeth meet on one side at a time. Chewing is a **side-to-side** grinding movement in which the masseter plays the main role.

## Origin

Facial crest (*crista facialis*) of the maxilla and zygomatic arch.

## Insertion

Lateral surface of the mandibular ramus, as far as its ventral border and the angle.

## Action

Raises the mandible and closes the mouth. Contraction on one side shifts the mandible towards that side and produces the grinding stroke.

## Innervation

Masseteric nerve, a branch of the mandibular nerve (cranial nerve V).

## Clinical relevance

- Because of anisognathia and the lateral stroke, wear leaves **sharp enamel points** on the buccal edge of the upper cheek teeth and the lingual edge of the lower ones, which injure the cheek and tongue. They are rasped ("floated") at routine dental checks.
- The **facial artery**, with the facial vein and parotid duct, crosses the ventral border of the mandible at the rostral edge of the masseter. This is the usual place to take the **pulse** in the horse.
- Atrophy limited to one side suggests a lesion of the mandibular nerve on that side.
