## Overview

In the horse the masseter is very large and forms the rounded surface of the cheek.

It is made up of **several layers** separated by tendinous sheets, grouped into a superficial and a deep part.

The equine mandible is narrower than the maxilla. Because of this **anisognathia**, the cheek teeth meet on one side at a time.

Chewing is a **side-to-side** grinding movement, and the masseter plays the main role in it.

## Origin

Facial crest (*crista facialis*) of the maxilla and zygomatic arch.

## Insertion

Lateral surface of the mandibular ramus, as far as its ventral border and the angle.

## Action

Raises the mandible and closes the mouth. Contraction on one side shifts the mandible towards that side and produces the grinding stroke.

## Innervation

Masseteric nerve, a branch of the mandibular nerve (cranial nerve V).

## Clinical relevance

- **Sharp enamel points**. Because of anisognathia and the lateral stroke, wear leaves points on the buccal edge of the upper cheek teeth and the lingual edge of the lower ones. They injure the cheek and tongue. They are rasped, or "floated", at routine dental checks.
- **Pulse**. The **facial artery**, with the facial vein and parotid duct, crosses the ventral border of the mandible at the rostral edge of the masseter. This is the usual place to take the pulse in the horse.
- **Unilateral atrophy**. Suggests a lesion of the mandibular nerve on that side.
