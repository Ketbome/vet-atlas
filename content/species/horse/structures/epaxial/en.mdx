---
title: Epaxial muscles
summary: Muscle mass dorsal to the transverse processes along the vertebral column; the longissimus carries the saddle and rider.
---

## Overview

The epaxial muscles form three parallel columns, from lateral to medial:

- **Iliocostalis (*m. iliocostalis*)**: from the ilium and lumbar vertebrae to the ribs.
- **Longissimus (*m. longissimus*)**: the largest, from the ilium to the skull, between the transverse and spinous processes. Its lumbar part is covered by a thick aponeurosis on which the cranial extension of the gluteus medius rests.
- **Transversospinalis (*m. transversospinalis*)**: short bundles next to the spinous processes (spinalis, semispinalis, multifidus, rotatores).

The model shows the three columns as a single mass, from the sacrum to the axis. The horse has a well developed **nuchal ligament**, with a funicular and a laminar part, which passively carries the weight of the head and neck.

The equine back, with 18 thoracic and usually 6 lumbar vertebrae, is long and fairly stiff. The epaxials stabilise it during locomotion and bear the **saddle and the rider's weight**, which rest on the longissimus on either side of the spinous processes.

## Origin and insertion

Ilium, sacrum, vertebral processes and ribs; each bundle spans one or several segments.

## Action

Extend and stabilise the vertebral column. Acting on one side only, they bend it laterally. They also raise the head and neck.

## Innervation

Dorsal branches of the spinal nerves.

## Clinical relevance

- **Back pain** is a common complaint in sport horses. Tense epaxial muscles that are painful on palpation can be primary or secondary to other causes: spinous processes that touch or overlap in the saddle region (kissing spines), sacroiliac injury or limb lameness.
- A **poorly fitting saddle** concentrates pressure on the longissimus and causes pain and, over time, muscle atrophy beside the withers and along the back.
- In **exertional rhabdomyolysis** ("tying-up") the loin and croup muscles become hard and painful; blood creatine kinase rises.
