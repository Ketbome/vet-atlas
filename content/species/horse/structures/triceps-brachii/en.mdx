## Overview

It fills the angle between the caudal border of the scapula and the humerus. Its outline shows under the skin behind the arm.

It has three heads, long, lateral and medial, that end on the olecranon. The long head is the bulkiest.

Under its insertion tendon, on the olecranon, there is a subtendinous bursa. The one that swells in **capped elbow** is a different one: the subcutaneous olecranon bursa, at the point of the elbow.

## Origin

- **Long head** (*caput longum*). Caudal border of the scapula. It is the only head that crosses the shoulder.
- **Lateral head** (*caput laterale*). Tricipital line on the lateral surface of the proximal humerus.
- **Medial head** (*caput mediale*). Medial surface of the humerus.

## Insertion

Olecranon tuber (*tuber olecrani*) of the ulna.

## Action

Extends the elbow and holds it fixed when the horse bears weight. The long head also flexes the shoulder.

## Innervation

Radial nerve (*n. radialis*).

## Clinical relevance

- **Radial nerve paralysis**. It follows humeral fractures or compression during anaesthesia in lateral recumbency. The triceps is inactive: the elbow drops ("dropped elbow") and the horse cannot bear weight on the limb.
- **Olecranon fractures**. The pull of the triceps distracts the proximal fragment, so the horse cannot fix the elbow either. They are repaired with a tension band or a plate that neutralises that pull.
- **Capped elbow**. Subcutaneous olecranon bursitis. It occurs when the hoof or shoe strikes the point of the elbow as the horse lies down.
