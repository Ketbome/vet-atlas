---
title: Rectus abdominis
summary: Ventral muscular band on each side of the linea alba, from the sternum to the pubis.
---

## Overview

It is wrapped in the rectus sheath, formed by the aponeuroses of the oblique and transverse abdominal muscles. The two recti are separated by the **linea alba**. It is crossed by numerous transverse **tendinous intersections**. Caudally it ends in the **prepubic tendon**, which anchors it to the pubis together with the aponeuroses of the obliques.

## Origin

Ventral surface of the sternum and costal cartilages.

## Insertion

Pecten of the pubis, through the prepubic tendon.

## Action

Compresses the abdomen and flexes the vertebral column.

## Innervation

Ventral branches of the caudal thoracic and cranial lumbar nerves.

## Clinical relevance

- **Ventral midline laparotomy**, with the horse in dorsal recumbency, is the standard approach for colic surgery: it passes through the linea alba between the two recti and allows much of the intestine to be exteriorised. Its best known complications are wound infection and **incisional hernia**.
- **Prepubic tendon rupture** occurs mainly in mares in late pregnancy, often of heavy breeds or with twins or hydrops of the fetal membranes: the abdomen drops, ventral oedema appears and the udder is displaced cranially.
- **Umbilical hernia** in foals occurs through the umbilical ring, in the linea alba.
