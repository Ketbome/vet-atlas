## Overview

It is the most superficial muscle of the scapular region. It has two parts, separated by an aponeurotic strip over the scapular spine:

- **Cervical part** (*pars cervicalis*). From the nuchal ligament to the scapular spine. Its fibres run caudoventrally.
- **Thoracic part** (*pars thoracica*). From the spinous processes of the cranial thoracic vertebrae, which form the **withers**, to the scapular spine. Its fibres run cranioventrally.

## Origin

Funicular part of the nuchal ligament (cervical part) and supraspinous ligament of the cranial thoracic vertebrae (thoracic part).

## Insertion

Tuber of the scapular spine (*tuber spinae scapulae*).

## Action

Raises and abducts the thoracic limb. The cervical part draws the scapula craniodorsally and the thoracic part caudodorsally.

## Innervation

Dorsal branch of the accessory nerve (*n. accessorius*), cranial nerve XI.

## Clinical relevance

- **Saddle fit**. The thoracic part lies under the front of the saddle. A saddle that bears on the withers or pinches the scapular region causes pain. Over time it atrophies the muscle, seen as a hollow behind the scapula.
- **Fistulous withers**. Deep to the trapezius, over the spinous processes of the withers, lies the **supraspinous bursa**. Its infection has been linked to *Brucella abortus* and *Actinomyces bovis*. It causes a swelling that ends up draining through sinus tracts.
