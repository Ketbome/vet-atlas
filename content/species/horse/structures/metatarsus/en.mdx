title: Metatarsus
summary: The cannon of the pelvic limb, formed by the third metatarsal bone, longer and rounder than that of the thoracic limb, and the two vestigial second and fourth metatarsal bones.
---

## Overview

As in the thoracic limb, the equine metatarsus is dominated by the **third metatarsal bone** (*os metatarsale III*) or **cannon bone**, which carries all the weight, flanked by the vestigial **second** (medial) and **fourth** (lateral) **metatarsal bones**. Mt III is **longer** than Mc III and **rounder in cross-section**. The first and fifth metatarsal bones are absent.

## Key features

- **Proximal end**: articulates with the third tarsal bone and, through the splint bones, with the first-and-second and fourth tarsal bones at the **tarsometatarsal joint**.
- **Mt II and Mt IV**: taper distally and end in a small button. The **lateral one (Mt IV)** is longer and thicker than the medial one.
- **Dorsolateral groove**: between Mt III and Mt IV runs the **dorsal metatarsal artery III**, the main artery of the distal pelvic limb, whose pulse is palpable here.
- **Plantar surface**: holds the **suspensory ligament** (*m. interosseus medius*) between the two splint bones, arising from the proximal part of Mt III.
- **Distal end**: a condyle with a **sagittal ridge** that articulates with the proximal phalanx and the proximal sesamoid bones at the fetlock.

## Clinical relevance

- **Proximal suspensory desmitis**: common in the pelvic limbs of sport horses; the ligament is enclosed between the splint bones and the fascia, and the prognosis with conservative treatment is worse than in the thoracic limb.
- **Fractures of Mt IV**: mainly from **kicks**, as it is exposed on the lateral side.
- **Bone sequestra**: the dorsal surface of the cannon, covered only by skin and tendons, can develop sequestra after wounds or blows.
- **Condylar fractures**: less common than in the metacarpus, but with the same pattern in racehorses.
