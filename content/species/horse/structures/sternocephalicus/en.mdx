---
title: Sternocephalicus
summary: Long muscle on the ventral neck, from the sternum to the mandible; it forms the ventral border of the jugular groove.
---

## Overview

It arises on the manubrium of the sternum and runs along the ventrolateral neck to the head. In the horse it has only the **mandibular part** (sternomandibularis); there is no mastoid part. The two muscles run together at the base of the neck and diverge cranially. Its terminal tendon passes deep to the parotid gland.

## Origin

Manubrium of the sternum.

## Insertion

Caudal border of the mandibular ramus, near the angle.

## Action

Draws the head and neck ventrally or to the same side.

## Innervation

Ventral branch of the accessory nerve (*n. accessorius*).

## Clinical relevance

- It forms the **ventral border of the jugular groove**. In the cranial half of the neck the omohyoideus separates the external jugular vein from the common carotid artery; in the caudal half there is no such barrier, and a misdirected injection can enter the carotid and cause immediate seizures or collapse. Jugular venipuncture is therefore done in the cranial third of the neck.
- **Jugular thrombophlebitis** is a complication of catheters and of irritant injections outside the vein.
- In surgery for **crib-biting** (modified Forssell procedure) the ventral branch of the accessory nerve, which supplies this muscle, is cut and the neighbouring infrahyoid muscles are resected.
