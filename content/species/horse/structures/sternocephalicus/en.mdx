## Overview

It arises on the manubrium of the sternum and runs along the ventrolateral neck to the head.

In the horse it has only the **mandibular part** (sternomandibularis); there is no mastoid part.

The two muscles run together at the base of the neck and diverge cranially. Its terminal tendon passes deep to the parotid gland.

## Origin

Manubrium of the sternum.

## Insertion

Caudal border of the mandibular ramus, near the angle.

## Action

Draws the head and neck ventrally or to the same side.

## Innervation

Ventral branch of the accessory nerve (*n. accessorius*).

## Clinical relevance

### Jugular groove

It forms the ventral border of the jugular groove.

- In the cranial half of the neck the omohyoideus separates the external jugular vein from the common carotid artery.
- In the caudal half there is no such barrier. A misdirected injection can enter the carotid and cause immediate seizures or collapse.

Jugular **venipuncture** is therefore done in the cranial third of the neck.

### Jugular thrombophlebitis

A complication of catheters and of irritant injections outside the vein.

### Crib-biting

In its surgery, the modified Forssell procedure, the ventral branch of the accessory nerve, which supplies this muscle, is cut and the neighbouring infrahyoid muscles are resected.
