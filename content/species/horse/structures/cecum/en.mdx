## Overview

The equine caecum is huge compared with that of ruminants or carnivores.

Together with the ascending colon it is where microorganisms ferment plant fibre. They produce **volatile fatty acids**, the horse's main energy source.

It has **four taeniae** (dorsal, ventral, medial and lateral). They gather the wall into four rows of **sacculations** (*haustra*).

## Parts

- **Base** (*basis caeci*). The dorsal, widest, curved part, in the right paralumbar fossa. It is attached to the abdominal roof, next to the right kidney and the pancreas. Its lesser curvature carries, close together, the **ileal orifice** and the **caecocolic orifice**. Through the latter, ingesta passes into the right ventral colon.
- **Body** (*corpus caeci*). Runs cranially down the right side and then along the abdominal floor.
- **Apex** (*apex caeci*). Free, on the abdominal floor near the xiphoid cartilage.

### Folds

- The **ileocaecal fold** joins the ileum to the dorsal taenia.
- The **caecocolic fold** joins the lateral taenia to the right ventral colon.

## Clinical relevance

### Caecal impaction

It may cause only mild pain and end in **rupture** with little warning. It has been described in horses hospitalised for other problems, after anaesthesia or on anti-inflammatory drugs.

On rectal palpation, the taut ventral taenia and the full caecum are felt in the right caudal quadrant.

### Caecal tympany

It is decompressed with a cannula or trocar through the **right paralumbar fossa**, at the point of greatest resonance on percussion.

### Caecocaecal and caecocolic intussusception

It is associated with *Anoplocephala perfoliata* at the ileocaecal junction. It requires surgery.

### Auscultation

Caecal borborygmi are heard in the right paralumbar fossa. Their **absence** is an important finding in a horse with colic.
