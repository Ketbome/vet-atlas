## Overview

The equine urinary bladder (*vesica urinaria*) is pear-shaped and large. Empty, it retracts into the pelvic cavity, on the pelvic floor; as it fills it advances cranially along the abdominal floor. It has an **apex**, a **body** and a **neck**, which continues as the urethra. The ureters open in the dorsal wall, near the neck, at the **trigone of the bladder**.

## Relations and ligaments

- **Lateral ligaments**: carry in their free border the **round ligament of the bladder**, the remnant of the umbilical artery.
- **Median ligament**: joins the ventral surface to the floor of the pelvis and abdomen; in the fetus it contains the **urachus**, which connects the bladder to the allantois through the umbilicus.
- **In the mare**: the uterus and vagina lie dorsally. The **urethra** is short and wide and opens on the floor of the vagina, at the junction with the vestibule; there is no suburethral diverticulum, so catheterisation is easy.
- **In the male**: the deferent ducts, vesicular glands and prostate lie dorsally. The urethra is long and ends at the **urethral process**, which projects into the fossa of the glans.

## Clinical relevance

- **Urolithiasis**: equine bladder stones are calcium carbonate, often spiculated; they are more common in males and cause haematuria at the end of urination or after exercise, stranguria and incontinence. They are felt per rectum and seen by cystoscopy.
- **Bladder rupture in the foal**: usually occurs at foaling, mainly in colts; urine in the abdomen (uroperitoneum) causes abdominal distension, hyponatraemia, hypochloraemia and hyperkalaemia. The peritoneal fluid to plasma creatinine ratio confirms the diagnosis.
- **Patent urachus**: urine drips from the navel of the foal; it may be complicated by umbilical infection.
- **Paralytic bladder and sabulous cystitis**: with lesions of the sacral spinal cord or cauda equina (for example in equine herpesvirus 1 myeloencephalopathy), the bladder distends and accumulates sediment.
- **Eversion and prolapse**: in the mare, during foaling, the bladder can evert through the urethra or protrude through a vaginal tear.
