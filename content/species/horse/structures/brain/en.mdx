## Overview

The equine brain (*encephalon*) fills a cranial cavity that is small compared with the head. The head is mostly face: the long nasal region and the continuously erupting teeth take up most of the skull.

The cranial cavity lies **caudal to the orbits**, under the nuchal crest and the frontal and parietal bones. It is continuous with the vertebral canal through the foramen magnum.

## Parts

- **Cerebrum**. Two hemispheres with many gyri, separated by the longitudinal fissure. The cortex integrates vision, behaviour and voluntary movement.
- **Cerebellum**. Caudal to the cerebrum, over the fourth ventricle. It coordinates movement and balance.
- **Brainstem**. Midbrain, pons and medulla oblongata. Most cranial nerves arise from it.

## Blood supply

Unlike cattle, the horse has no **rostral epidural rete mirabile**.

The **internal carotid artery** reaches the cerebral arterial circle directly. On its way it runs along the dorsal wall of the **guttural pouch**, together with cranial nerves IX to XII.

## Clinical relevance

### Head trauma

A horse that rears and falls over backwards hits its poll. The pull of the ventral head muscles can fracture the **basisphenoid** and basioccipital bones.

The result is haemorrhage into the brainstem, with vestibular signs and blood from the nostrils or ears.

### Viral encephalitides

Eastern, Western and Venezuelan equine encephalomyelitis and West Nile virus are transmitted by **mosquitoes**. They cause fever, behavioural change, ataxia and recumbency. Vaccines are available.

### Hepatic encephalopathy

Ammonia and other toxins that the diseased liver fails to clear cause:

- depression
- head pressing
- blindness
- yawning

### Leukoencephalomalacia

Liquefactive necrosis of the cerebral white matter caused by **fumonisins** in mouldy maize (*Fusarium verticillioides*). It progresses quickly and is usually fatal.

### Rabies

It must be on the differential list for any horse with rapidly progressing neurological signs. Necropsy and sample handling require precautions.

### Euthanasia by captive bolt or free bullet

The point of aim is where the lines joining each eye to the base of the opposite ear cross, **slightly above** it. The brain lies further caudal than the shape of the head suggests.
