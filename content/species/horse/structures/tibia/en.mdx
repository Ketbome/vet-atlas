## Overview

The **tibia** (*tibia*) is the main bone of the leg. It articulates proximally with the femur, through the menisci, at the **stifle**, and distally with the talus at the **hock**. Its medial surface is **subcutaneous** along most of its length. The **fibula** (*fibula*) is much reduced in the horse: its head articulates with the lateral condyle of the tibia, its shaft is a slender rod that ends free about halfway down the leg, and its distal end fuses with the tibia to form the **lateral malleolus**.

## Proximal end

- **Medial and lateral condyles**: articular surfaces for the femoral condyles, separated by the **intercondylar eminence**, where the cruciate ligaments and menisci attach.
- **Tibial tuberosity (*tuberositas tibiae*)**: large, on the cranial surface; receives the **three patellar ligaments**.
- **Extensor groove (*sulcus extensorius*)**: on the lateral border; it carries the common tendon of origin of the long digital extensor and the peroneus tertius.
- **Tibial crest**: continues the tuberosity distally on the cranial surface.

## Distal end

- **Cochlea of the tibia (*cochlea tibiae*)**: articular surface with **two oblique grooves** separated by an **intermediate ridge**, which fit the ridges of the trochlea of the talus.
- **Medial malleolus**: belongs to the tibia; origin of the medial collateral ligament of the tarsus.
- **Lateral malleolus**: the distal end of the fibula fused with the tibia; origin of the lateral collateral ligament.

## Clinical relevance

- **Tibial stress fractures**: in **young racehorses**, a common cause of pelvic limb lameness early in training; confirmed by scintigraphy or radiography.
- **Kick fractures**: the medial surface, with no muscle to protect it, takes kicks from other horses; incomplete fissures are monitored because they can become complete.
- **Osteochondrosis of the distal intermediate ridge**: the most common site of osteochondrosis in the tarsus; it causes distension of the tarsocrural joint in young horses and is resolved arthroscopically.
- **Fibula on radiographs**: its ossification from several centres leaves radiolucent lines that can be mistaken for fractures.
