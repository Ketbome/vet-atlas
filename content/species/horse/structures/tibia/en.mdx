## Overview

The **tibia** (*tibia*) is the main bone of the leg. It articulates:

- proximally with the femur, through the menisci, at the **stifle**
- distally with the talus, at the **hock**

Its medial surface is **subcutaneous** along most of its length.

The **fibula** (*fibula*) is much reduced in the horse:

- its head articulates with the lateral condyle of the tibia
- its shaft is a slender rod that ends free about halfway down the leg
- its distal end fuses with the tibia to form the lateral malleolus

## Proximal end

- **Medial and lateral condyles**. Articular surfaces for the femoral condyles. They are separated by the **intercondylar eminence**, where the cruciate ligaments and menisci attach.
- **Tibial tuberosity** (*tuberositas tibiae*). Large, on the cranial surface. It receives the **three patellar ligaments**.
- **Extensor groove** (*sulcus extensorius*). On the lateral border. It carries the common tendon of origin of the long digital extensor and the peroneus tertius.
- **Tibial crest**. Continues the tuberosity distally on the cranial surface.

## Distal end

- **Cochlea of the tibia** (*cochlea tibiae*). Articular surface with two oblique grooves separated by an **intermediate ridge**. It fits the ridges of the trochlea of the talus.
- **Medial malleolus**. Belongs to the tibia. Origin of the medial collateral ligament of the tarsus.
- **Lateral malleolus**. The distal end of the fibula fused with the tibia. Origin of the lateral collateral ligament.

## Clinical relevance

- **Tibial stress fractures**. In young racehorses they are a common cause of pelvic limb lameness early in training. They are confirmed by scintigraphy or radiography.
- **Kick fractures**. The medial surface, with no muscle to protect it, takes kicks from other horses. Incomplete fissures are monitored because they can become complete.
- **Osteochondrosis of the distal intermediate ridge**. The most common site of osteochondrosis in the tarsus. It causes distension of the tarsocrural joint in young horses and is resolved arthroscopically.
- **Fibula on radiographs**. It ossifies from several centres, which leave radiolucent lines that can be mistaken for fractures.
