---
title: Gluteus medius
summary: The great muscle mass of the equine croup; it reaches cranially over the loin and is the main extensor of the hip.
---

## Overview

It is by far the largest of the gluteal muscles and gives the equine croup its bulk. Besides covering the wing of the ilium, it **extends cranially over the longissimus lumborum**, in a depression of that muscle's aponeurosis, as far as the cranial lumbar region. Its deep part is sometimes described as a separate muscle, the **accessory gluteal** (*m. gluteus accessorius*). In dissection it is partly covered by the superficial gluteal and biceps femoris.

## Origin

Aponeurosis of the longissimus lumborum, gluteal surface of the ilium, coxal and sacral tuberosities, sacroiliac ligaments and broad sacrotuberous ligament.

## Insertion

Greater trochanter of the femur (*trochanter major*). The tendon of its deep part glides over the **trochanteric bursa** before inserting.

## Action

Extends the hip and abducts the pelvic limb. It is the main driver of propulsion, take-off, jumping and kicking, and takes part in rearing.

## Innervation

Cranial gluteal nerve (*n. gluteus cranialis*).

## Clinical relevance

- In **exertional rhabdomyolysis** ("tying-up") the gluteal and loin muscles become hard and painful; the horse sweats, is reluctant to move and blood creatine kinase rises.
- **Trochanteric bursitis** causes hind limb lameness with pain on pressure over the greater trochanter; it has been described mainly in Standardbreds.
- Intramuscular injections into the croup are not advised in the horse: if an abscess forms, it drains poorly from this deep muscle mass.
