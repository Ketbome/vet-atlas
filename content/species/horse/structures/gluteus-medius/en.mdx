## Overview

It is by far the largest of the gluteal muscles and gives the equine croup its bulk.

Besides covering the wing of the ilium, it **extends cranially over the longissimus lumborum**. It lies in a depression of that muscle's aponeurosis and reaches the cranial lumbar region.

Its deep part is sometimes described as a separate muscle, the **accessory gluteal** (*m. gluteus accessorius*).

In dissection it is partly covered by the superficial gluteal and biceps femoris.

## Origin

- Aponeurosis of the longissimus lumborum
- Gluteal surface of the ilium
- Coxal and sacral tuberosities
- Sacroiliac ligaments and broad sacrotuberous ligament

## Insertion

Greater trochanter of the femur (*trochanter major*). The tendon of its deep part glides over the **trochanteric bursa** before inserting.

## Action

Extends the hip and abducts the pelvic limb.

It is the main driver of propulsion, take-off, jumping and kicking, and takes part in rearing.

## Innervation

Cranial gluteal nerve (*n. gluteus cranialis*).

## Clinical relevance

- **Exertional rhabdomyolysis** ("tying-up"). The gluteal and loin muscles become hard and painful. The horse sweats and is reluctant to move, and blood creatine kinase rises.
- **Trochanteric bursitis**. It causes hind limb lameness with pain on pressure over the greater trochanter. It has been described mainly in Standardbreds.
- **Intramuscular injections**. They are not advised in the equine croup: if an abscess forms, it drains poorly from this deep muscle mass.
