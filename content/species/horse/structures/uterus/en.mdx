---
title: Uterus
summary: Organ of pregnancy. In the mare it is T- or Y-shaped, with a large body and short, straight horns; it has no caruncles, the placenta is diffuse, and the cervix is soft and dilates easily.
---

## Overview

The mare's uterus hangs from the roof of the abdomen and pelvis by the **broad ligament** (*mesometrium*), which attaches to the dorsal curvature of the horns; the ventral curvature is free. Outside pregnancy, the body and horns lie mainly in the abdomen, in front of the pelvic inlet, resting on the intestinal loops; the small colon and rectum lie dorsally.

## Parts

- **Horns (*cornua uteri*)**: relatively short, they diverge from the body almost straight towards the ovaries, without coiling. Their base is where the embryo becomes fixed.
- **Body (*corpus uteri*)**: long and wide, nearly as long as the horns; this is the most visible difference from the cow.
- **Endometrium**: with longitudinal folds and no caruncles. The placenta is **diffuse and epitheliochorial**, with microcotyledons spread over the whole chorion. Early in pregnancy, trophoblast cells invade the endometrium and form the **endometrial cups**, which produce equine chorionic gonadotropin (eCG).
- **Cervix (*cervix uteri*)**: short, soft-walled, with longitudinal folds that continue into the vagina; it relaxes in oestrus and admits one or more fingers, which makes transcervical techniques easy.

## Clinical relevance

- **Pregnancy diagnosis**: transrectal ultrasound detects the embryonic vesicle from two weeks. The vesicle is mobile until it becomes fixed at around day 16 at the base of one horn.
- **Twin pregnancy**: the mare tolerates it poorly (abortion or weak foals); it is therefore looked for at the first scans and one vesicle is reduced, ideally before fixation.
- **Endometritis**: persistent post-breeding endometritis affects mares with poor uterine clearance; it is treated with lavage and oxytocin. Endometrial biopsy grades fibrosis and inflammation and guides the fertility prognosis.
- **Ascending placentitis**: enters through the cervix and causes abortion or weak foals.
- **Retained placenta**: if the membranes are not passed within about 3 hours of foaling they are considered retained; it may be followed by metritis, endotoxaemia and laminitis.
- **Uterine torsion**: in late pregnancy; it causes colic and is diagnosed by rectal palpation of the taut, crossed broad ligaments.
