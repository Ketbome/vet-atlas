## Overview

As in other herbivores, it is a relatively small muscle. Chewing depends mainly on the masseter.

The equine temporal fossa is wide. It covers the dorsolateral surface of the cranium, between the external sagittal crest and the zygomatic arch.

Rostral to it, above the eye, is the **supraorbital fossa**, filled with fat.

The muscle passes medial to the zygomatic arch to the mandible.

## Origin

Temporal fossa, bounded medially by the external sagittal crest and temporal line.

## Insertion

Coronoid process of the mandible. In the horse it is tall and sits medial to the zygomatic arch.

## Action

Raises the mandible and closes the mouth.

## Innervation

Mandibular nerve (*n. mandibularis*), a branch of the trigeminal nerve (cranial nerve V).

## Clinical relevance

- **One-sided atrophy**. Atrophy of the temporalis and masseter on one side suggests a lesion of the mandibular nerve on that side.
- **Sunken supraorbital fossa**. It reflects loss of fat, not of the temporalis muscle. It is a sign of old age or poor body condition.
