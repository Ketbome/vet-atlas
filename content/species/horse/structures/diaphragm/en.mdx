## Overview

It is convex cranially. Its periphery is muscular and its centre is a tendon (*centrum tendineum*).

It has three openings:

- the aortic hiatus
- the oesophageal hiatus
- the caval foramen

## Topography in the horse

The line of attachment is oblique. Ventrally it attaches to the xiphoid process. From there it rises caudodorsally along the costal cartilages and the costal arch to the last ribs.

The dome projects far cranially, so the intrathoracic part of the abdomen is large. The **liver**, the **stomach** and much of the **spleen** rest on its abdominal surface, under the ribs.

On its thoracic surface, the lungs fill the costodiaphragmatic recesses and the accessory lobe fills the mediastinal recess.

## Origin

- Lumbar part: the right and left crura, on the bodies of the cranial lumbar vertebrae.
- Costal part: medial surface of the caudal ribs and their cartilages, along the costal arch.
- Sternal part: xiphoid process.

## Insertion

Central tendon.

## Action

When it contracts it flattens, enlarges the thoracic volume and produces inspiration.

## Innervation

Phrenic nerve (*n. phrenicus*), formed by ventral branches of the caudal cervical nerves.

## Clinical relevance

### Diaphragmatic hernia

It can be congenital or acquired through trauma, falls, foaling or strenuous exertion.

Loops of small intestine, or less often colon, pass into the thorax and may become strangulated. The main sign is **colic**, sometimes with respiratory difficulty.

It is diagnosed by ultrasound or radiography and repaired surgically.

### Synchronous diaphragmatic flutter

Also called "thumps". The phrenic nerve runs over the heart and depolarises with each heartbeat, so the flank twitches in time with the heart.

It appears with calcium and other electrolyte disturbances:

- typically in exhausted endurance horses
- in hypocalcaemia of the lactating mare
