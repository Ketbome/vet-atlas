## Overview

The oesophagus starts dorsal to the cricoid cartilage, over the larynx. Its course:

- in the neck it first runs dorsal to the trachea
- from the middle third on it lies on its **left side**, deep in the left jugular groove
- at the thoracic inlet it returns to the dorsal surface of the trachea
- it crosses the base of the heart in the mediastinum
- it passes through the diaphragm at the oesophageal hiatus

The abdominal part is very short and ends at the cardia.

## Structure

- **Muscular coat**. Striated in the cranial two-thirds and smooth in the caudal third, where the wall thickens and the lumen narrows.
- **Mucosa**. Stratified squamous epithelium, with longitudinal folds that flatten as the bolus passes.
- **Cardia**. The oesophagus enters the stomach obliquely, and the muscle of the cardia forms a powerful sphincter. Together with the position of the stomach under the ribs, this means that horses **can hardly vomit** or belch.

## Clinical relevance

### Oesophageal obstruction

Also called choke. A bolus of dry feed, such as pellets or unsoaked beet pulp, or a foreign body lodges, often in the neck or at the thoracic inlet.

The horse:

- discharges saliva and food from both nostrils
- coughs and stretches its neck
- may show a swelling in the left jugular groove

The main complication is **aspiration pneumonia**.

### Strictures

After an obstruction with circumferential mucosal damage, scar strictures may form. They predispose to new obstructions and are monitored by endoscopy.

### Nasogastric intubation

The tube is passed through the ventral nasal meatus.

- **In the oesophagus**, it is seen or felt in the left jugular groove and meets resistance as it advances.
- **In the trachea**, it slides in without resistance and vibrates with breathing.
