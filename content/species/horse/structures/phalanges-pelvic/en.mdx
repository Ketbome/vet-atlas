## Overview

The digit of the pelvic limb repeats the structure of the thoracic one:

- three phalanges
- two proximal sesamoid bones at the fetlock, the **metatarsophalangeal** joint
- a distal sesamoid or navicular bone, plantar to the coffin joint

The differences are of shape and proportion. The hind pastern is usually somewhat shorter and more upright. The **hoof is narrower and more pointed**, with a steeper dorsal wall.

## Phalanges

- **Proximal phalanx** (*phalanx proximalis*). The long pastern bone. It articulates with the condyle of Mt III and with the proximal sesamoid bones.
- **Middle phalanx** (*phalanx media*). The short pastern bone, short and partly inside the hoof.
- **Distal phalanx** (*phalanx distalis*). The **coffin bone**, narrower and longer than that of the thoracic limb, with a more concave solar surface. It has the dorsal extensor process, the flexor surface for the deep digital flexor tendon and the plantar processes, which carry the ungular cartilages.

## Sesamoid bones

- **Proximal sesamoid bones**. Two, on the plantar side of the fetlock. They receive the branches of the suspensory ligament and, with the intersesamoidean ligament, form the channel for the flexor tendons.
- **Navicular bone** (*os sesamoideum distale*). Plantar to the distal interphalangeal joint. The deep digital flexor tendon glides over it, separated by the **navicular bursa**.

## Clinical relevance

- **Navicular syndrome**. Much **less common** than in the thoracic limb, because the pelvic limb bears less weight.
- **Ringbone** (osteoarthritis of the pastern joint). Common in the pelvic limbs of horses that make sharp turns and sudden stops. In advanced cases it is treated by arthrodesis of the joint, after which many horses return to work.
- **Fractures of the proximal phalanx and proximal sesamoid bones**. In racehorses and sport horses, as in the thoracic limb.
- **Laminitis**. Affects the thoracic limbs more often and more severely, but in severe forms it can involve all four.
