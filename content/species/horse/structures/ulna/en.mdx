## Overview

The ulna (*ulna*) is **much reduced** in the horse. Its proximal part is robust and forms the **point of the elbow**, but the shaft thins quickly and fuses with the caudolateral surface of the radius; the distal end is incorporated into the radius as its **lateral styloid process**. As a result it bears no weight and there is no movement between the two forearm bones.

## Key features

- **Olecranon (*olecranon*)**: a long lever projecting caudal to the elbow; its **tuberosity** receives the triceps brachii, the main extensor of the elbow.
- **Anconeal process (*processus anconeus*)**: the cranial beak of the olecranon, which fits into the olecranon fossa of the humerus when the elbow is extended.
- **Trochlear notch (*incisura trochlearis*)**: a concave articular surface that grips the trochlea of the humerus.
- **Shaft**: slender and triangular, it tapers distally and is joined to the radius by fibrous tissue that eventually ossifies.
- **Interosseous space**: small, between ulna and radius, below the elbow.

## Clinical relevance

- **Olecranon fractures**: common from **kicks** or falls. The triceps pulls the proximal fragment and the horse cannot lock the elbow: it adopts a **dropped elbow** posture, with the carpus flexed. Displaced fractures are repaired with a tension band plate on the caudal surface.
- **Physeal fractures of the olecranon**: in foals, through the physis of the tuberosity.
- **Olecranon bursitis ("capped elbow", "shoe boil")**: an acquired bursa over the point of the elbow, caused mainly by the heel of the shoe rubbing when the horse lies with its limbs folded.
