---
title: Biceps brachii
summary: Muscle on the cranial surface of the arm that flexes the elbow and extends the shoulder; in the horse it is a key part of the stay apparatus of the thoracic limb.
---

## Overview

It lies on the cranial surface of the humerus. Its tendon of origin crosses the shoulder joint and glides in the **intertubercular groove**, which in the horse is divided in two by an **intermediate tubercle**. There the tendon is partly fibrocartilaginous and rests on the **intertubercular bursa** (*bursa intertubercularis*).

In the horse the muscle is traversed by an **internal tendinous cord** running from origin to insertion. From its distal end arises the **lacertus fibrosus**, a band that joins the tendon of the extensor carpi radialis and travels with it to the metacarpus.

## Origin

Supraglenoid tubercle of the scapula (*tuberculum supraglenoidale*).

## Insertion

Radial tuberosity (*tuberositas radii*) and, through the lacertus fibrosus, the tuberosity of the third metacarpal bone.

## Action

Flexes the elbow and extends the shoulder.

## Innervation

Musculocutaneous nerve (*n. musculocutaneus*).

## Clinical relevance

- It is part of the **stay apparatus** of the thoracic limb. In the standing horse, the weight of the trunk tends to flex the shoulder; the tendinous cord of the biceps prevents this without muscular effort. Through the lacertus fibrosus and the extensor carpi radialis, the same tension keeps the carpus extended.
- **Intertubercular (bicipital) bursitis** causes forelimb lameness with pain on flexion and extension of the shoulder; it is investigated with ultrasonography and radiography of the shoulder.
