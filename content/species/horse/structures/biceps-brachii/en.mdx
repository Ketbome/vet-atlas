## Overview

It lies on the cranial surface of the humerus.

Its tendon of origin crosses the shoulder joint and glides in the **intertubercular groove**. In the horse this groove is divided in two by an **intermediate tubercle**. There the tendon is partly fibrocartilaginous and rests on the **intertubercular bursa** (*bursa intertubercularis*).

In the horse the muscle is traversed by an **internal tendinous cord** running from origin to insertion.

From its distal end arises the **lacertus fibrosus**. It is a band that joins the tendon of the extensor carpi radialis and travels with it to the metacarpus.

## Origin

Supraglenoid tubercle of the scapula (*tuberculum supraglenoidale*).

## Insertion

- Radial tuberosity (*tuberositas radii*).
- Tuberosity of the third metacarpal bone, through the lacertus fibrosus.

## Action

Flexes the elbow and extends the shoulder.

## Innervation

Musculocutaneous nerve (*n. musculocutaneus*).

## Clinical relevance

### Stay apparatus

It is part of the **stay apparatus** of the thoracic limb.

In the standing horse, the weight of the trunk tends to flex the shoulder. The tendinous cord of the biceps prevents this without muscular effort.

Through the lacertus fibrosus and the extensor carpi radialis, the same tension keeps the carpus extended.

### Intertubercular bursitis

**Intertubercular (bicipital) bursitis** causes forelimb lameness with pain on flexion and extension of the shoulder. It is investigated with ultrasonography and radiography of the shoulder.
