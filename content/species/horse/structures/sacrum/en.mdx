## Overview

The sacrum (*os sacrum*) results from the **fusion of five sacral vertebrae** and forms the roof of the pelvic cavity. Its base articulates cranially with the last lumbar vertebra, and its **wings** with the ilium at the **sacroiliac joints**, through which the thrust of the pelvic limbs is transmitted to the spine. In the horse the wings also articulate with the transverse processes of **L6** (intertransverse joint).

## Key features

- **Base and promontory**: the cranial surface of the body of S1; the **promontory** projects ventrally at the pelvic inlet.
- **Wings (*alae ossis sacri*)**: broad and triangular, with the **auricular surface** for the ilium on their dorsolateral side.
- **Spinous processes**: joined at their base but **free at the tips**, unlike the continuous sacral crest of cattle. Beside them rise the **tubera sacralia** of the ilium, which in the horse lie level with the tips of the spinous processes or slightly above them.
- **Dorsal and ventral sacral foramina**: transmit the branches of the sacral nerves.
- **Ligaments**: the **dorsal and ventral sacroiliac ligaments** join the sacrum to the ilium, and the **broad sacrotuberous ligament** closes the pelvic cavity laterally.

## Clinical relevance

- **Sacroiliac joint disease**: a recognised cause of croup pain and poor performance in sport horses; diagnosis is difficult because the joint is deeply covered by muscle.
- **Asymmetry of the tubera sacralia ("hunter's bump")**: prominence of one or both tubera sacralia, which can point to an old injury of the sacroiliac ligaments, although it is also seen in horses without signs.
- **Sacral fractures**: in horses that fall over backwards or onto the croup; they can injure the sacral and caudal nerves, causing tail paralysis, urinary and faecal incontinence and loss of perineal sensation.
