## Overview

It is the deepest muscle of the caudal forearm.

It has three heads, **humeral**, **ulnar** and **radial**. The humeral head is the largest. All three join above the carpus in a common tendon.

The tendon follows this course:

- it passes through the **carpal canal**
- at mid-cannon it receives its **accessory ligament**, the carpal or "inferior" check ligament, which arises from the palmar carpal ligament
- at the fetlock it passes through the ring of the superficial digital flexor and glides over the palmar surface of the proximal sesamoid bones, inside the **digital sheath**
- in the pastern it widens and enters the hoof
- inside the hoof it passes over the flexor surface of the **navicular bone**, the distal sesamoid, separated from it by the **navicular bursa** (*bursa podotrochlearis*), and inserts on the third phalanx

## Origin

- Humeral head, on the medial epicondyle of the humerus.
- Ulnar head, on the medial surface of the olecranon.
- Radial head, on the caudal surface of the radius.

## Insertion

Flexor surface of the third phalanx (*facies flexoria*).

## Action

Flexes all the joints of the digit and the carpus.

With the limb planted, it supports the fetlock and the distal interphalangeal joint.

## Innervation

Median and ulnar nerves (*n. medianus*, *n. ulnaris*).

## Clinical relevance

### Stay apparatus

With its accessory ligament, which anchors it to the carpus, it is part of the **stay apparatus** of the thoracic limb.

### Podotrochlear apparatus

The tendon, navicular bone and navicular bursa form the **podotrochlear apparatus**.

Their lesions are a major cause of **palmar foot pain**, or navicular syndrome. Magnetic resonance imaging often shows tendon lesions at the level of the navicular bone.

### Surgery

- **Desmotomy of the accessory ligament**, the inferior check ligament, is used to correct flexural deformity of the distal interphalangeal joint in foals, known as club foot.
- In chronic **laminitis** with rotation of the third phalanx, **tenotomy** of this tendon reduces its pull on the bone. It is kept for severe cases that do not respond to other treatment.
