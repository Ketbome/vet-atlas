## Overview

The carpus (*ossa carpi*) is the group of short bones between the forearm and the metacarpus. In equestrian language it is called the **knee**, although it corresponds to the human wrist; the anatomical knee of the horse is the **stifle**. The horse has **seven or eight carpal bones** in two rows, bound by numerous ligaments. They form three stacked joints: the **antebrachiocarpal**, which provides most of the flexion, the **middle carpal**, with moderate movement, and the **carpometacarpal**, which is almost immobile.

## Bones

- **Proximal row**: from medial to lateral, the **radial carpal bone** (*os carpi radiale*), the **intermediate carpal bone** (*os carpi intermedium*) and the **ulnar carpal bone** (*os carpi ulnare*). Caudolaterally, the disc-shaped **accessory carpal bone** (*os carpi accessorium*) projects and is easily palpated.
- **Distal row**: the **second, third and fourth carpal bones**. The **third carpal bone** is the largest and bears most of the weight. The **first carpal bone** is small and **inconstant**.
- **Carpal canal (*canalis carpi*)**: bounded laterally by the accessory carpal bone and palmarly by the flexor retinaculum; the digital flexor tendons pass through it inside the **carpal sheath**.

## Clinical relevance

- **Chip fractures**: small fragments off the dorsal margin of the carpal bones, mainly the **radial carpal**, the **third carpal**, the intermediate carpal and the distal radius, in racehorses. They result from repeated loading in hyperextension and are removed arthroscopically.
- **Slab fractures of the third carpal bone**: run through the bone from proximal to distal on its dorsal aspect; they are repaired with screws.
- **Carpal hygroma**: subcutaneous fluid accumulation over the dorsal carpus from repeated knocks (for instance, against the stable door).
- **Incomplete ossification of the cuboidal bones**: in premature or dysmature foals the carpal and tarsal bones are still cartilaginous and can collapse under weight, leaving a permanent angular deformity; it is diagnosed radiographically and requires exercise restriction.
