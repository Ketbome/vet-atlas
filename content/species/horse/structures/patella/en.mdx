## Overview

The patella (*patella*) is the largest sesamoid bone in the body. It lies in the tendon of insertion of the **quadriceps femoris** and glides over the femoral trochlea. In this way it changes the line of pull of the muscle to extend the stifle.

In the horse it is pyramid-shaped, with a proximal base and a distal apex. It is part of the **stay apparatus** of the pelvic limb.

## Key features

- **Articular surface** (*facies articularis*). Caudal. It fits the femoral trochlea.
- **Parapatellar fibrocartilage** (*fibrocartilago parapatellaris*). Extends the patella medially. With the medial patellar ligament it forms a loop that grips the medial ridge of the trochlea.
- **Patellar ligaments** (*ligamentum patellae laterale, intermedium et mediale*). There are **three**, lateral, intermediate and medial, running from the patella to the tibial tuberosity. They are easily palpated on the cranial aspect of the stifle.

### Locking mechanism

When the horse is at rest, the patella rotates medially. Its loop of fibrocartilage and medial patellar ligament hooks over the **tubercle of the medial ridge** of the trochlea.

The stifle is locked in extension with almost no muscular effort. Through the **reciprocal apparatus** (peroneus tertius and superficial digital flexor), the hock is locked as well.

This lets the horse doze standing, with its weight on one pelvic limb while resting the other.

## Clinical relevance

- **Upward fixation of the patella**. The patella stays hooked and the horse cannot release it. The limb locks in extension and the animal drags the toe. It can be intermittent, with a momentary catch at walk. It is more common in young horses, horses in poor condition or with an underdeveloped quadriceps. It is treated first with exercise to strengthen the quadriceps. **Medial patellar desmotomy** is kept for persistent cases, because it can cause fragmentation of the patellar apex.
- **Patellar fractures**. From kicks or hitting obstacles when jumping.
