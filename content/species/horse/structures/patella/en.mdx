## Overview

The patella (*patella*) is the largest sesamoid bone in the body. It lies in the tendon of insertion of the **quadriceps femoris** and glides over the femoral trochlea, changing the line of pull of the muscle to extend the stifle. In the horse it is pyramid-shaped, with a proximal **base** and a distal **apex**, and it is part of the **stay apparatus** of the pelvic limb.

## Key features

- **Articular surface (*facies articularis*)**: caudal, fits the femoral trochlea.
- **Parapatellar fibrocartilage (*fibrocartilago parapatellaris*)**: extends the patella medially; with the medial patellar ligament it forms a loop that grips the medial ridge of the trochlea.
- **Patellar ligaments**: **three**, **lateral**, **intermediate** and **medial** (*ligamentum patellae laterale, intermedium et mediale*), running from the patella to the **tibial tuberosity**. They are easily palpated on the cranial aspect of the stifle.
- **Locking mechanism**: when the horse is at rest, the patella rotates medially and its loop of fibrocartilage and medial patellar ligament hooks over the **tubercle of the medial ridge** of the trochlea. The stifle is locked in extension with almost no muscular effort and, through the **reciprocal apparatus** (peroneus tertius and superficial digital flexor), so is the hock. This lets the horse doze standing, with its weight on one pelvic limb while resting the other.

## Clinical relevance

- **Upward fixation of the patella**: the patella stays hooked and the horse cannot release it; the limb locks in extension and the animal drags the toe. It can be **intermittent**, with a momentary catch at walk, and is more common in young horses, horses in poor condition or with an underdeveloped quadriceps. It is treated first with exercise to strengthen the muscle; **medial patellar desmotomy** is kept for persistent cases, because it can cause fragmentation of the patellar apex.
- **Patellar fractures**: from kicks or hitting obstacles when jumping.
