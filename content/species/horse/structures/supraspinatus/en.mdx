## Overview

Thick muscle that fills the supraspinous fossa and bulges beyond the cranial border of the scapula.

The horse has no true collateral ligaments at the shoulder. The supraspinatus and infraspinatus, together with the subscapularis, act as **contractile ligaments** that stabilise the joint.

It ends in two branches, on either side of the biceps brachii tendon.

## Origin

Supraspinous fossa (*fossa supraspinata*), spine and cartilage of the scapula.

## Insertion

Cranial parts of the greater tubercle (*tuberculum majus*) and lesser tubercle (*tuberculum minus*) of the humerus.

## Action

Extends and stabilises the shoulder.

## Innervation

Suprascapular nerve (*n. suprascapularis*).

## Clinical relevance

In **sweeny**, the suprascapular nerve is injured where it winds around the cranial border of the scapula. It is caused by collisions or, in draught horses, by the collar.

The supraspinatus and infraspinatus waste and the scapular spine stands out. Without these stabilisers, the shoulder shifts laterally when the limb bears weight.
