## Overview

The axis (*axis*) is the second cervical vertebra and the longest of the equine spine. Its characteristic feature is the **dens**, a cranial projection of the body that sits in the ventral arch of the atlas. The atlas, and with it the head, rotates around it at the **atlantoaxial joint**.

## Key features

- **Dens**: in the horse it is **broad and spade-shaped**, concave dorsally, rather than cylindrical as in the dog. It is attached to the atlas by a strong **longitudinal ligament of the dens**.
- **Spinous process**: a **long, high crest** that widens caudally; it gives attachment to the nuchal ligament and neck muscles.
- **Body**: long, with a marked **ventral crest** and a broad cranial head that continues into the dens.
- **Transverse processes**: small and directed caudally, pierced by the **transverse foramen**.
- **Caudal articular processes**: articulate with the third cervical vertebra.

## Clinical relevance

- **Fractures of the axis**: mainly in **foals** after falls or blows to the head; they usually involve the dens or the physis between the dens and the body, and can compress the spinal cord.
- **Cervical stenotic myelopathy (wobbler syndrome)**: it affects the cervical vertebrae caudal to the axis, but radiographic examination of the neck always includes it.
