## Overview

The axis (*axis*) is the second cervical vertebra and the longest of the equine spine.

Its characteristic feature is the **dens**, a cranial projection of the body that sits in the ventral arch of the atlas. The atlas, and with it the head, rotates around it at the **atlantoaxial joint**.

## Key features

- **Dens**. In the horse it is **broad and spade-shaped**, concave dorsally, rather than cylindrical as in the dog. A strong **longitudinal ligament of the dens** attaches it to the atlas.
- **Spinous process**. A **long, high crest** that widens caudally. It gives attachment to the nuchal ligament and neck muscles.
- **Body**. Long, with a marked **ventral crest** and a broad cranial head that continues into the dens.
- **Transverse processes**. Small and directed caudally, pierced by the **transverse foramen**.
- **Caudal articular processes**. They articulate with the third cervical vertebra.

## Clinical relevance

- **Fractures of the axis**. Seen mainly in **foals** after falls or blows to the head. They usually involve the dens or the physis between the dens and the body, and can compress the spinal cord.
- **Cervical stenotic myelopathy** (wobbler syndrome). It affects the cervical vertebrae caudal to the axis, but radiographic examination of the neck always includes it.
