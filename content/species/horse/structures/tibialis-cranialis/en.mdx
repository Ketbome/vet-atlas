---
title: Tibialis cranialis
summary: Hock flexor on the cranial crus; in the horse it is accompanied by the peroneus tertius, the cranial component of the reciprocal apparatus.
---

## Overview

It lies directly on the cranial surface of the tibia, covered by the long digital extensor. Distally its tendon passes through that of the **peroneus tertius** and splits into two branches:

- **Dorsal branch**: to the proximal end of the third metatarsal bone.
- **Medial branch** (**cunean tendon**): crosses the medial hock to the first and second tarsal bones, over the **cunean bursa**.

In the horse the **peroneus tertius** (*m. fibularis tertius*) is an **entirely tendinous cord**, with no muscle fibres. It arises from the extensor fossa of the femur, runs cranial to the tibialis cranialis and ends on the third metatarsal and the tarsal bones.

## Origin

Proximal end of the tibia (lateral condyle and region of the tuberosity).

## Insertion

Proximal end of the third metatarsal bone (dorsal branch) and first and second tarsal bones (cunean tendon).

## Action

Flexes the hock.

## Innervation

Deep peroneal (fibular) nerve (*n. fibularis profundus*).

## Clinical relevance

- The peroneus tertius is the cranial component of the **reciprocal apparatus**: it couples stifle and hock so that when one flexes, the other does too. Its **rupture** breaks this coupling: with the stifle flexed the hock can be extended and the common calcanean tendon goes slack and dimples.
- In **bone spavin** (osteoarthritis of the distal tarsal joints) the cunean bursa can become inflamed; **cunean tenectomy** has been used as part of treatment.
- In **peroneal nerve paralysis** the hock stays extended and the horse stands with the fetlock knuckled forward.
