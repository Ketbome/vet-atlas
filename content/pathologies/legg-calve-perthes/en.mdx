## What it is

It is aseptic (avascular) necrosis of the femoral head and neck during growth.

For reasons that are not fully understood, the blood supply to the femoral epiphysis is interrupted and the subchondral bone dies. When the body tries to revascularise and repair the area, the softened bone **collapses** under load.

The head loses its spherical shape and osteoarthritis develops.

A hereditary component has been proposed. High heritability has been shown in the Manchester terrier.

## Who it affects

Small and toy breeds:

- Yorkshire terrier
- West Highland white terrier
- toy and miniature poodle
- Manchester terrier
- Cairn terrier
- pug
- miniature pinscher

The typical age at presentation is between **4 and 11 months**. It can affect one or both hips.

## Anatomy involved

- The **femoral head** (*caput ossis femoris*) flattens, deforms and may fragment.
- The **femoral neck** (*collum ossis femoris*) remodels along with the head.
- The joint with the **acetabulum** becomes incongruent and develops secondary osteoarthritis.

## Clinical signs

Progressive lameness of a pelvic limb. It starts insidiously and may progress to non-weight-bearing.

- pain and crepitus on manipulation of the hip
- atrophy of the thigh muscles
- irritability in some puppies

## Diagnosis

It is made with a **ventrodorsal pelvic radiograph**.

Early changes are subtle irregularities in the density of the femoral head, which can appear before the lameness.

Later, osteolytic foci, flattening and collapse of the head, fragmentation, joint incongruity and osteoarthritis appear.

It must be distinguished from capital physeal fracture and from hip dysplasia.

## Treatment

Conservative management, with rest and anti-inflammatories, may be enough in mild, early cases, but the deformity often progresses.

The treatment of choice is **femoral head and neck excision arthroplasty (FHO)**. In a small dog, the resulting fibrous pseudoarthrosis allows an almost normal gait to be recovered, especially with early physiotherapy.

In selected cases a total hip replacement may be considered.

The prognosis after surgery is good to excellent.
