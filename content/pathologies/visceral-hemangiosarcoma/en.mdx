## What it is

Hemangiosarcoma is a malignant neoplasm of the vascular endothelium. The tumour forms fragile, blood-filled cavities that rupture easily and bleed.

In the dog the visceral form is one of the most aggressive cancers.

Common primary sites are the **spleen** and the **right atrium**, which can be affected at the same time. Other organs, such as the liver, can also be involved.

Metastasis by the bloodstream and implantation in the abdomen after rupture are early. Most dogs therefore have **micrometastatic disease** when diagnosed.

## Who it affects

Middle-aged to older dogs of large breeds: German shepherd, golden retriever, Labrador retriever and others.

Not every splenic mass is hemangiosarcoma. Haematomas and nodular hyperplasia are also common, and the distinction requires **histopathology**.

## Anatomy involved

In the **spleen** it forms single or multiple cavitated masses that may rupture and cause haemoabdomen.

In the heart it arises in the wall of the **right atrium and auricle**. Rupture causes haemorrhagic pericardial effusion and **cardiac tamponade**.

The liver and lungs are common sites of metastasis.

## Clinical signs

There is often **acute collapse** from internal haemorrhage, with:

- weakness
- pale mucous membranes
- tachycardia and weak pulses
- abdominal distension from haemoabdomen

Episodes may be intermittent, with spontaneous recovery as bleeding stops.

Cardiac tumours present with signs of tamponade: muffled heart sounds, jugular distension, weak pulses, ascites and collapse. Arrhythmias may occur.

## Diagnosis

- Abdominal ultrasound shows a cavitated splenic or hepatic mass of mixed echogenicity and free fluid.
- Echocardiography shows a right atrial or auricular mass and pericardial effusion, with signs of tamponade.
- Abdominocentesis or pericardiocentesis yields haemorrhagic fluid.
- Blood work shows regenerative anaemia, coagulation disorders and **thrombocytopenia**, which is associated with shorter survival.
- Staging includes thoracic radiographs or CT and abdominal and cardiac imaging.
- **Histopathology** of the removed mass confirms the diagnosis.

## Treatment

Splenic tumours are treated by **splenectomy**, after stabilising the patient.

Cardiac tamponade is relieved by pericardiocentesis. Resection of an auricular mass or pericardiectomy can be done in selected cases.

Adjuvant **doxorubicin-based chemotherapy** modestly prolongs survival.

Even with surgery and chemotherapy the prognosis is poor, with median survival measured in months.
