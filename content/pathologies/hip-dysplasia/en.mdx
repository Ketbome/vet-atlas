---
title: Hip dysplasia
summary: Abnormal development of the coxofemoral joint with joint laxity that ends in osteoarthritis. The most common inherited orthopaedic disease of the dog.
---

## What it is

Hip dysplasia is a developmental disorder of the coxofemoral joint. The puppy is born with normal hips, but during growth the femoral head and the acetabulum fail to fit properly: the joint becomes lax, the head subluxates with every step and the cartilage wears down. The long-term result is secondary osteoarthritis.

## Who it affects

Large and giant fast-growing breeds: German shepherd, Labrador, golden retriever, rottweiler, Saint Bernard. It has a polygenic genetic basis modulated by environmental factors, especially excess weight and exercise during growth.

## Anatomy involved

- **Femoral head and neck**: the head loses its spherical shape and flattens; the neck thickens with osteophytes.
- **Acetabulum**: becomes shallow; the dorsal rim rounds off and no longer contains the head.
- **Ligament of the femoral head and joint capsule**: stretch, which perpetuates the laxity.

## Clinical signs

Pelvic limb lameness, "bunny hopping" gait when running, difficulty rising, pain on hip extension, atrophy of the thigh muscles. In puppies of 4 to 12 months painful laxity predominates; in adults, osteoarthritis.

## Diagnosis

- **Ortolani sign**: the subluxation is reduced under sedation and a "clunk" is felt.
- **Ventrodorsal radiograph with extended hips**: the standard projection of screening schemes (OFA, FCI). Acetabular coverage of the head, the Norberg angle and the presence of osteophytes are assessed.
- **PennHIP**: measures the distraction index, a quantitative indicator of laxity, from 4 months of age.

## Treatment

Conservative (weight control, controlled exercise, anti-inflammatories, physiotherapy) or surgical depending on age and grade: juvenile pubic symphysiodesis in very young puppies, triple pelvic osteotomy before osteoarthritis appears, and in adults with osteoarthritis total hip replacement or femoral head and neck excision arthroplasty.
