## What it is

Entropion is the inversion of all or part of the **eyelid margin** toward the eye. The hairs of the eyelid skin then rub on the cornea and conjunctiva (trichiasis), causing pain, chronic irritation and corneal damage. Three forms are recognised:

- **Conformational (developmental)**: inherited and related to the shape of the skull, the size of the palpebral fissure, the depth of the orbit and excess facial skin. The most common form in the dog.
- **Spastic**: caused by blepharospasm secondary to another painful eye disease, which pulls the lid inward; it can then perpetuate itself.
- **Cicatricial**: caused by scarring of the eyelid after trauma or surgery.

## Who it affects

Conformational entropion appears in puppies and young dogs of predisposed breeds: **shar-pei** (in which it may appear in the first weeks of life), chow chow, English bulldog, Rottweiler, Saint Bernard, mastiffs, Labrador and golden retriever, spaniels, and others. Giant breeds with large palpebral fissures may have combined entropion and ectropion ("diamond eye").

## Anatomy involved

Each **eyelid** (*palpebra*) is a fold of skin lined inside by palpebral conjunctiva. It contains the **orbicularis oculi muscle**, a weak fibrous tarsus and the tarsal glands, which open at the margin and supply the lipid layer of the tear film. In the dog only the upper lid carries true eyelashes (*cilia*). Entropion most often affects the **lateral part of the lower lid**; in breeds with heavy facial folds, such as the shar-pei and chow chow, the upper lid is frequently involved too.

## Clinical signs

Epiphora, **blepharospasm**, ocular discharge, conjunctival hyperaemia and rubbing of the eye. Chronic cases show keratitis with corneal vascularisation and pigmentation; **corneal ulcers** are common. The rolled-in lid margin is often visible, with the hairs touching the eye.

## Diagnosis

- Examination of the eyelids in the awake dog, **before sedation** or topical anaesthesia, which change lid position.
- Application of a topical anaesthetic: if the entropion disappears, a spastic component is present.
- **Fluorescein** to look for corneal ulcers, and examination for other causes of pain (distichiasis, ectopic cilia, keratoconjunctivitis sicca).

## Treatment

In puppies, temporary **eyelid tacking** sutures evert the lid until the head grows, and may avoid definitive surgery. In adults, or when tacking fails, a **Hotz-Celsus procedure** or one of its modifications removes a strip of skin and orbicularis muscle parallel to the margin to evert it; in giant breeds, shortening of the palpebral fissure may also be needed. Overcorrection must be avoided because it produces ectropion. Spastic entropion is treated by removing the cause of pain. Because the conformational form is heritable, affected dogs should not be used for breeding.
