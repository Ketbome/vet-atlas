---
title: Myxomatous mitral valve disease
summary: The most common acquired heart disease of the dog. Progressive myxomatous degeneration of the mitral valve causes regurgitation, left atrial enlargement and, in some dogs, left-sided congestive heart failure.
---

## What it is

Myxomatous mitral valve disease (MMVD), also called degenerative mitral valve disease or endocardiosis, is a chronic degeneration of the atrioventricular valve apparatus. The fibrous layer of the leaflets breaks down and the spongy layer proliferates, so the leaflets thicken, become nodular, prolapse and lose their ability to coapt; the chordae tendineae also degenerate and may rupture. The tricuspid valve is often affected as well, usually to a lesser degree. The leak (mitral regurgitation) overloads the left atrium and ventricle with volume, and both chambers dilate progressively.

## Who it affects

Small breeds, mainly in middle-aged and older dogs: Cavalier King Charles spaniel (early onset, inherited), dachshund, miniature and toy poodles and other small breeds. It is about 1.5 times more common in males. Many affected dogs never reach heart failure.

## Anatomy involved

- **Mitral valve (*valva atrioventricularis sinistra*)**: thickened leaflets with prolapse into the **left atrium**.
- **Left atrium and ventricle**: eccentric dilation from volume overload. A severely enlarged heart can press on the adjacent bronchi.
- **Lungs**: when left atrial pressure rises enough, pulmonary venous congestion leads to **cardiogenic pulmonary oedema**.

## Clinical signs

A left apical systolic murmur is the first finding and may precede signs by years. With progression: exercise intolerance, tachypnoea and raised sleeping respiratory rate, cough, dyspnoea from pulmonary oedema and occasionally syncope. Rupture of a chorda tendinea or of the left atrium can cause sudden decompensation.

## Diagnosis

- **Auscultation**: left apical systolic murmur.
- **Thoracic radiographs**: left atrial and ventricular enlargement, increased vertebral heart score and, in heart failure, signs of pulmonary oedema.
- **Echocardiography**: confirms thickened, prolapsing leaflets and regurgitation and measures chamber size (left atrium to aorta ratio, normalised left ventricular diameter), which defines stage.
- **ACVIM staging**: A (at risk), B1 and B2 (preclinical, without and with significant cardiac remodelling), C (current or past heart failure) and D (refractory heart failure).

## Treatment

Stage B1 needs only periodic re-examination. In stage B2, pimobendan delays the onset of heart failure. In stage C, treatment combines furosemide, pimobendan and an ACE inhibitor, often with spironolactone; acute pulmonary oedema needs oxygen and parenteral furosemide. Owners can monitor the sleeping respiratory rate at home. Surgical mitral valve repair is available in a few specialist centres.
