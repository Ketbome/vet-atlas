## What it is

Myxomatous mitral valve disease (MMVD), also called degenerative mitral valve disease or endocardiosis, is a chronic degeneration of the atrioventricular valve apparatus.

The fibrous layer of the leaflets breaks down and the spongy layer proliferates. The leaflets thicken, become nodular, prolapse and lose their ability to coapt. The chordae tendineae also degenerate and may rupture.

The tricuspid valve is often affected as well, usually to a lesser degree.

The leak, or mitral regurgitation, overloads the left atrium and ventricle with volume, and both chambers dilate progressively.

## Who it affects

Small breeds, mainly middle-aged and older dogs:

- Cavalier King Charles spaniel, with early onset and inherited
- dachshund
- miniature and toy poodles
- other small breeds

It is about **1.5 times more common in males**. Many affected dogs never reach heart failure.

## Anatomy involved

- **Mitral valve** (*valva atrioventricularis sinistra*). The thickened leaflets prolapse into the left atrium.
- **Left atrium and ventricle**. They dilate eccentrically from volume overload. A severely enlarged heart can press on the adjacent bronchi.
- **Lungs**. When left atrial pressure rises enough, pulmonary venous congestion leads to **cardiogenic pulmonary oedema**.

## Clinical signs

The first finding is a **left apical systolic murmur**, which may precede signs by years.

With progression come:

- exercise intolerance
- tachypnoea and a raised sleeping respiratory rate
- cough
- dyspnoea from pulmonary oedema
- occasionally, syncope

Rupture of a chorda tendinea or of the left atrium can cause sudden decompensation.

## Diagnosis

- Auscultation reveals the left apical systolic murmur.
- Thoracic radiographs show left atrial and ventricular enlargement and an increased vertebral heart score. In heart failure they show signs of pulmonary oedema.
- **Echocardiography** confirms thickened, prolapsing leaflets and regurgitation. It also measures chamber size (left atrium to aorta ratio, normalised left ventricular diameter), which defines the stage.

### ACVIM staging

- **A**: at risk.
- **B1**: preclinical, without significant cardiac remodelling.
- **B2**: preclinical, with significant cardiac remodelling.
- **C**: current or past heart failure.
- **D**: refractory heart failure.

## Treatment

- Stage B1 needs only periodic re-examination.
- In stage B2, **pimobendan** delays the onset of heart failure.
- In stage C, treatment combines furosemide, pimobendan and an ACE inhibitor, often with spironolactone.
- Acute pulmonary oedema needs oxygen and parenteral furosemide.

Owners can monitor the **sleeping respiratory rate** at home.

Surgical mitral valve repair is available in a few specialist centres.
