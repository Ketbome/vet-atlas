## What it is

In the dog, hypothyroidism is almost always **primary**, caused by loss of thyroid tissue. It has two main forms:

- **lymphocytic thyroiditis**, an immune-mediated destruction of the gland with antibodies against thyroglobulin
- **idiopathic follicular atrophy**, in which the thyroid tissue is lost and replaced by fat

Secondary (pituitary) and congenital hypothyroidism are rare.

Lack of thyroxine (T4) and triiodothyronine (T3) slows the metabolism of almost every tissue.

## Who it affects

Middle-aged dogs of medium to large breeds.

Breeds with a reported higher risk include the golden retriever, Dobermann, Irish setter, miniature schnauzer, dachshund, cocker spaniel and Airedale terrier.

## Anatomy involved

### Thyroid gland (*glandula thyroidea*)

Two lobes on the lateral surfaces of the trachea, just caudal to the larynx. In the dog the connecting isthmus is indistinct.

In hypothyroidism the lobes lose volume, which an experienced operator can see on ultrasound.

### Skin

Thyroid hormone deficiency delays hair regrowth and causes alopecia. In severe cases the skin thickens (myxoedema).

## Clinical signs

Signs are diffuse, variable and often nonspecific.

### Metabolic

- lethargy and mental dullness
- exercise intolerance
- weight gain without increased appetite
- heat-seeking, from cold intolerance

### Dermatological

- bilaterally symmetrical, non-pruritic **alopecia** of the trunk, flanks and tail ("rat tail")
- failure of hair to regrow after clipping
- dry, dull coat
- hyperpigmentation and secondary pyoderma
- thickened facial skin from myxoedema, with a "tragic" expression

### Others

- reproductive disorders in intact dogs
- uncommon neurological disorders: megaoesophagus, laryngeal or facial nerve paralysis, vestibular disease
- in rare cases, myxoedema coma with bradycardia and hypothermia

## Diagnosis

- Routine blood work typically shows mild non-regenerative anaemia and **hypercholesterolaemia**.
- A normal **total T4** makes hypothyroidism unlikely. A low value is not enough on its own, because illness and many drugs (glucocorticoids, phenobarbital, sulfonamides) lower it: this is **euthyroid sick syndrome**. **Sighthounds** normally have low T4.
- **Free T4 by equilibrium dialysis** and endogenous TSH are measured. A low free T4 with a high TSH supports the diagnosis.
- Thyroglobulin autoantibodies indicate lymphocytic thyroiditis.
- Improvement of signs during supplementation confirms the diagnosis.

## Treatment

Lifelong oral **levothyroxine** (synthetic T4).

Activity and mental state improve within days to weeks. The coat and body weight take months.

The dose is adjusted by measuring total T4 a few hours after the dose. Over-supplementation can cause thyrotoxicosis, with polyuria, panting, weight loss and tachycardia.

With treatment the prognosis is good, and treatment failure is uncommon.
