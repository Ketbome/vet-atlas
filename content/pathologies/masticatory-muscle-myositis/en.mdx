---
title: Masticatory muscle myositis
summary: Immune-mediated inflammation restricted to the muscles of mastication, caused by antibodies against a myosin found only in those muscles. It causes jaw pain and trismus, then marked atrophy of the temporalis and masseter.
---

## What it is

Masticatory muscle myositis (MMM) is a focal **immune-mediated myopathy**. The muscles of mastication contain a special fibre type, **type 2M fibres**, with a myosin isoform not found in limb muscles. In MMM the immune system produces **autoantibodies against 2M fibre myosin**, and inflammatory cells (mainly B lymphocytes and macrophages) infiltrate these muscles. Limb muscles are not affected, which distinguishes MMM from polymyositis.

## Who it affects

Dogs of any breed, usually young adults. Large breeds such as the **German shepherd**, Labrador and golden retriever and Dobermann are frequently reported, and an atypical form with eye involvement has been described in Cavalier King Charles spaniels.

## Anatomy involved

The muscles of mastication are the **temporalis**, **masseter**, medial and lateral pterygoid muscles and the rostral belly of the digastricus, all innervated by the **mandibular nerve**, a branch of the trigeminal nerve. The digastricus usually lacks 2M fibres and is spared. In the acute phase the swollen muscles can be accompanied by exophthalmos.

## Clinical signs

- **Acute phase**: swelling of the temporalis and masseter, pain when opening the mouth or handling the head, **trismus** (inability to open the mouth), exophthalmos, fever and lymphadenopathy. Not every dog shows a clear acute phase.
- **Chronic phase**: marked, usually bilateral **atrophy of the temporalis and masseter** ("skull-like" head) and restricted jaw opening from fibrosis; some dogs present with slowly progressive atrophy without a previous acute phase.

## Diagnosis

- **Serum 2M antibody test**: highly specific; it should be taken before immunosuppressive treatment, which can cause false negatives.
- **Biopsy of the temporalis or masseter**: inflammatory infiltrate targeting 2M fibres, necrosis and, in chronic cases, fibrosis.
- Creatine kinase may be raised in the acute phase. Electromyography shows abnormalities restricted to the masticatory muscles.
- Rule out trigeminal neuropathy (the dog cannot close the mouth, rather than cannot open it), temporomandibular joint disease, retrobulbar disease, polymyositis and infectious myositis.

## Treatment

**Immunosuppressive doses of corticosteroids**, tapered slowly over weeks to months to the lowest dose that keeps signs from recurring; other immunosuppressants such as azathioprine are added when needed. Forcing the jaw open under anaesthesia is not recommended. Soft food or a feeding tube supports nutrition while the dog cannot eat. Prognosis is good when treatment starts early; relapses are common if treatment is stopped too soon, and fibrosis that has already formed does not reverse.
