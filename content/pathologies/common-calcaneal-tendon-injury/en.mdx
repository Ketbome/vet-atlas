## What it is

The common calcaneal tendon, or Achilles mechanism, can be damaged in two main ways.

### Acute traumatic injury

It results from laceration, direct trauma or rupture from a sudden overload. Any or all of its components may be cut.

### Chronic degenerative tendinopathy

The gastrocnemius tendon degenerates progressively and ruptures partially near its insertion, without a specific traumatic event. The tendon shows fibrous thickening.

### Superficial digital flexor tendon luxation

A related condition. The tendon slips off the calcaneal tuber, usually laterally, when the retinaculum that holds it tears. It is described mainly in **Shetland sheepdogs** and **collies**.

## Who it affects

Lacerations occur in dogs of any age and breed.

Chronic tendinopathy affects mainly middle-aged dogs of medium to large breeds. The **Labrador retriever** is overrepresented, and obesity has been suggested as a predisposing factor.

## Anatomy involved

The **common calcaneal tendon** (*tendo calcaneus communis*) inserts on the **calcaneal tuber** of the tarsus. It has three components:

- the **tendon of the gastrocnemius**, the main extensor of the hock
- the tendon of the **superficial digital flexor**, which runs over the calcaneal tuber, widens into a cap held by medial and lateral retinacula and continues distally to the digits
- the **combined tendon**, formed by contributions of the biceps femoris, semitendinosus and gracilis

Because the superficial digital flexor continues to the toes, an injury of the gastrocnemius with an intact superficial flexor makes the **digits flex** as the hock drops.

## Clinical signs

- Complete rupture causes a **plantigrade stance**: the hock and metatarsus touch the ground when the dog bears weight.
- In partial or chronic injury the hock drops partly, with flexed digits. There is mild lameness and a firm swelling of the tendon just above the calcaneus.
- Lacerations are often found as a wound on the caudal aspect of the leg.

## Diagnosis

- **Palpation** of the tendon looks for gaps, thickening and the posture of the digits.
- Radiographs show soft-tissue swelling, mineralisation within the tendon, enthesophytes on the calcaneal tuber and avulsion fragments.
- Ultrasound or MRI defines which components are affected and the extent of degeneration.

## Treatment

Most injuries are treated **surgically**:

- debridement of degenerated tissue
- suture of the tendon ends with a tendon-holding pattern, such as a locking-loop or three-loop pulley
- or reattachment to the calcaneus through bone tunnels

The repair is protected by keeping the **hock in extension** for several weeks, with a calcaneotibial screw, a transarticular external fixator or a splint. A gradual, controlled return to activity follows.

A luxated superficial digital flexor tendon is replaced and the torn retinaculum repaired.

Complications are common. In a multicentre series of 80 dogs, about **half** of tendon repairs had complications and about a quarter needed further surgery, such as pantarsal arthrodesis. Pantarsal arthrodesis is also used as a first-line or salvage option.
