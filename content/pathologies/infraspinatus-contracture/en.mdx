## What it is

Infraspinatus contracture is a fibrotic shortening of the infraspinatus muscle.

It is believed to start with an **acute strain** or partial tear of the muscle during strenuous exercise. The injured muscle fibres are replaced by fibrous tissue, which contracts over the following weeks. The muscle can no longer lengthen.

As a result, the shoulder stays in **external rotation and abduction**, and its range of motion is restricted.

## Who it affects

Active dogs, especially **hunting and working breeds**. It can be unilateral or bilateral.

## Anatomy involved

The **infraspinatus** (*m. infraspinatus*) fills the infraspinous fossa of the scapula, caudal to the spine. It inserts by a strong tendon on the lateral surface of the **greater tubercle of the humerus**.

It acts as a lateral collateral ligament of the shoulder and rotates the arm outward. It also flexes or extends the shoulder depending on the position of the joint.

It is innervated by the suprascapular nerve.

## Clinical signs

### Initial phase

Sudden lameness and pain over the shoulder after exercise, which improves over one to four weeks.

### Chronic phase

Weeks later, a non-painful lameness with a typical posture appears:

- at rest, the elbow is held adducted and the paw turned outward, abducted and externally rotated
- when walking, the lower limb **swings outward in an arc** (circumduction) during the swing phase
- atrophy of the muscle over the scapula is visible
- full flexion of the shoulder is restricted

## Diagnosis

- History, posture and gait are typical, and shoulder motion is restricted on manipulation. Flexing the shoulder and elbow makes the distal limb rotate outward.
- **Ultrasound** or **MRI** shows the fibrotic muscle.
- Radiographs are usually normal and serve to rule out other shoulder diseases.

## Treatment

**Tenotomy** of the infraspinatus tendon at its insertion on the greater tubercle releases the shoulder. The range of motion and the gait usually improve immediately, on the operating table.

Removal of part of the fibrotic tendon is sometimes added.

Prognosis after surgery is **excellent**, and most dogs return to full function.
