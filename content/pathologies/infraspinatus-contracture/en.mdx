---
title: Infraspinatus contracture
summary: Fibrosis and shortening of the infraspinatus muscle after a strain, typically in active hunting and working dogs. The shoulder becomes fixed in external rotation and the limb swings outward when the dog walks.
---

## What it is

Infraspinatus contracture is a fibrotic shortening of the infraspinatus muscle. It is believed to start with an **acute strain** or partial tear of the muscle during strenuous exercise. The injured muscle fibres are replaced by fibrous tissue that contracts over the following weeks, so the muscle can no longer lengthen. As a result the shoulder stays in **external rotation and abduction**, and its range of motion is restricted.

## Who it affects

Active dogs, especially **hunting and working breeds**. It can be unilateral or bilateral.

## Anatomy involved

The **infraspinatus** (*m. infraspinatus*) fills the infraspinous fossa of the scapula, caudal to the spine, and inserts by a strong tendon on the lateral surface of the **greater tubercle of the humerus**. It acts as a lateral collateral ligament of the shoulder and rotates the arm outward; it also flexes or extends the shoulder depending on the position of the joint. It is innervated by the suprascapular nerve.

## Clinical signs

- **Initial phase**: sudden lameness and pain over the shoulder after exercise, which improves over one to four weeks.
- **Chronic phase** (weeks later): non-painful lameness with a typical posture. At rest the elbow is held adducted and the paw is turned outward (abducted and externally rotated). When walking, the lower limb **swings outward in an arc** (circumduction) during the swing phase. Atrophy of the muscle over the scapula is visible, and full flexion of the shoulder is restricted.

## Diagnosis

- **Typical history, posture and gait**, and restricted shoulder motion on manipulation. Flexing the shoulder and elbow makes the distal limb rotate outward.
- **Ultrasound** or **MRI** to show the fibrotic muscle. Radiographs are usually normal and serve to rule out other shoulder diseases.

## Treatment

**Tenotomy** of the infraspinatus tendon at its insertion on the greater tubercle releases the shoulder; the range of motion and the gait usually improve immediately on the operating table. Removal of part of the fibrotic tendon is sometimes added. Prognosis after surgery is excellent, and most dogs return to full function.
