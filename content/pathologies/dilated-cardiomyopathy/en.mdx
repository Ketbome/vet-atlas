---
title: Dilated cardiomyopathy
summary: Primary disease of the myocardium with dilation of the ventricles and loss of systolic function. Typical of large and giant breeds, especially the Dobermann; it causes congestive heart failure, arrhythmias and sudden death.
---

## What it is

Dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) is a disease of the heart muscle in which the ventricles, above all the left, dilate and contract poorly (reduced systolic function). It has a long preclinical (occult) phase in which the only changes are ventricular arrhythmias or mild dilation, followed by an overt phase with congestive heart failure. In most breeds it is considered hereditary. Secondary forms with a similar picture exist: taurine deficiency (described in some breeds and associated with certain diets), tachycardia-induced cardiomyopathy and doxorubicin toxicity.

## Who it affects

Large and giant breeds, usually middle-aged to older: Dobermann (very high prevalence), Great Dane, Irish wolfhound, boxer (in which ventricular arrhythmias dominate), Newfoundland and others. Cocker spaniels and some retrievers can develop taurine-responsive forms.

## Anatomy involved

- **Ventricles**: thin walls and dilated chambers, left more than right; the atria dilate secondarily.
- **Atrioventricular valves**: the stretched annulus prevents the leaflets from closing, causing functional mitral and tricuspid regurgitation without primary valve lesions.
- **Conduction**: ventricular premature complexes and ventricular tachycardia (Dobermann, boxer) or atrial fibrillation (giant breeds).
- **Lungs and serosal cavities**: left-sided failure causes pulmonary oedema; right-sided failure causes ascites and pleural effusion.

## Clinical signs

In the occult phase there may be no signs, or only syncope; sudden death can be the first event, especially in Dobermanns. In the overt phase: exercise intolerance, weakness, weight loss, tachypnoea, cough, dyspnoea, abdominal distension from ascites, weak and irregular pulse, soft systolic murmur or gallop sound.

## Diagnosis

- **Echocardiography**: dilated left ventricle with reduced fractional shortening and ejection fraction; it is the key test.
- **Holter monitoring (24 h)**: detects ventricular arrhythmias in the occult phase; together with annual echocardiography it is the screening method for Dobermanns.
- **Electrocardiogram**: atrial fibrillation, ventricular premature complexes.
- **Thoracic radiographs**: generalised cardiomegaly and signs of congestion (pulmonary oedema, pleural effusion).
- **Biomarkers**: NT-proBNP and cardiac troponin I support screening. Plasma or whole-blood taurine is measured when diet or breed suggests deficiency.

## Treatment

In the occult phase, pimobendan delays the onset of heart failure in Dobermanns, and significant ventricular arrhythmias are treated (sotalol, mexiletine, amiodarone). In heart failure: pimobendan, furosemide, an ACE inhibitor and spironolactone; heart rate control in atrial fibrillation (diltiazem, digoxin). Dietary change and taurine supplementation can reverse taurine-related cases. The prognosis of overt hereditary DCM is poor, especially in Dobermanns.
