---
title: Degenerative lumbosacral stenosis
summary: Narrowing of the vertebral canal and intervertebral foramina at the lumbosacral junction that compresses the nerve roots of the cauda equina. It causes lumbosacral pain and pelvic limb lameness in middle-aged large-breed dogs.
---

## What it is

Degenerative lumbosacral stenosis (DLSS) is the most common cause of **cauda equina syndrome** in the dog. Several degenerative changes at the L7–S1 junction add up: Hansen type II protrusion of the L7–S1 disc, thickening of the ligamentum flavum and of the joint capsules, osteophytes of the articular processes and instability of the joint. Together they narrow the vertebral canal and the intervertebral foramina through which the L7 nerve roots leave. Transitional lumbosacral vertebrae and osteochondrosis of the sacral endplate predispose to it.

## Who it affects

Middle-aged to older dogs of medium and large breeds, especially the **German shepherd** and active working dogs. Males are affected more often.

## Anatomy involved

In the dog the spinal cord ends at about the level of L6–L7, so the canal of the last lumbar vertebra and the **sacrum** contains no cord, only the **cauda equina**: the nerve roots of L7, the sacral and the caudal segments, which leave through the **L7–S1 intervertebral foramina** and the sacral foramina. The L7 root can become trapped in a narrowed L7–S1 foramen (foraminal stenosis).

## Clinical signs

- **Lumbosacral pain**, reproduced by pressing on the lumbosacral area and by hyperextending the joint (lordosis test) or lifting the tail.
- Reluctance to jump, climb stairs or rise; stiffness on rising.
- Pelvic limb lameness from compression of a nerve root, sometimes with muscle atrophy, proprioceptive deficits or a weak withdrawal reflex.
- In advanced cases: weak tail, and urinary or faecal incontinence.

## Diagnosis

- **Neurological and orthopaedic examination**; other pelvic limb orthopaedic diseases, such as hip dysplasia, must be ruled out.
- **Radiographs** show spondylosis, endplate sclerosis or a step between L7 and the sacrum, but they are not diagnostic.
- **MRI** or **CT** to show the compression of the cauda equina and the foramina.

## Treatment

**Conservative**: weight loss, restricted and controlled exercise, NSAIDs and analgesics, and, in some cases, epidural corticosteroid injections. **Surgical** for dogs with persistent pain or neurological deficits: dorsal laminectomy with or without partial discectomy, foraminotomy for root entrapment, and fixation-fusion (stabilisation) when instability predominates. Working dogs may not return to their previous level of work.
