---
title: Pectus excavatum
summary: Congenital deformity in which the caudal sternum and costal cartilages are displaced dorsally, flattening the thorax dorsoventrally. Mild cases are silent; severe ones compress the heart and lungs.
---

## What it is

Pectus excavatum (funnel chest) is a congenital deformity of the thoracic wall in which the **caudal sternebrae** and their **costal cartilages** deviate dorsally into the thorax. The result is a thorax that is narrow from dorsal to ventral and, in severe cases, displacement and compression of the heart and lungs. The cause is not clear; an imbalance in the pull of the diaphragm and of the muscles attached to the sternum, and abnormal development of the cartilages, have been proposed. It may occur together with the "swimmer puppy" syndrome.

## Who it affects

Puppies, usually noticed in the first weeks or months of life. **Brachycephalic breeds** (English bulldog, French bulldog, pug, Boston terrier) are overrepresented, but it occurs in other breeds.

## Anatomy involved

The canine **sternum** has eight sternebrae: the manubrium, six intermediate sternebrae and the xiphoid process, joined by intersternebral cartilages. The costal cartilages of **ribs 1 to 9** articulate with it. In pectus excavatum the caudal sternebrae and the attached cartilages are bent dorsally, so the floor of the thorax rises toward the vertebral column. The heart is often pushed to one side, usually the left.

## Clinical signs

Mild cases have no clinical signs apart from a palpable depression of the caudal sternum. Severe cases show dyspnoea, tachypnoea, exercise intolerance, poor growth, recurrent respiratory infections and, sometimes, a heart murmur caused by the displacement of the heart. Other congenital anomalies should be looked for.

## Diagnosis

- **Palpation** of a flat or concave caudal sternum.
- **Thoracic radiographs** (lateral and ventrodorsal) show the dorsal deviation of the sternum and the displacement of the heart. Indices that compare the width and height of the thorax or the sternum-to-vertebra distance with the size of a vertebral body are used to grade severity.
- **Echocardiography** if there is a murmur, to rule out concurrent heart disease.

## Treatment

Asymptomatic or mild cases may need no treatment; in very young puppies, gentle lateral compression of the chest by the owner has been used while the cartilages are pliable. Moderate or severe cases in young puppies are treated with an **external splint**: sutures placed around the sternebrae pull the sternum ventrally toward a rigid splint shaped to the chest, left for a few weeks while the compliant chest remodels. Older dogs with a rigid thorax may need surgical correction with sternal osteotomies and internal fixation. Prognosis is good when treated early.
